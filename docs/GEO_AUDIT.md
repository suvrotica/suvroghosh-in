# GEO Audit — suvroghosh.in

Technical audit of conventional-search and generative-search discoverability. The original review
was performed against a production build (`npm run build`) served locally with `npm run preview`.
The provider-specific research and crawler policy were refreshed on 2026-07-22. The resulting
technical discoverability release was verified in Vercel production at Git commit
`5398bacd272e6a1558ffe78f4b80ab3599ebad5d` on 2026-07-22. Evidence comes from official provider
documentation and the final server-rendered HTML of representative routes, not only Svelte source.

Audit dates: 2026-07-18; provider refresh 2026-07-22.

## Production and decision record

- **Technical deployment:** complete and verified for commit
  `5398bacd272e6a1558ffe78f4b80ab3599ebad5d`. The earlier “not yet deployed” state is closed.
- **Editorial pilot:** selected in [GEO_CONTENT_QUEUE.md](GEO_CONTENT_QUEUE.md), but not yet claimed
  as deployed. Its production release will start the 30/60/90 measurement clock in
  [GEO_MEASUREMENT.md](GEO_MEASUREMENT.md).
- **Search and user-directed retrieval:** keep the named search/index bots and user fetchers
  accessible. Their job is current discovery or retrieval, not proof of model training.
- **Training and open-corpus crawling:** retain the current allow decision for `GPTBot`,
  `ClaudeBot`, `KimiBot`, and `CCBot` at the owner's present direction. This is a separate policy
  choice and can later be reversed without intentionally blocking the corresponding documented
  search/retrieval agents.
- **Meaning of permission:** an allowed search bot can fetch a page for indexing or live retrieval;
  an allowed training crawler can only make it eligible for that crawler's data pipeline. Neither
  access mode proves indexing, corpus inclusion, model influence, attribution, or citation, and
  `robots.txt` permission is not a republication or training licence.
- **`llms.txt`:** retain it as an advisory navigation experiment. It is not a recognised web
  standard and none of the reviewed provider documentation describes it as a ranking or ingestion
  signal.

## Provider-specific crawler and retrieval findings

The key distinction is between **search/citation indexing**, **user-directed retrieval**, and
**possible future model training**. These are separate systems. Allowing a training crawler does
not guarantee dataset inclusion, model influence, memory, attribution, or a citation. For this site,
live search citation is the nearer and measurable objective.

The explicit `robots.txt` rules match the owner's stated wish to participate in both live discovery
and the open corpus: search agents, user fetchers, `GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `KimiBot`, and `CCBot` are
allowed. The wildcard rule already made the site permissive; the explicit groups clarify intent and
let the validator catch future accidental blocking. This is eligibility, not a licence declaration
or a promise of inclusion. Training access can later be reversed independently without sacrificing
OpenAI, Anthropic, or Kimi search access.

`CCBot` is explicitly allowed because Common Crawl supplies an open research corpus and reads the
sitemap announced in `robots.txt`. Common Crawl describes its dataset as a sample of the web rather
than a complete archive, so access and sitemap discovery still do not guarantee capture.

None of the OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Kimi, Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent, Sogou, or Shenma sources
reviewed describes publisher-hosted `llms.txt` as a ranking or ingestion signal. Its use here is a
curated signpost for agents that choose to read it, not a substitute for ordinary crawlability.

## External corroboration — prioritised owner actions

These steps require the site owner or a legitimate third party. They are not automated outreach,
and none has been submitted as part of this audit.

1. **Connect the scholarly publication to durable identity records.** Check that the DOI resolves to
   correct title and author metadata, then add the work to the author's ORCID and any maintained
   institutional or scholarly profile. Link those profiles back to the canonical résumé or
   publication entry where their rules permit it. Request a metadata correction only from the
   publisher or registration agency that controls the record.
2. **Make professional identity consistent.** Update genuinely maintained profiles—such as an
   employer biography, LinkedIn, ORCID, GitHub, or a professional association profile—to use a
   concise consistent role description and one canonical site destination. Prefer the résumé for
   identity and a specific project/article for subject expertise; do not create duplicate profiles
   merely to gain links.
3. **Seek institutional corroboration only where it is true.** Ask project owners, conference
   organisers, publications, or former institutional collaborators to correct or add authorship,
   contributor, talk, or project links when a public record is missing. Do not imply endorsement or
   disclose confidential employment details.
4. **Earn descriptive technical links through useful work.** Contribute an implementation note,
   reproducible example, diagram, correction, or answer in relevant healthcare IT,
   interoperability, clinical-data, statistics, or applied-AI communities, then link to the exact
   page only when it genuinely completes the contribution.
5. **Corroborate original interactive work.** Give each maintained demonstration a stable project
   or repository record with creator, purpose, licence, and canonical article link. Seek inclusion
   in a reputable showcase only when the work meets its editorial criteria.
6. **Review results before outreach expands.** Record referring domain, target URL, context,
   qualified visits, and any resulting enquiry at 30/60/90 days. Stop channels that create noise,
   irrelevant traffic, or misleading affiliation.

Do not buy backlinks, exchange links at scale, submit to mass directories, post automated community
comments, or ask institutions to make claims they cannot verify. A small number of accurate,
descriptive references is more useful than a large synthetic link profile.

## Summary

The site's technical foundation is stronger than the original concern suggested: full SSR article
text, canonical redirects, per-article social metadata, coherent entity-linked JSON-LD, a complete
sitemap, RSS autodiscovery, deep internal links, and permissive crawler access are in place. Live
production checks on 2026-07-22 returned `200` for representative pages to the named AI user agents;
the sitemap exposed 654 canonical URLs and the RSS feed exposed 25 recent items.

The site is therefore facing more of a **selection and authority problem than a crawlability
problem**. A broad web search found indexed pages but did not place the site above established
institutional sources for generic HIE queries. The next gains will come from making selected pages
uniquely useful primary sources: explicit claims, evidence and outbound citations, stable identity,
truthful update dates, external corroboration/backlinks, and selective bilingual summaries where a
Chinese-language audience is genuinely relevant. The ranked implementation order and pilot
rationale are recorded in [GEO_CONTENT_QUEUE.md](GEO_CONTENT_QUEUE.md). Mass FAQs, keyword
repetition, schema inflation, or 530 near-identical “AI summaries” would add noise rather than
authority.
