# Build One. Now Build a Thousand.

## Concept and physical mapping (written before geometry)

One wooden phone stand becomes a promise to many people. The stand remains the
same size while its fitted workbench opens sideways: making one exposes the
object; repeating it exposes cutting preparation; a larger batch empties the
material tray; the packing wing carries the promise to a buyer's shelf.

The drawing is a bounded Hero object. It is an explanatory workshop instrument,
not a factory layout or an engineering drawing for manufacturing a real stand.
The phone remains visible to keep the customer's purpose in the scene.

Planned purposeful parts, grouped before geometry (target 100–110 solids):

- Main bench (15): four feet, four leg sockets, thick top, two cross rails,
  two side cheeks, two drawer runners.
- Cutting area (15): cutting mat, rail, two rail stops, two clamps with separate
  knobs, sanding block with abrasive cap, square, three unassembled stand pieces,
  and two fitted guide shoulders.
- Familiar stand and buyer (10): three stand pieces, phone body, screen inset,
  receiver shelf, two shelf feet, parcel base and lid.
- Material tray (17): floor, four walls, two slide rails, handle bridge and two
  handle mounts, six stock planks and empty-rack stop.
- Assembly tray (17): floor, four walls, two runners, handle and two mounts,
  instruction card with two corner clips, a three-piece sample and repair block.
- Packing wing (21): floor, four walls, two support brackets, two runners,
  packing mat, four parcel bodies, four fitted lids, tape spindle, tape roll,
  protective liner and packing stop.
- Repeat row (9): three recognisable three-piece stands. At high quantities these
  are labelled counted bundles in HTML, never a thousand moving objects.

The finished workshop has 118 purposeful solids, including two continuous
staging pads that support the moving stand pieces and six fitted runner hangers.
The final count is reported by `generate-geometry.mjs`; geometry is measured from
the same solids it draws, with painter order solved by the vendored Anatomy
audit. No runtime import reaches the author's skill folder. The MIT attribution
remains in generated metadata and the existing vendored licence.

## Model and state

`model.ts` owns strict validation, all declared assumptions, immutable calculated
snapshots, matched baseline comparison, exact counted bundles and reset defaults.
The Svelte runtime owns only current inputs, guided beat, inspection, comparison
and presentation progress. One input snapshot supplies the scene, results and
plain-language calculation. There is no endpoint, network service or persistence.

Quantities, minutes and working days are invented teaching assumptions for one
maker. Fixes happen before sending. Delivery problems occur afterwards and add
no production or returns work. All material arrives before production starts;
the batch ships together. Arrival, wages, money, safety and real suppliers are
outside the model.

- Geometry: `node src/lib/visualizations/build-one-build-a-thousand/generate-geometry.mjs --audit --check`.

  captured from the implemented default scene. Provenance is in the collection
