# The Museum of Almost

## Concept before geometry

An intimate specimen cabinet asks, “These things did not work out. What, if anything,
survived?” Four labelled drawers hold four different endings. The cabinet is a
physical archive, not a scorecard: only the lamp has a tested onward journey.

The global label and every opened record retain **Demonstration collection —
fictional projects and evidence**. These objects, observations and decisions are
not Suvro Ghosh’s personal history. No numerical study outcomes or costs are
invented. Observation status and reuse status have separate contracts.

Physical mapping: drawer = bounded project record; fitted liner = its purpose;
removable trouble spot = the observation under inspection; unfolded record =
observation / interpretation / unknown; bench = supported original–revision
comparison. The lamp’s keyed connector transfers; the recorder’s sketch is
untested; the planter closes; the web trial remains unresolved.

Grouped purposeful parts, before geometry: cabinet feet/plinth/carcass and caps
(14); four pairs of rails and rear stops (12); four drawers’ bottoms, sides,
backs, fronts, handles, mounts, label plates and liners (52); four specimen
assemblies (23); shallow inspection bench with feet and paper (7). Target: 108
purposeful solids. The geometry receipt records the final count. Screws, seams,
printed rules and closed slots belong to those parts and are not counted as
additional solids. A drawer travels within the overlap of its fitted rail and
retains support; it cannot pass through another drawer. The mobile composition
uses stacked readable drawer fronts and enlarges the selected specimen tray.

Final artwork: **104 purposeful solids**. Drawers travel 66 model units with
27 units of rail engagement remaining. The inspection bench begins 7 units beyond
the fully open handle envelope. The actual Anatomy geometry audit ran against
the closed state and 13 sampled positions for each independently moving drawer
(53 poses): zero support, sunk-pair, depth-order, mid-flight, coverage, clearance,
fold or overlap failures. No proxy shapes or audit exclusions were used.

## Boundaries

The ordinary Markdown article imports `MuseumShell.svelte`. The shell owns lazy
activation and a representative SSR cabinet. `MuseumRuntime.svelte` owns local
selection and guided beats. The pure TypeScript archive validates fixtures,
references, onward targets, costs and allowable selections. Geometry is controlled
SVG generated at authoring time using the already-vendored MIT Anatomy kit; the
browser does not load the kit or anything from the skills directory.

No endpoint, external runtime service, account, secret or persistence is required.
The shell excludes the complete experience from narration with `data-tts-exclude`.
The authored article explains the lesson when JavaScript is unavailable.

## Replacing fictional records with verified records

1. Replace a record in `fixtures.ts` with a stable record ID of your choice. The
   physical cabinet has four existing slots, with at most one record per
   illustration: `lamp`, `recorder`, `planter` and `page`. Reuse the appropriate
   illustration slot even when the stable record ID changes: the renderer binds
   geometry, crop and travel to `illustration`, not to the record ID. Keep its
   trouble-spot `partId` as `lamp-connector`, `recorder-controls`, `planter-seam`
   or `page-purchase` respectively. One to four records are supported; duplicate
   illustration slots, a fifth record and incompatible part IDs are rejected.
   An additional object shape needs authored geometry, rather than relabelling
   an unrelated illustration. Keep purpose,
   observed event, possible explanation, alternatives and still-unknown fields
   separate. Unknown is useful content; never fill a gap with a plausible story.
2. Set `contentStatus: 'verified'` only when explicit publishable sources support
   the personal assertions. Add provenance entries with source identity, source
   summary, a locatable HTTPS URL or `repo:path/to/public-record` and publication
   permission. Keep private authoring notes out of this
   public module. Omit/redact a record if its permission is private or absent.
3. Each observation references an evidence record, and each documented evidence
   record references its explicit public `provenanceIds`: claim → evidence →
   locatable public source. An unrelated provenance entry cannot verify a claim.
   A synthetic source stays
   `fictional`; it cannot verify personal history. A documented observation alone
   does not demonstrate reuse or establish a causal explanation.
4. A comparison needs a registered target, exact transferred element, evidence
   references and its own `proposed`, `reused` or `tested` status. Do not add a
   downstream project merely to give a record an ending. Tested reuse requires
   follow-up evidence, not just the original problem report.
5. Supply a known cost only with a finite non-negative value, unit and provenance.
   Missing time, money and material remain **Unknown**. A relative over-budget
   decision does not supply an amount.
6. Run the feature’s validation tests, check both mixed-status labels and each
   comparison, then regenerate and audit changed geometry. Update the article and
   asset receipt if the content or default composition changes. Recheck the
   global label: a mixed archive must never claim that every record is verified.
   The shared SSR/runtime ledger and closed-cabinet description derive record
   counts, statuses and outcomes from the archive, so replacing a demonstration
   with a verified record also updates its collection-level account.
   Recapture the social image through `maker-collection:assets`: capture status
   follows the current archive as `demonstration`, `mixed` or `verified` and the
   visible collection label must agree. The saved manifest retains that status;
   offline checks verify its allowed value alongside source and pixel provenance.
