Bengali emotional words often name a relationship as well as a feeling: who may feel it toward whom, what has gone unsaid, and what social response is expected. The transliterations below are practical reading aids, not phonetic notation.

<!-- resource-copy:start -->

## Tenderness, care and affection

- **স্নেহ — sneho — affectionate care.** Warmth expressed through concern, protection or gentle attention, often from an older person toward someone younger. Common in speech and writing; less reciprocal in tone than romantic love.
- **মমতা — momota — tender concern.** A soft, sustaining care that may extend to a child, person, animal or place. Literary and formal as a standalone noun, but readily understood in everyday Bengali.
- **আদর — ador — fond attention or cherishing.** Affection made visible through touch, feeding, praise, special care or indulgence. Everyday: _ওকে খুব আদর করে_ means someone treats the person with conspicuous fondness.
- **সোহাগ — shohag — fond caressing or intimate cherishing.** Warmer and more demonstrative than generic affection; common in songs and literary speech, and sometimes deliberately old-fashioned or playful in conversation.
- **মায়া — maya — tender attachment, pitying care, or the pull that makes leaving difficult.** There is no single English equivalent. In philosophical contexts the same word can mean illusion, so the surrounding relationship is essential.
- **টান — taan — an emotional pull.** Literally a pull or tension; colloquially, _ওর প্রতি টান আছে_ describes attachment without declaring love. It can connect people to family, language, neighbourhood or homeland.
- **দরদ — dorod — heartfelt sympathy or feeling.** Suggests that another person's pain has entered one's concern. It can also describe emotional depth in a voice or song; colloquial and literary.
- **বাৎসল্য — batsolyo — parental tenderness.** Affection associated especially with a parent or parent-like elder toward a child. Formal and literary; too elevated for many casual sentences.

## Wounded closeness, missing and separation

- **অভিমান — obhiman — hurt affection that withdraws or reproaches.** Often directed at someone close whose neglect matters precisely because the bond matters. “Sulking,” “resentment” and “hurt feelings” each capture only part of it.
- **মন কেমন করা — mon kemon kora — to feel wistfully unsettled or to miss someone or somewhere.** An everyday phrase for a low, hard-to-name inward pull. Context decides whether it is homesickness, sadness, unease or longing.
- **বিরহ — biroho — longing in separation.** Strongly literary and musical, especially for lovers or devotional separation. It carries more duration and poetic convention than simply “missing.”
- **আকুলতা — akulota — yearning urgency.** A deep longing that presses toward expression or fulfilment. Literary-formal, but natural in serious prose and songs.
- **ব্যাকুলতা — byakulota — restless, anxious eagerness.** Combines longing with agitation: waiting for news can produce _ব্যাকুলতা_ even when romance is absent.
- **হাহাকার — hahakar — anguished lament or desolate outcry.** Very intense and often collective: grief after a loss or devastation. In ordinary comic speech it may be used hyperbolically, so tone matters.

## Embarrassment, reserve and social discomfort

- **লজ্জা — lojja — shame, embarrassment, bashfulness or modesty.** A broad everyday word. _লজ্জা পেলাম_ may mean “I felt embarrassed,” while moral context can make it genuine shame.
- **সংকোচ — shongkoch — self-conscious reserve or hesitation.** The feeling that makes someone hold back from asking, entering, accepting or speaking. More restrained and polite than fear.
- **অস্বস্তি — oshwosti — discomfort or unease.** Can be emotional, social or physical. _কথাটা শুনে অস্বস্তি হলো_ marks unease without specifying shame, fear or offence.
- **কুণ্ঠা — kuntha — inhibition or reluctant self-consciousness.** Formal and literary. Often appears in phrases such as _কুণ্ঠাবোধ_ or _বিনা কুণ্ঠায়_; less common in casual Kolkata conversation.
- **বিব্রত — bibroto — embarrassed or put in an awkward position.** A state rather than a pure emotion. Useful when attention, a question or a social mistake leaves someone unsure how to respond.
- **চক্ষুলজ্জা — chokkhulojja — reluctance produced by social delicacy or shame before others.** Formal and somewhat old-fashioned. It refers to what one cannot comfortably do “in front of people's eyes,” not literal eye trouble.
- **লোকলজ্জা — loklojja — fear of public or communal shame.** Concern about “what people will say.” It often names social pressure rather than an individual's own moral judgment.
- **জড়তা — jorota — awkward stiffness or inhibition.** In a social scene it can mean an inability to relax or speak freely; elsewhere it can mean physical inertia or sluggishness, so it is potentially ambiguous.

## Irritation, anger and grievance

- **রাগ — raag — anger.** The ordinary broad term, ranging from momentary annoyance to serious anger. In musical discussion _রাগ_ also means a raga; context removes the ambiguity.
- **বিরক্তি — birokti — annoyance mixed with aversion or weariness.** Repeated noise, interruption or foolishness can produce it. Usually cooler and more distancing than _রাগ_.
- **ক্ষোভ — khobh — aggrieved anger or indignation.** Often follows injustice, neglect or institutional failure. Common in news and public speech; heavier than a passing irritation.
- **অসন্তোষ — oshontosh — dissatisfaction or discontent.** A measured negative judgment that may exist without visible anger. Common in formal, workplace and political contexts.
- **উষ্মা — ushma — expressed irritation or indignation.** Formal and literary in this emotional sense, often encountered as _উষ্মা প্রকাশ_. The word can also refer to warmth or heat in other contexts.
- **জ্বালা — jwala — burning irritation, torment or exasperation.** Literally burning and therefore also used for physical symptoms. Colloquially, _কী জ্বালা!_ means roughly “what a maddening nuisance!”

## Regret, remorse and incompleteness

- **আক্ষেপ — akkhep — regret tinged with lament.** Often concerns what did not happen or what might have been: _একটাই আক্ষেপ_ introduces a lasting regret rather than an immediate apology.
- **আফসোস — afsos — regret or “what a pity.”** Common and conversational, often lighter than moral remorse. It may express sympathy for an outcome without accepting responsibility for it.
- **অনুতাপ — onutap — remorse or repentance.** Implies looking back on one's own action with moral regret. More formal and ethically weighted than _আফসোস_.
- **খেদ — khed — regret, sorrow or displeasure.** Concise and formal-literary; familiar in phrases such as _খেদের সঙ্গে জানাচ্ছি_ but less frequent in casual speech.
- **গ্লানি — glani — remorseful shame or an inward sense of moral stain.** Literary and serious. It can also suggest dejection or weariness, so it is stronger and less tidy than simple guilt.
- **অতৃপ্তি — otripti — unfulfilled dissatisfaction.** The feeling that desire, curiosity, affection or ambition remains unsatisfied. It need not involve blame.

## Obligation, gratitude and relational debt

- **দায় — day — responsibility, liability or burden.** Short and context-heavy: it may name loving responsibility, legal liability, blame or an unwanted burden.
- **দায়িত্ববোধ — dayittobodh — a felt sense of responsibility.** More inward than a formally assigned duty; it says the person recognises an obligation and is moved by it.
- **কর্তব্যবোধ — kortobbyobodh — a sense of duty.** Formal and principled, often tied to role, ethics or social expectation rather than personal affection.
- **কৃতজ্ঞতা — kritoggyota — gratitude.** Recognition of help or kindness received. Formal as a noun, though the related adjective _কৃতজ্ঞ_ is common in careful speech.
- **ঋণী — rini — indebted, literally or emotionally.** _আমি আপনার কাছে ঋণী_ can express deep gratitude without referring to money; the metaphor gives thanks a durable relational weight.
- **বাধ্যবাধকতা — badhyobadhokota — obligation or constraint.** Formal and often external: rules, circumstances or relationships require an action. It should not be mistaken for affection merely because both can motivate care.

## Usage notes

Pronunciation and preferred register vary across West Bengal, Bangladesh, age groups and families. These entries favour widely established forms and common Kolkata understandings; they are not claims that one English gloss can contain the whole Bengali word. When writing dialogue, match the speaker rather than displaying every word you know.
