Corporate shorthand becomes sludge when it conceals the actor, action, evidence, cost or deadline. The alternatives below are not automatic substitutions: first decide what you are actually trying to say, then choose the sentence that makes that intention testable.

<!-- resource-copy:start -->

## Meetings and next steps

1. **Circle back**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** The subject needs to be revisited after a named event, or you do not want to decide it now.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Let us revisit this after Friday's cost estimate”; “I will reply when Legal confirms the licence”; “We are deferring the decision until 12 August.”

2. **Touch base**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** You want a short conversation, an update or a decision.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Can we speak for ten minutes tomorrow?”; “Please send the current status by 3 p.m.”; “Let us decide who owns the migration.”

3. **Take this offline**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** A smaller group should discuss the issue separately because it is detailed, sensitive or irrelevant to the present meeting.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Maya and I will resolve the API question after this meeting”; “This contains salary information, so let us discuss it privately”; “The deployment details are outside today's agenda; I will send a separate invitation.”

4. **Align stakeholders**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** Particular people or groups must agree, be consulted, approve a choice or understand its consequences.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Finance and Operations must approve the revised budget”; “We will ask nurses and patients to review the new intake process”; “Tell the regional managers what changes on 1 September.”
   - **Keep the specialised term when:** _Stakeholder_ genuinely includes several affected parties whose interests and authority have been identified. Even then, name them where space permits.

5. **Socialize the idea**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** You want people to hear a proposal, criticise it, support it or prepare for it before a formal decision.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Share the draft with the clinic leads for comment”; “Explain the proposal to the board before Thursday's vote”; “Ask the support team what would fail in practice.”

6. **Action this**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** Someone must perform a particular task by a particular time.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Priya, please update the contract by Tuesday”; “Open an incident ticket and assign it to Platform”; “Remove the duplicate records before the next import.”

7. **Put it in the parking lot**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** The point will not be discussed now, but it may need a recorded owner and return date.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Record this for the 14 August planning meeting”; “This is outside today's scope, so I am closing it”; “Jon will investigate the tax question and report on Monday.”

8. **Run it up the flagpole**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** A senior person must review, approve or react to the proposal.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Ask the managing director to approve the £60,000 increase”; “Send the draft to the clinical director for comment”; “Test the proposal with the executive team before we commit.”

## Strategy, value and claims

9. **Move the needle**
   - **What are you really trying to say?** You expect an action to cause a meaningful, measurable change in a named outcome.
   - **Plain alternatives:** “Increase completed registrations by at least five percentage points”; “Reduce average waiting time by ten minutes”; “This change is unlikely to affect renewal rates.”

10. **Strategic synergy**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** Two teams, products or organisations may share costs, capabilities, customers or distribution in a way that creates a specific benefit.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Both teams can use the same fulfilment network”; “Combining the contracts would save about £200,000 a year”; “The acquisition gives us access to the supplier's laboratory.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** _Synergy_ denotes a quantified combined effect greater than the separate effects, especially in a merger analysis. State its source, amount and assumptions.

11. **Value-add**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** A feature, activity or person provides a particular benefit beyond the baseline.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “The review catches dosage errors before publication”; “Weekly summaries save each manager about one hour”; “This step adds no benefit, so remove it.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** _Value added_ is a defined economic or accounting measure. Give the calculation rather than using the hyphenated phrase as general praise.

12. **Low-hanging fruit**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** Some improvements are relatively easy, cheap or low-risk and can be done first.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Start by fixing the three broken links”; “These changes take less than a day and need no database migration”; “Do the low-cost safety improvements before the structural work.”

13. **Quick win**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** A limited change can produce a visible result soon, though it may not address the underlying problem.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Adding the missing label will fix the form today”; “This change should reduce support calls this week”; “The temporary cache buys time but does not solve the capacity limit.”

14. **North star**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** One objective or measure should guide trade-offs among several competing choices.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Our primary goal is to reduce preventable readmissions”; “When priorities conflict, protect task completion for first-time users”; “We will judge the programme by retained monthly users.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** The team has a formally defined _North Star metric_ with an owner, calculation and known limitations. Name the metric in the same sentence.

15. **Best-in-class**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The product or result outperforms a defined comparison group on a named measure.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “It had the lowest error rate among the six systems tested”; “The service meets ISO 27001 requirements”; “We have not compared this feature with competitors.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** A credible benchmark defines the class, date, metric and result. Without those, it is advertising rather than information.

16. **Customer-centric**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** A decision was based on observed customer needs, participation or outcomes.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “We redesigned the form after testing it with 24 customers”; “Customers can now cancel without calling support”; “The policy gives account holders control over data sharing.”

17. **Thought leadership**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** A person or organisation publishes useful original analysis, evidence or proposals in a field.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Publish the results of our three-year audit”; “Explain the new regulation with worked examples”; “Propose a method that other laboratories can test.”

## Work, capacity and staffing

18. **Bandwidth**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** A person or team lacks time, attention, staff or a particular skill.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “I can spend two hours on this on Thursday”; “The team has no available engineer this sprint”; “We can do the audit only if another deadline moves.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** You mean measurable communications or signal capacity, such as “the link has 100 Mbps of available bandwidth.”

19. **Deliverables**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The work must produce particular documents, systems, decisions or other outputs.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “By Friday we will provide the tested application and migration guide”; “The contract requires three drawings and a cost schedule”; “The workshop must end with an approved priority list.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** _Deliverable_ is a defined project-management or contractual category. List the actual deliverables rather than leaving the noun abstract.

20. **Right-size**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** You plan to change staffing, capacity or scope, often by reducing it.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “The company will remove 40 posts”; “Reduce the server pool from 20 machines to 12”; “Narrow the pilot from six hospitals to two.”
    - **Plain-language warning:** Do not use _right-size_ to hide redundancies, service cuts or lost capacity from the people affected.

21. **Streamline**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** You will remove, combine or automate specified steps in a process.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Replace three approvals with one”; “Pre-fill the patient number from the existing record”; “Remove the duplicate weekly report.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** The process has actually become simpler and the removed steps, risks and measured effect are stated.

## Systems, execution and change

22. **Operationalize**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** You need to turn a concept, policy or research variable into a procedure that people can carry out or measure.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Convert the policy into a checklist, owner and deadline”; “Define anxiety as the score on this named instrument”; “Train reception staff and begin the new process on Monday.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** _Operationalize_ has its research-methods sense: defining how an abstract construct will be observed or measured. State the operational definition.

23. **Leverage**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** You intend to use an asset, relationship, tool or advantage for a stated purpose.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Use the existing customer list to recruit participants”; “Reuse the authentication component”; “Ask the regional offices to distribute the survey.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** You mean mechanical leverage, financial leverage or another defined technical relationship. Supply the ratio, mechanism or exposure.

24. **Scalable solution**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The system can handle a specified increase in users, transactions, data or locations without unacceptable cost or failure.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “It supports 20,000 concurrent users with p95 latency below 300 ms”; “A new clinic can adopt it without hiring another administrator”; “Storage cost grows roughly in proportion to the number of records.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** Scale, performance limits, resource growth and test conditions are stated. _Scalable_ without a dimension or threshold says very little.

25. **Robust**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The method or system continues to work under named errors, variation, load or adverse conditions.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “It recovers automatically after a network interruption”; “The estimate changes by less than two per cent when outliers are removed”; “Every supported input passes the malformed-data tests.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** Robustness has a defined statistical, control-theory or engineering meaning and an explicit test or uncertainty set.

26. **Agile**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The team works in short cycles, changes plans when evidence changes or follows a named Agile framework.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “We release a tested increment every two weeks”; “The team can reprioritise after each user test”; “This project uses Scrum with fortnightly sprints.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** You mean a specified Agile method or set of practices. Name the practice that affects the work.

27. **End-to-end**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The scope covers a process from a named starting point to a named finishing point without an unexamined hand-off.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “The test follows an order from checkout through refund”; “Encryption applies from the patient's device to the hospital server”; “We own the process from application receipt to final decision.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** It is a defined property such as end-to-end encryption or testing. Identify both endpoints and any excluded intermediary.

28. **Ecosystem**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** Several participants, products or institutions depend on one another and exchange data, money, services or influence.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Hospitals, laboratories and insurers exchange claims data”; “The toolset includes the compiler, package registry and editor extensions”; “Local suppliers depend on the manufacturer's certification programme.”
    - **Keep the specialised term when:** Interdependence, feedback or co-evolution is part of the analysis, or when discussing a literal ecological system. Otherwise use _industry_, _network_, _toolset_ or _community_.

29. **Transformation journey**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** An organisation is changing particular systems, roles or practices over a stated period.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Over 18 months we will replace the records system and retrain 600 staff”; “The programme moves purchasing authority from head office to regional teams”; “We are halfway through merging the two support operations.”

30. **Boil the ocean**
    - **What are you really trying to say?** The proposed scope is too broad for the available time, evidence or resources.
    - **Plain alternatives:** “Limit this phase to outpatient appointments”; “We cannot answer all five research questions with this dataset”; “Choose one region for the pilot instead of launching nationally.”

<!-- resource-copy:end -->

Some specialised business language is useful because contracts, accounting, research methods and engineering give it a stable meaning. Keep the term when that defined meaning is the point; otherwise name the people, action, measure and deadline.
