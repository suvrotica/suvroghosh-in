This is a working vocabulary for observing Calcutta at street and room level. It includes old materials and contemporary systems without treating either as the city's entire truth. Use only what a particular scene has actually earned.

<!-- resource-copy:start -->

## Sound

- **Pressure-cooker whistle** — A rising domestic shriek that crosses courtyards and thin walls; useful for locating a scene in lived-in residential time, not as generic “Indian ambience.”
- **Conductor's route-call** — Place names compressed into a rhythmic shout at a bus door. Describe its pitch, repetition or urgency rather than writing merely that “the street was noisy.”
- **Local-train iron howl** — The long wheel-and-horn sound that travels farther at night, when traffic thins and the railway becomes audible beyond its own neighbourhood.
- **Generator cough** — The uneven combustion of backup power beneath a shop, clinic or wedding tent: a mechanical splutter followed by a steadier, bass-heavy throb.

## Smell

- **Mustard-oil sting** — The sharp, nasal lift of hot mustard oil, especially when green chilli hits the pan; more argumentative than the soft richness of ghee.
- **Wet-concrete mineral smell** — The cool, stony smell released when rain reaches a roof, pavement or unfinished column after accumulated heat.
- **Diesel-and-dust haze** — Exhaust carrying road grit and construction powder. It belongs to buses, lorries, flyovers and building sites, not to a vague romance of “old Calcutta.”
- **Mosquito-coil acridity** — A sweetish burnt smell that clings to curtains and ankles after dusk; domestic protection with a faint chemical rasp.

## Texture

- **Sweat-slick handrail** — Metal made warm and slippery by many palms in a bus, stairwell or station; intimate evidence of shared public infrastructure.
- **Grit under sandals** — Fine sand, pulverised concrete and roadside dust working between skin and footwear. It suggests abrasion, not picturesque earthiness.
- **Damp-plaster bloom** — Wall paint raised into cool blisters by trapped moisture; touch may shift from chalky to soft depending on how recent the seepage is.
- **Tar-softened road** — Asphalt that gives slightly under fierce sun and grips a sole at the edge of a repaired patch.

## Light

- **Tin-white noon** — Hard summer light reflected from pale roofs, shutters and vehicle tops until surfaces lose colour and shadows become brutally short.
- **Flyover shade** — Broad concrete dimness broken by moving traffic light, dust and the intermittent silver of vehicles above.
- **LED spill** — Blue-white pharmacy, mobile-shop or billboard light flattening faces and leaking into an otherwise warm lane; distinctly contemporary.
- **Monsoon pewter** — A low grey light that turns glass, standing water and unpainted concrete into related shades without making the city literally colourless.

## Heat

- **Trapped-room heat** — Heat held by walls, roof and furniture after sunset, when outside air has cooled slightly but the room keeps returning the day.
- **Radiant pavement heat** — Heat rising from concrete or asphalt against the legs and face; different from hot air because the ground itself seems to emit it.
- **Humidity cling** — Moisture that keeps cloth attached to the back and prevents sweat from doing its cooling work. “Sticky” is accurate but incomplete.
- **Power-cut stillness** — The instant a fan stops: sound falls away first, then accumulated heat becomes newly legible on the skin.

## Rain

- **First-rain dust lift** — The brief smell and visible darkening when early drops strike hot, dusty ground before steady rain washes the loose material away.
- **Gutter surge** — Brown water accelerating along a kerb with leaves, wrappers and foam. It describes motion and load, not just “flooding.”
- **Veranda spray** — Fine wind-driven rain that passes railings and wets the supposedly sheltered edge of a floor, chair or drying cloth.
- **Waterlogged-road mirror** — Standing water reflecting brake lights, shop signs and apartment windows while concealing the depth and condition of the road below.

## Streets

- **Patched asphalt** — Adjacent repairs with different ages, colours and heights; read the road as accumulated maintenance rather than a single failed surface.
- **Paving-block wobble** — A loose block tipping underfoot and releasing stored rainwater, often after the upper surface appears deceptively dry.
- **Cable canopy** — Electric, telecom and service lines crossing a lane at several heights; describe sag, bundle, shadow or attachment instead of invoking chaos automatically.
- **Construction-mesh veil** — Green or blue netting over an active building, filtering light and dust while revealing silhouettes of new floors and moving workers.

## Transport

- **Bus-door crush** — Bodies negotiating entry and exit through the same narrow opening while the conductor urges movement inward; pressure has direction and sequence.
- **Metro brake wind** — The cool, dusty push of air preceding a train, followed by metal braking noise and the platform's organised surge.
- **App-cab brake-light queue** — Red light repeating across air-conditioned cars stalled beneath flyovers or outside stations; a current urban texture, not an old-city relic.
- **Auto-rickshaw rattle** — A light frame amplifying engine vibration, loose fittings and road irregularities into one persistent metallic conversation.

## Buildings

- **Limewashed stairwell** — A pale, matte interior that catches handprints, shoe marks and narrow shafts of daylight; cooler in appearance than glossy modern paint.
- **Grille shadow** — Repeated bars, curls or rectangles projected across a floor or wall, moving as the sun changes while the security grille stays fixed.
- **Tarred-roof tack** — Black waterproofing warmed until it smells bituminous and feels faintly adhesive near a repaired seam.
- **Air-conditioner drip-line** — Condensate ticking onto an awning, wall or pavement and leaving a dark vertical stain beneath otherwise sealed rooms.

## Food

- **Green-chilli flare** — The moment frying chilli reaches eyes and throat before the eater has tasted anything; an airborne warning, not simply “spice.”
- **Phuchka-water tang** — Sour tamarind, salt, chilli and mashed filling arriving with the brittle shell's collapse; texture and temperature matter as much as flavour.
- **Telebhaja crackle** — Batter shedding bubbles in hot oil, then changing to a dry, fragile sound when lifted and drained.
- **Sweet-tea glass heat** — A small glass warming fingertips while condensed milk, tannin and sugar produce a dense taste unlike delicate leaf-tea language.

## Human movement

- **Side-step negotiation** — Two pedestrians repeatedly choosing the same escape direction before one yields; comic at low speed, consequential beside traffic.
- **Shoulder-folding** — Turning and narrowing the upper body to pass through a crowded bus, market aisle or doorway without claiming full personal width.
- **Umbrella choreography** — Tilting, lifting and briefly closing umbrellas so strangers can cross on a narrow pavement during rain.
- **Platform surge** — A crowd compressing toward a train, loosening at each doorway and closing again behind those who board; movement behaves in pulses, not as one solid mass.

## Usage notes

Observation outranks reputation. A tram, crow, crumbling wall or tea glass may be exactly right when it is present and doing specific work; it becomes cliché when it substitutes for looking. Calcutta also contains sealed glass, delivery riders, QR-code stickers, Metro wind, LED glare, app-cab queues and construction mesh. No single street contains the whole city.
