These expressions are current, not universal: usage shifts with age, neighbourhood, class, friendship and setting, while English–Bengali code-switching is ordinary rather than exceptional. The transliterations are conservative reading aids. Many examples use intimate _তুই_ verb forms such as _নিস_, _করিস_ and _দিস_; among strangers, elders or colleagues they may sound rude. Several scolding expressions are subjective judgements, not neutral descriptions.

## Plans, pressure and arrangements

- **কী কেস? — _ki kes?_** **Literal:** “What case?” **Practical meaning:** What is happening, what is the plan, or what has gone wrong? **Register/context:** Casual urban code-switching, especially among friends and younger or middle-aged speakers; tone decides whether it expresses curiosity, suspicion or concern. **Example:** _কালকের কী কেস—যাচ্ছিস তো?_ — “What is the situation tomorrow—are you going?”

- **সিন কী? — _sin ki?_** **Literal:** “What is the scene?” **Practical meaning:** What is the plan, arrangement or current state of affairs? **Register/context:** Younger, urban and distinctly code-switched; common when arranging an evening or checking where matters stand, but unsuitable for formal speech. **Example:** _আজ সন্ধের সিন কী—কোথায় বসছিস?_ — “What is the plan this evening—where are you hanging out?”

- **চাপ নেই — _chaap nei._** **Literal:** “There is no pressure.” **Practical meaning:** No problem; do not worry; it is under control. **Register/context:** Very common informal reassurance across age groups. It can also be ironic when the speaker is plainly in trouble. **Example:** _টিকিট আমি কেটে রেখেছি, চাপ নেই।_ — “I have already bought the tickets; no worries.”

- **চাপ নিস না — _chaap nish na._** **Literal:** “Do not take pressure.” **Practical meaning:** Do not worry or stress yourself about it. **Register/context:** Intimate, supportive speech to a friend or younger person. Use _নেবেন না_ rather than merely changing the pronoun when speaking respectfully. **Example:** _ইন্টারভিউটা নিয়ে এত চাপ নিস না।_ — “Do not stress so much about the interview.”

- **ম্যানেজ হয়ে যাবে — _manage hoye jabe._** **Literal:** “It will become managed.” **Practical meaning:** It can be arranged or sorted out. **Register/context:** Everyday Bengali–English code-switching in families, offices and friend groups. It may be genuine reassurance or a deliberately vague promise, so context matters. **Example:** _আর দুটো চেয়ার লাগবে? ম্যানেজ হয়ে যাবে।_ — “Need two more chairs? We will sort it out.”

- **সেটিং আছে — _setting achhe._** **Literal:** “There is a setting.” **Practical meaning:** Someone has an arrangement, useful connection or inside route. **Register/context:** Informal Indian-English code-switching; often teasing or sceptical, and sometimes suggestive of favouritism. In other contexts _সেটিং_ can simply mean a technical configuration or a romantic arrangement. **Example:** _ও এত তাড়াতাড়ি পাস পেল কী করে? সেটিং আছে নাকি?_ — “How did he get the pass so quickly? Does he have a connection?”

## Leaving, vanishing and making room

- **কেটে পড় — _kete por._** **Literal:** Roughly, “cut away.” **Practical meaning:** Leave quickly, slip away or get going. **Register/context:** Familiar and brisk; friendly in _চল, কেটে পড়ি_, but potentially dismissive as a bare command. **Example:** _চল, বৃষ্টি নামার আগে কেটে পড়ি।_ — “Come on, let us leave before the rain starts.”

- **ডুব মারিস না — _dub marish na._** **Literal:** “Do not take a dive.” **Practical meaning:** Do not disappear, skip the event or fail to show up without warning. **Register/context:** Casual peer speech, especially about classes, meetings or social plans; mildly reproachful rather than literal. **Example:** _কাল আবার ক্লাসে ডুব মারিস না।_ — “Do not skip class again tomorrow.”

- **একটু চাপুন — _ektu chaapun._** **Literal:** “Press or squeeze a little.” **Practical meaning:** Shift along and make room for another person. **Register/context:** A durable Kolkata public-transport expression, heard in buses, local trains and crowded shared seating. Grammatically respectful, though delivery can be brisk. **Example:** _দাদা, একটু চাপুন—আর একজন বসবেন।_ — “Dada, please move over a little—one more person will sit.”

## Approval, intensity and style

- **ফাটাফাটি — _fatafati._** **Literal:** The word evokes bursting or cracking; its modern approving sense is lexicalised. **Practical meaning:** Excellent, spectacular or great fun. **Register/context:** Broadly understood informal praise, exuberant but not necessarily adolescent. **Example:** _নতুন ক্যাফেটার ছাদটা ফাটাফাটি।_ — “The new café's rooftop is fantastic.”

- **ব্যাপক — _byapok._** **Literal:** Extensive, widespread or enormous. **Practical meaning:** A strong colloquial intensifier: extremely, massively or impressively. **Register/context:** Common informal Kolkata Bengali. It can intensify something good or bad, so it is not automatically a compliment. **Example:** _আজ মেট্রোয় ব্যাপক ভিড় ছিল।_ — “The Metro was incredibly crowded today.”

- **হেব্বি — _hebbi._** **Literal:** A Bengali localisation of English “heavy.” **Practical meaning:** Very, really, excellent or impressive, depending on what follows. **Register/context:** Informal urban code-switching that now spans more than one generation; playful spellings reflect colloquial pronunciation. **Example:** _ওই দোকানের কচুরিটা হেব্বি ভালো।_ — “That shop's kochuri is really good.”

- **জমে গেছে — _jome gechhe._** **Literal:** “It has set” or “congealed.” **Practical meaning:** The occasion has come alive, the combination works, or things have turned out splendidly. **Register/context:** Warm, cross-generational colloquial speech, especially for parties, performances, food combinations and adda. **Example:** _শেষ গানটায় অনুষ্ঠানটা জমে গেছে।_ — “The last song really brought the event alive.”

- **জমে ক্ষীর — _jome khir._** **Literal:** “Set into kheer.” **Practical meaning:** A combination or occasion has become thoroughly successful and enjoyable. **Register/context:** Playful and slightly old-school, but still understood and used; warmer and more comic than plain _ভালো হয়েছে_. **Example:** _বৃষ্টি, চা, তেলেভাজা—আড্ডাটা জমে ক্ষীর।_ — “Rain, tea and fritters—the adda has turned out perfectly.”

- **ঘ্যাম — _ghyam._** **Literal:** The slang word itself means high-grade or top-class; it has no dependable separate word-for-word image. **Practical meaning:** Stylish, impressive, formidable or extremely good. **Register/context:** Kolkata college-and-adda slang, jocular and dated to some ears but still recognisable; never formal. **Example:** _পোস্টারটার ডিজাইনটা বেশ ঘ্যাম।_ — “The poster's design is seriously stylish.”

- **কেতা আছে — _keta achhe._** **Literal:** “There is style or panache.” **Practical meaning:** Someone does something with flair, ceremony or a distinctive manner. **Register/context:** Cross-generational and mildly old-school; it can admire genuine elegance or tease someone's performance of it. **Example:** _পুরনো স্কুটার, কিন্তু চালানোর কেতা আছে।_ — “The scooter is old, but he rides it with style.”

## Trouble, teasing and tall talk

- **গণ্ডগোল আছে — _gondogol achhe._** **Literal:** “There is disorder.” **Practical meaning:** Something is wrong, confused or suspicious. **Register/context:** Broad everyday Bengali rather than youth slang; tone can range from practical diagnosis to comic suspicion. **Example:** _হিসেবটা মিলছে না—কোথাও গণ্ডগোল আছে।_ — “The figures do not match—something is wrong somewhere.”

- **কেলো হয়ে গেছে — _kelo hoye gechhe._** **Literal:** “It has become a mess.” **Practical meaning:** Things have gone embarrassingly or comically wrong. **Register/context:** Informal, often humorous and somewhat old-school; better for a muddle or mishap than for grave harm. **Example:** _নামটা ভুল ছাপা হয়েছে; ব্যাপারটা কেলো হয়ে গেছে।_ — “The name was printed incorrectly; the whole thing has become a mess.”

- **মাথা খাস না — _matha khash na._** **Literal:** “Do not eat my head.” **Practical meaning:** Stop pestering me, repeating yourself or making me think about this. **Register/context:** Intimate and blunt. It may be comic between close friends but is rude towards a stranger, elder or colleague. **Example:** _একই কথা দশবার বলে মাথা খাস না।_ — “Do not drive me mad by saying the same thing ten times.”

- **খিল্লি করিস না — _khilli korish na._** **Literal:** “Do not make mockery.” **Practical meaning:** Do not ridicule, tease or troll someone. **Register/context:** Informal and common in speech and social-media commentary; it can describe friendly ribbing or public humiliation, depending on stakes. **Example:** _আমার উচ্চারণ নিয়ে খিল্লি করিস না।_ — “Do not make fun of my accent.”

- **পাকামো করিস না — _pakamo korish na._** **Literal:** “Do not behave over-ripe.” **Practical meaning:** Do not act precocious, over-clever or like a know-it-all. **Register/context:** Often said by adults to younger people or among peers in mock reproof; inherently condescending if used seriously. **Example:** _সব জানিস এমন পাকামো করিস না।_ — “Do not act as though you know everything.”

- **ন্যাকামো করিস না — _nyakamo korish na._** **Literal:** “Do not perform affected coyness.” **Practical meaning:** Stop acting falsely sweet, helpless, coy or theatrically delicate. **Register/context:** Familiar but judgemental. It is often gendered and can police harmless mannerisms, so use it as character speech rather than an objective label. **Example:** _সোজা করে বল, এত ন্যাকামো করিস না।_ — “Say it plainly; stop being so affected.”

- **বাতেলা মারিস না — _batela marish na._** **Literal:** Roughly, “do not spin tall talk”; _বাতেলা_ itself means swank, rant or boastful talk. **Practical meaning:** Do not brag, bluff or tell an implausibly grand story. **Register/context:** Kolkata adda-and-rock speech, often affectionate scepticism among friends; dismissive in an argument. **Example:** _দশ মিনিটে নাকি গোটা অ্যাপ বানিয়েছিস? বাতেলা মারিস না।_ — “You built the whole app in ten minutes, did you? Do not give me that tall tale.”

- **ভাট বকিস না — _bhat bokish na._** **Literal:** “Do not talk _ভাট_”; in this slang construction _ভাট_ denotes empty or nonsensical talk, not rice (_ভাত_). **Practical meaning:** Stop rambling, inventing nonsense or speaking without substance. **Register/context:** College, adda and close-peer speech. _ভাট বকা_ can also affectionately describe aimless conversation, so it is not always a hostile charge. **Example:** _ডেটা না দেখে ভাট বকিস না।_ — “Do not talk nonsense without looking at the data.”

## Taking offence, ignoring noise and speaking plainly

- **গায়ে লাগাস না — _gaye lagash na._** **Literal:** “Do not attach it to your body.” **Practical meaning:** Do not take the remark personally or let it hurt you. **Register/context:** Intimate reassurance or advice. It can also minimise a real insult, so whether it comforts or dismisses depends on the situation. **Example:** _ও মজা করেছে, গায়ে লাগাস না।_ — “She was joking; do not take it personally.”

- **পাত্তা দিস না — _patta dish na._** **Literal:** “Do not give it importance or notice.” **Practical meaning:** Ignore it; do not reward it with attention. **Register/context:** Broad casual Bengali, often used for gossip, provocation, unwanted messages or an unimpressive rival. **Example:** _ওই কমেন্টগুলোকে পাত্তা দিস না।_ — “Do not pay attention to those comments.”

- **ঝেড়ে কাশ — _jhere kash._** **Literal:** “Shake it clear and cough.” **Practical meaning:** Say the whole thing plainly; stop hinting and come clean. **Register/context:** Vivid, cross-generational colloquial speech. It is direct and may sound impatient, though friends often use it playfully. **Example:** _আসল সমস্যা কী, ঝেড়ে কাশ।_ — “What is the real problem? Out with it.”

- **ধুর ছাই — _dhur chhai._** **Literal:** An exasperated “away with it—ashes.” **Practical meaning:** Oh, damn; what rubbish; how annoying. **Register/context:** A mild, cross-generational outburst rather than strong profanity. Directed at an idea it dismisses nonsense; said after a mishap it expresses frustration. **Example:** _ধুর ছাই, আবার ছাতাটা ফেলে এলাম।_ — “Damn, I have left the umbrella behind again.”

## Explicitly code-switched reactions

- **চিল কর — _chill kor._** **Literal:** “Do chill.” **Practical meaning:** Relax, calm down or wait without worrying. **Register/context:** Younger and urban, though widely understood; friendly among peers and deliberately casual. Avoid it when another person's concern needs serious acknowledgement. **Example:** _ট্রেন লেট, চিল কর—হাতে সময় আছে।_ — “The train is late; relax—we have time.”

- **এত ওভার করিস না — _eto over korish na._** **Literal:** “Do not do so much ‘over.’” **Practical meaning:** Do not exaggerate, overreact, show off or take the performance too far. **Register/context:** Younger Bengali–English code-switching; the exact criticism depends on context. Like “calm down,” it can unfairly dismiss a legitimate response. **Example:** _একটা ভুল হয়েছে, এত ওভার করিস না।_ — “One mistake happened; do not make such a huge performance of it.”

A convincing Kolkata voice comes from relationship, rhythm and situation, not from stacking slang. Use one expression a speaker would naturally choose, preserve their age and social position, and remember that the same phrase can sound affectionate, comic, rude or evasive when the relationship changes.
