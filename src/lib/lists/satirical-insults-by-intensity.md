These phrases are tools for fictional narration, affectionate sparring and criticism of conduct, not a licence to pursue a person. Aim the joke at vanity, muddle or a bad decision; avoid identity, vulnerability and anything likely to turn a room against one unwilling target.

<!-- resource-copy:start -->

## Mild raised eyebrow

These can register scepticism without declaring war. Delivery should be dry, and the surrounding relationship should make the playfulness legible.

1. **A triumph of confidence over preparation.** Best for a plan launched before anyone checked the timetable. It criticises the mismatch, not the planner's intelligence.
2. **Not burdened by unnecessary precision.** A restrained description of an answer that treats dates, quantities or names as optional furniture.
3. **An enthusiast for the approximate.** Lightly comic when somebody is consistently almost right; too pointed if accuracy failures have caused real harm.
4. **A punctual guest at the wrong conclusion.** Useful when reasoning arrives quickly but evidence has gone elsewhere.
5. **Possessed of an adventurous relationship with evidence.** A polite-looking phrase for cherry-picking or unsupported certainty. Its formality supplies the sting.
6. **Bringing the energy of a committee that forgot its agenda.** Suits amiable confusion, overlapping speeches and no clear next step.
7. **A solution in search of a problem.** Use for an elaborate intervention imposed before the need has been established; this is also a serious analytical phrase, so context controls the humour.
8. **Impressively undefeated by context.** For a claim repeated after the circumstances that once supported it have disappeared.

## Wry and unmistakable

These are conspicuously comic. They work better in prose, performance or consensual banter than in a tense workplace exchange.

9. **A lighthouse built several miles inland.** Bright, conspicuous and of limited help to the people who actually need navigation.
10. **A footnote that has mistaken itself for the thesis.** For a minor objection, vanity or side issue demanding to govern the whole argument.
11. **All frontage and no load-bearing wall.** A polished presentation with little structure behind it; useful for proposals and performances, not bodies or appearance.
12. **A weather vane in a sealed room.** Constantly changing direction even though no new information has entered.
13. **The human equivalent of a reply-all chain.** Noisy, multiplying and difficult to stop. Reserve it for broad comic characterisation rather than naming a colleague in the chain.
14. **A master key to doors nobody locked.** A grand answer to a difficulty that did not exist.
15. **An orchestra of second thoughts conducted as certainty.** For hesitation disguised by volume, jargon or repeated declarations.
16. **A fountain pen filled with meeting minutes.** Elegant apparatus producing an unexpectedly long record of very little.

## Pointed indictment

The joke now carries a real accusation: wasted effort, evaded responsibility or inflated authority. Use it for systems, fictional antagonists or conduct you are prepared to criticise plainly.

17. **A small empire of avoidable complications.** For someone or something that expands its authority by making simple work dependent on it.
18. **The patron saint of preventable rework.** Theatrical shorthand for decisions that repeatedly send finished work back to the beginning.
19. **A foghorn mounted on a compass.** Loud guidance with no reliable sense of direction.
20. **A procedural cul-de-sac wearing a lanyard.** A comic bureaucratic figure who can explain every rule except the route forward.
21. **An audit trail of unearned confidence.** For a sequence of bold decisions whose supporting evidence grows thinner at every step.
22. **A baroque machine for converting time into delay.** Better aimed at a process than an individual; the ornament and inefficiency are the joke.
23. **A fire drill conducted entirely by memo.** A response that produces urgent paperwork while leaving the urgent condition untouched.
24. **A monument to the proposition that volume can substitute for thought.** Heavy enough to end friendly banter; reserve it for narration, polemic or deliberately broad farce.

## Operatic condemnation

These are stage thunder. Their extravagance must be obvious, or they cease to be satire and become simple hostility. Do not use them against a captive audience or as part of repeated personal ridicule.

25. **A cathedral of bad judgement with every bell ringing.** Maximal, ceremonial disapproval for a spectacular series of connected errors.
26. **The final flourishing signature on a blank page.** For self-important certainty attached to work that contains no substance.
27. **A sovereign republic of misplaced certainty.** A character who has declared independence from correction and evidence alike.
28. **A three-act opera in which every fact misses its cue.** Suits an elaborate explanation whose details repeatedly arrive late, wrong or not at all.
29. **An eclipse caused by a hat far larger than the head beneath it.** A fable-like image of status obscuring capacity; use only where the theatrical metaphor cannot be mistaken for ridicule of appearance.
30. **A grand staircase rising magnificently to a locked broom cupboard.** Lavish effort, confident ascent and a destination of almost comic smallness.
31. **The ceremonial unveiling of a problem everyone else had already solved.** For belated discovery presented as original insight.
32. **An ark built after the flood, with the holes itemised as features.** The heaviest phrase here: a late, defective remedy marketed as foresight. Keep it for fictional, systemic or unmistakably hyperbolic condemnation.

Intensity is not merely a property of the words. Hierarchy, privacy, repetition and the listener's ability to answer back matter more. When in doubt, make the object of satire a decision, document or institution rather than a person.
