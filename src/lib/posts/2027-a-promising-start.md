I have decided, with the full ceremony of a man who owns exactly one working decision, that 2027 will be a promising year. Promising. Not good. Not prosperous. Promising the way a gangrene promises amputation: with sincerity, with momentum, with a certain wet inevitability. I am alive. That is the entire prospectus. That is the whole glossy brochure the universe has mailed me, and let me tell you, the universe's marketing department has been gutted by budget cuts, because the brochure is one page, one line, one word: alive. Still. Somehow. Against the vigorous objections of thermodynamics, economics, and my own lymphatic system.

Let me inventory the assets. I have books. Piles of them. A hoard of paper and ink so dense that if a burglar ever broke in, he would assume the place was already robbed by someone with a PhD in disappointment. No robber with clear motives would visit me. My bank account is a rumor. Worse, it is a rumor that costs money. Every month, my own bank, that temple of fiduciary trust, that marble palace of other people's liquidity, deducts from me a negative sum for the crime of possessing less than six thousand rupees. Let me say that again with the solemnity it deserves: I am fined for being poor. The mathematics of this is elegant in its obscenity. If you have nothing, they take something. If you have less than nothing, they take more. Somewhere in an air-conditioned office, a man in a tie has a spreadsheet, and in that spreadsheet my poverty is not a tragedy but a fee structure. He does not know my name. He knows my insufficiency. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of modern civilization.

And the heat. Let us speak of the heat, because the heat will not permit us to speak of anything else for much longer. Calcutta in summer is not weather. It is an interrogation. The sun does not shine here; it audits. Every rooftop in this city is a griddle, every room a slow oven, every ceiling fan a decorative lie spinning its little lies in circles. And when the loadshedding comes, when the grid sighs and gives up, when the fan stops mid-lie and the refrigerator begins its slow surrender to the laws of entropy, then the city becomes a kiln full of two croissants, I mean crores, of people being fired into terracotta. This summer, mark my words, the heat alone will kill more people in this city than any dedicated murderer with a mustache and a machete ever managed. No villain required. No motive, no weapon, no dramatic chase. Just the mercury, climbing, climbing, and the power, leaving, leaving. The perfect crime is the one committed by the weather, because you cannot handcuff the sky. You cannot cross-examine the humidity. The humidity sits in the witness box and says nothing, and sweats, and wins.

I came back from the United States some years ago, and every successive year since my return I have been poorer. This is not a complaint. This is a trend line. If my finances were a stock, you would short me. If my finances were a weather system, the meteorologists would name me and issue warnings. I have suffered, as is compulsory for the dirty filthy scumbag of Calcutta, a litany of chronic illnesses, each one a badge, each one a little souvenir from the body's long war against its own landlord. The skin remembers. The gut testifies. The joints file their grievances in the morning, in the evening, in the small hours when the ceiling becomes a screen for the projector of regret. Yes, sir, I am a scumbag. I have read too many books, and the books have ruined me for honest misery. An illiterate man can suffer cleanly. I suffer with footnotes.

And yet. And yet. The one thing that is glaringly, insultingly, magnificently true is that I am still here. Breathing this particulate air. Sweating this historic sweat. Typing these words on a machine that will outlast me and knows it. I envision that 2027 will not go well. I envision this the way a man on a sinking ship envisions wet socks: not as prophecy but as physics. I do not think I will be lucky enough to be run over by the Calcutta metro, though who knows, miracles happen, the Lord provides, and the metro, when it finally runs through the veins of this city like a steel tapeworm, will need its martyrs. But until then, there is this stubborn, unglamorous, unkillable fact: I am alive, and the bastards will have to try harder.
