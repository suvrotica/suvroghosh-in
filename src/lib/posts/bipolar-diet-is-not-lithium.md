The cabbage sits in the market with a confidence it has not earned.

This is not the cabbage's fault. It is a respectable vegetable. It can be chopped, fried, softened, salted, made into something cheap and useful beside rice. The fault belongs to the human habit of turning every ordinary thing into a miracle if the internet repeats it often enough.

Mental health attracts miracle advice the way damp walls attract stains.

Eat this. Avoid that. Take this powder. Sleep exactly so many hours. Never drink coffee. Drink only this tea. Walk barefoot. Breathe in a square. Avoid screens. Embrace sunlight. Reject sugar. Worship fermented food. The advice may contain small truths, but small truths become dangerous when they dress as complete rescue.

Food matters. Sleep matters. Routine matters. Caffeine can matter. Alcohol can matter. Movement can matter. Social rhythm can matter. None of this is nonsense.

The nonsense begins when support becomes replacement.

A person can improve diet and still need proper care. A person can sleep better and still struggle. A person can avoid late-night screens and still wake under a heavy sky. A person can eat vegetables, take walks, reduce stimulants, keep a diary, and still require serious help from qualified professionals. The body is not a simple machine that rewards virtue with guaranteed peace.

The wellness industry dislikes that sentence.

It prefers controllable stories. If you eat correctly, you will be well. If you remain unwell, you must not have eaten correctly enough. This is spiritual accounting with better fonts. It flatters the healthy and burdens the suffering.

In Calcutta, this becomes almost comic. Someone will recommend papaya, someone else will recommend no papaya, one uncle will blame phone use, one neighbor will blame weak character, one online expert will blame inflammation, and someone with a ring light will speak solemnly about the gut as if the gut had become the governor of Bengal.

The gut is important. It is not the whole republic.

Sensible care is boring because it is not theatrical. Eat regular meals if possible. Notice what disrupts sleep. Do not treat alcohol as a private doctor. Be careful with caffeine if it unsettles the body. Walk when the weather and knees permit. Keep appointments. Tell the truth to the clinician. Do not turn supplements into a parallel health system run from search results and panic.

This is the small adult position: lifestyle can support care, but it should not impersonate care.

There is also dignity in refusing magical blame. A difficult mind is not proof that one failed at spinach. A rough week is not always a moral invoice. A bad month is not a spreadsheet error in breakfast. People suffer inside complicated bodies, histories, economies, rooms, families, cities, and seasons. Cabbage cannot carry that whole load.

I still respect cabbage.

It does what it can. That is more than many ideologies do. It sits in the basket, green and practical, waiting to become dinner. It does not promise salvation. Humans added that nonsense later.

Let dinner be dinner.

Let care be care.
