I am fifty-one years old, and I have come to understand that hope is not a virtue, it is a secretion, a glandular accident, a biological fart in the dark that keeps the organism from curling into a question mark and expiring quietly in the corner of a rented room in Calcutta, which is where I find myself, staring at a ceiling that I may not be able to afford to stare at for much longer, and the ceiling stares back, indifferent, plastered, peeling, a metaphor for my financial prospects if metaphors were not already the first luxury to go when the wallet begins to weep.

Let me tell you about hope, that rude, uninvited guest who crashes the funeral of your ambitions and insists on playing the sitar, that bastard hope, that residual, unorganized, rudimentary smear of something sticky that clings to the inside of the skull like the last dreg of chutney in a jar you cannot afford to replace, hope is not noble, hope is not the thing with feathers, hope is the thing with teeth, gnawing at the base of your spine, keeping you upright when every signal from the external world suggests that horizontal is the correct posture for what comes next, and what comes next, I can see it, I can smell it, it has the odor of silicon and desperation, the perfume of millions of AI agents joining the chorus of pretensions that was already deafening before they arrived.

I was educated in the United States, fifteen years in healthcare research, a white-collar real estate that I thought I had built, brick by bureaucratic brick, into something that could not be washed away by the tide of whatever fashionable nonsense the tech bros were peddling this quarter, but the tide does not discriminate between the sturdy and the merely cosmetic, and competence, real competence, the kind that comes from staring at data until your eyes bleed and your marriage frays, is being drowned out by the noise of cosmetic competence, the LinkedIn posts, the certificates, the jargon-spouting automata who have mastered the art of sounding like they know what they are talking about without the inconvenient burden of actually knowing anything at all.

And now the machines are joining them, not the machines of science fiction, not the gleaming robots of Asimov's fever dreams, but the soft, whispering algorithms that will write your reports, analyze your data, diagnose your patients, and do it all without the need for sleep, or health insurance, or a bathroom break, or the existential dread that creeps in at three in the morning when you realize that the skill you spent decades honing is now a button on a dashboard that a teenager in Bangalore can press while watching Netflix.

I am not an inveterate asshole, though I have been called worse by better people, I am not incapable, I am not lazy, I am not the man who failed to adapt, I am the man who adapted too well to a world that is now being unadapted around me, a world where the heterogeneous takeover is not coming, it is here, it is now, it is the email that says your contract will not be renewed, it is the job posting that requires fifteen years of experience and a willingness to work for the salary of a chaiwallah, it is the silence where there used to be the sound of human voices making decisions based on something other than the optimization of shareholder value.

The future is unsettled, but that is a euphemism, the future is a shitstorm, the future is a middle-aged man in Calcutta who cannot make his two ends meet, who looks at his bank account the way a diabetic looks at a mithai shop, with longing and terror in equal measure, and the most unsettling part, the part that keeps me awake when the bipolar pendulum swings toward the manic edge of the arc, is that I can see my story becoming the dominant narrative, I can see the millions of ordinary people, the clerks, the analysts, the researchers, the people who thought that education was a shield against the barbarians, realizing that the barbarians are not at the gates, the barbarians are in the cloud, distributed, decentralized, deleting your relevance one query at a time.

Hope, that glandular secretion, that biological imperative, that thing that evolution installed in us because the ones who did not have it lay down and died before they could reproduce, hope keeps me from the complete collapse, the shutdown, the final curtain call, and I resent it, I resent hope the way I resent air, because I need it, because without it I am nothing but a sack of water and regret, and yet hope gives me no guarantees, hope does not pay the rent, hope does not silence the creditors, hope does not explain to the landlord why the check is late again, hope merely says, keep going, you fool, keep going, something might turn up, and something might, or something might not, and hope does not care which, hope is not invested in outcomes, hope is invested in continuation, in the persistence of the organism, in the biological imperative that says die later, not now, later, always later.

I think of the etymology, because when you are broke and desperate you think of etymologies, the way a drowning man thinks of swimming lessons, hope comes from the Old English *hopian*, to wish, to expect, to look forward to, and before that from the Proto-Germanic *hopon*, and the sound of it is the sound of breath escaping, a small expiration, a puff, a fart of the soul, and that is what it is, a small, rude noise in the vast silence of the universe, a noise that says I am still here, I am still making sounds, I am still hoping, despite everything, despite the evidence, despite the arithmetic of my bank statements and the geometry of my shrinking prospects.

And the bipolar, oh yes, the bipolar, the type II that is not the grandiose mania of the movies but the grinding, churning, low-grade fever of existence, the hypomania that makes you think you can write your way out of this, that makes you think that if you just stay up for three more hours, if you just send one more email, if you just explain yourself clearly enough, the world will see your value, will recognize your competence, will save you from the chorus of pretensions that is swelling like a tumor, and then the depression that says no, they will not, you are nothing, you are a fart in the wind, a pouty, oily, rhetorical fart that dissipates before it reaches anyone's nostrils.

But here is the thing about hope, the thing that makes it both pathetic and magnificent, hope does not require evidence, hope is the evidence, hope is the refusal to become data, to become a statistic in the great AI-driven obsolescence of the human middle class, hope is the last act of defiance, the middle finger raised to the algorithm, the biological imperative that says I will persist, I will continue, I will find a way, not because I believe in a better future, but because the alternative is unthinkable, because the alternative is the shutdown, the collapse, the end, and something in me, something rudimentary, something unorganized, something that has not yet been optimized out of existence, refuses.

So I sit in my rented room in Calcutta, the city that was, the city that is, the city that will be, and I watch the ceiling peel, and I calculate the months until the money runs out, and I listen to the chorus of pretensions swelling outside my window, millions of AI agents singing in perfect harmony the song of my irrelevance, and I hope, I hope against hope, I hope like a man pissing into the wind, knowing he will get wet, knowing it is futile, knowing that futility is the only honest response to a world that has made honesty obsolete, and I write, because writing is the last real estate, the one acre that has not yet been colonized by the machines, the one place where a middle-aged man can still be a king, even if his kingdom is a rented room and his subjects are the dust motes dancing in the afternoon light.

The future is not what it used to be, and neither am I, but hope, that bastard, that secretion, that biological fart, keeps me going, keeps me from falling apart, keeps me writing these words that no one may read, in a language that the algorithms have not yet fully mastered, with a voice that is still, despite everything, irreducibly, inconveniently, expensively human.
