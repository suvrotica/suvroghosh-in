The sweat first gathers where dignity has no jurisdiction.

June in Calcutta is not weather. It is an argument filed by the atmosphere against the human body. The fan turns above me with the exhausted righteousness of a clerk who has stamped one thousand applications and will stamp one thousand more before lunch. It does not cool the room. It moves heat from one side to another and calls the transfer progress.

In that heat, unhappiness loses subtlety.

It becomes blunt, irritated, blind. The mind does not produce careful insights. It produces charges. Against the room. Against the body. Against the market. Against time. Against old mistakes. Against the small treacherous fact that a person can grow older without becoming calmer, richer, wiser, or less bothered by a sticky waistband.

This is not noble suffering.

It is ordinary, sweaty, undignified, and difficult to decorate. A fifty-one-year-old man in a small flat, feeling the body leak and complain, does not resemble a philosophical painting. He resembles a citizen whose internal municipality has again failed to manage drainage.

Loneliness adds a second heat.

Not always dramatic loneliness. More often the practical kind: no easy witness, no one to whom the small absurd complaint can be offered without becoming a burden or a performance. The room hears everything and replies with furniture. The phone offers the world, but mostly in formats that sharpen comparison.

Anger becomes tempting because it has edges.

Unhappiness is fog. Anger is a tool, or at least pretends to be one. It says: here is the enemy. Here is the insult. Here is the reason. The explanation may be wrong or incomplete, but the body enjoys the temporary architecture. Blind anger gives shape to what would otherwise spread everywhere.

The discipline is containment.

Not forgiveness. Not enlightenment. Not a smiling acceptance of heat, failure, and bureaucracy. Containment. Keep the harsh sentence inside until it can be examined. Do not throw the room's weather at another person. Do not let one sticky afternoon become an indictment of all existence. Do not trust every conclusion reached while the shirt is damp.

This is harder than it sounds.

The body, after a certain age, becomes noisy evidence. Every discomfort joins the prosecution. The mind points to the body and says: see, decline. The body points to the room and says: see, neglect. The room points to the city and says nothing, which is often the most accurate municipal response.

There are small countermeasures.

Water. A bath if the will permits. A clean shirt. A slower sentence. Tea allowed to cool. The decision not to answer a message while the internal climate is under emergency conditions. None of this solves the life. It only prevents additional damage.

The evening may still be bad.

The heat may remain. The loneliness may remain. The future may remain unimpressed by my complaint. But a contained day is not nothing. No one sees the effort because the visible result is merely the absence of a scene.

Outside, the light fades.

Inside, one stubborn organ continues its old work out of habit, spite, or some smaller unnamed force.
