The photograph has the stillness of a room that no longer exists.

Samarendra Kumar Ghosh belongs to my life not as a monument, but as a source of habits: books, skepticism, plain speech, and the inconvenient belief that a person should think rather than merely obey. These inheritances do not always make a life easier. They make it less false.

The family story moves through places that sound, when listed, like a railway map of disturbance: Bikrampur, Rangoon, Naihati, Sinthi. A few clothes. A return. A beginning rebuilt from what could be carried. History, in ordinary families, is rarely grand while it is happening. It is luggage, fear, rent, work, school fees, illness, rice, and the next practical decision.

Later, descendants turn it into narrative because narrative is how we repair the torn cloth.

What remains most vivid to me is not property or status, but the atmosphere around books. Books entered the house not as decoration but as permission. A child who receives books receives more than paper. He receives trapdoors. He learns that a room has exits invisible to adults busy with accounts and chores. He learns that the world has layers, and that authority is not the same thing as truth.

This is a dangerous gift.

Critical thinking sounds respectable when printed on school brochures. In actual life it can make a person troublesome. He asks why. Then why not. Then who benefits if nobody asks. A society built on hierarchy, flattery, fear, and convenience does not always welcome such questions from a lower-middle-class room.

Yet I would not trade the gift.

It did not make me rich. It did not make me easy. It did not produce a smooth career or a polished public life. It gave me something less marketable and more durable: the habit of not bowing before nonsense simply because nonsense has arrived well dressed.

Perhaps this is what family inheritance really is. Not only surname, place, photograph, or story, but a way of noticing. A way of refusing. A way of holding a sentence up to the light.

The photograph remains still. The questions it carried did not.
