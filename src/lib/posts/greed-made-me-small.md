The tea left a brown ring inside the cup, like a small pond after bad committees had finished improving it.

I was sitting too tightly in my own room. Knees close, shoulders narrow, body arranged as if apologizing for space. A man shrinks in stages. First the plans shrink. Then the posture. Then the number of calls he answers. Then the distance he is willing to travel for people who remember him only when they require something.

Greed made me small.

Not one grand greed. That would be easier to identify. The ordinary kind is worse. Greed with manners. Greed that says collaboration when it means free labor. Greed that says opportunity when it means risk for you and credit for someone else. Greed that says friendship while quietly examining your usefulness.

One betrayal has shape. It can be placed in a chair and questioned. Small selfishness arrives like damp. You notice a patch, then another, and one day the whole room smells faintly wrong.

After enough such weather, withdrawal begins to look like intelligence. Fewer calls. Fewer visits. Shorter explanations. Less hope loaned at no interest. The world calls this bitterness because it prefers the earlier version of you, the one with looser boundaries and better availability.

I do not want to become hard. Hardness is not wisdom. It is often injury that has found a uniform. But softness without discrimination becomes food.

Calcutta teaches this daily. The polite request, the cousinly pressure, the unpaid favor, the client who praises your mind just before discounting your labor, the acquaintance who asks how you are while already reaching for your time. None of it is spectacular. That is the problem. The smallness accumulates.

So my own life became smaller in response. Room, work, tea, notebook, careful yes, quicker no. Not a noble retreat. A budget cut.

Perhaps some expansion is still possible, but it will have to be slower now, and better guarded.

The ring in the cup remains after the tea is gone.

I wash it with more attention than it deserves.
