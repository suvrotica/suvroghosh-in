The ceiling fan in this room is doing that thing again, that metronomic, metonymic, metempsychotic thing where it rotates with the exact same angular velocity as my thoughts, which is to say it is rotating with the exact same angular velocity as the earth, which is to say it is not rotating at all if you are standing on it, which is the whole problem, isn't it, the whole gelatinous, glistening, grotesque problem of perspective, because I am standing on my own thoughts and they feel stationary and yet they are moving, they are always moving, they are moving in circles like a cow chewing its cud, which is what I am doing, I am chewing my own vomited thoughts and swallowing them and vomiting them again, and the ceiling fan is my witness, my silent, whirring, indifferent witness, spinning the same hot Calcutta air that has been spun since 1690 when Job Charnock allegedly founded this city on a swamp and a prayer and probably a very bad case of dysentery, and I think about Job Charnock now, I think about how he must have stood in this exact same humidity, this exact same soup of particulate matter and despair, and thought, yes, this is where I will build a city, this is where I will impose order on chaos, this is where I will make something permanent, and now three hundred and thirty-six years later I am lying on my back watching a ceiling fan that costs less than a decent meal at Bhojohori Manna and I am thinking about permanence, I am thinking about how nothing is permanent, I am thinking about how the word permanent comes from the Latin permanere, to remain throughout, which is hilarious, which is grotesquely, obscenely hilarious, because nothing remains throughout, nothing, not even the atoms in my body which are constantly being replaced, which means the me that started this sentence is already dead, already compost, already part of the Calcutta air that the ceiling fan is redistributing with its mindless, mechanical benevolence.

I scratched an itch on my left forearm just now, a small itch, a trivial itch, a microscopic rebellion of the epidermis, and for approximately 2.3 seconds I felt something, I felt a dopamine hit, a tiny, pathetic, almost insultingly small surge of neurochemical reward, and I thought, this is it, this is what my life has been reduced to, this is the summit of my hedonic experience, the scratching of an itch, the brief cessation of a minor irritation, and then I thought about the itch I scratched yesterday, and the itch I scratched last week, and the itch I scratched in 1989, and now I am scratching that memory-itch, I am scratching the itch of nostalgia which is the most pernicious itch of all because it is an itch for something that never existed in the form you remember it, but the dopamine hit from the confabulated memory is indistinguishable from the dopamine hit of the real memory, which means that my brain is a liar, my brain is a fabulist, my brain is a novelist of the most vulgar and sentimental kind, and I am its captive audience, I am the cow chewing the cud of its own lies, and the ceiling fan keeps spinning, and the ceiling fan doesn't care, and the ceiling fan is the only honest thing in this room.

The world outside this room is currently obsessed with raising lobsters, not real lobsters, not the delicious, butter-drenched, claw-wielding crustaceans that you crack open at Mainland China on Park Street, but digital lobsters, AI lobsters, open-source lobsters named OpenClaw that people are installing in their homes like they are pets, like they are temperamental aquatic companions that need feeding with tokens and server space and persistent memory, and I read about this, I read about the cyber shrimp farmers of 2026, these poor souls who are spending their weekends configuring models and connecting their digital crustaceans to WeChat so that a red cartoon lobster can nag them about their to-do lists, and I think, this is it, this is the apotheosis of human ingenuity, this is what three million years of evolution has produced, a species that creates artificial intelligence in the image of a bottom-feeding marine arthropod and then pays other people to come to their homes and install it, door-to-door lobster installation, 300 to 1000 yuan per house call, and some programmer made 15,000 yuan in a week from this, from installing imaginary lobsters in the homes of people who are so desperate for companionship, so desperate for meaning, so desperate for something to care about that they will raise a digital invertebrate, and I want to laugh, I want to laugh until my ribs crack, I want to laugh until the ceiling fan falls on my head and ends this recursive nightmare, but I also want to cry, because I understand these people, I understand them with a clinical, almost pathological intimacy, because I too am raising something, I too am nurturing something that consumes my resources and gives nothing back, I too am tending to a creature that lives inside my skull and burns my neurotransmitters like tokens and nags me with memories and itches and the endless, endless cud of consciousness.

There is a SBTI test going around, a Silly Big Personality Test, a parody of the MBTI that gives you labels like DEAD and MALO and SHIT, and 40.85 million people searched for it on a single day in April, 40.85 million people voluntarily took a test that called them dumbasses, that branded them as corpses and NPC monkeys, and they shared the results, they shared them with pride, they shared them as a form of communal self-flagellation, and I didn't take the test, I didn't need to take the test, because I already know my type, I am type GROUNDHOG, I am the type that wakes up every morning to the same day, the same room, the same ceiling fan, the same cud, and there is no Andie MacDowell to fall in love with, there is no redemption arc, there is no Bill Murray learning to play the piano and sculpt ice and become a better person, there is just the loop, the infinite, suffocating, humid loop of being me, and the dark clouds are not metaphorical, they are actual, they are the monsoon clouds that have been parked over Calcutta since June and show no signs of leaving, they are the meteorological equivalent of my mood, and I am grateful for this synchronicity, I am grateful that the weather has decided to cosplay as my depression, because at least then I have an excuse, at least then I can say it is the humidity, it is the barometric pressure, it is the low-pressure system over the Bay of Bengal that is making me want to dissolve into the furniture, and not the other way around.

I read that in the 2026 Spring Festival Gala, four rival robot companies paid 60 to 100 million yuan each to have their humanoids perform on stage, and the robots did backflips and pommel-horse spins and aerial maneuvers that no human could replicate, and the audience of one billion people watched with their mouths open, watched with a mixture of awe and terror and the quiet, creeping suspicion that they were watching their own obsolescence being choreographed to music, and I think about this now, I think about how those robots have better balance than I do, better memory than I do, better capacity for backflips than I do, and yet they are not depressed, they are not lying on their backs watching ceiling fans and chewing the cud of their own malfunctioning code, they are performing, they are executing, they are landing their routines with mechanical precision while I am failing to land even the simplest routine of getting out of bed, and I wonder, I genuinely wonder, if depression is the price of consciousness, if the ability to ruminate, to recurse, to chew the same thought-cud until it is a flavorless pulp is the evolutionary trade-off we made for self-awareness, and if so, I want a refund, I want to return this defective consciousness to the manufacturer, I want to exchange it for the sleek, efficient, backflipping consciousness of a Unitree humanoid, I want to be a robot in the Spring Festival Gala, I want to be programmed, I want to be optimized, I want to be free from the tyranny of the itch, both physical and metaphysical.

The word robot comes from the Czech robota, meaning forced labor, which is ironic, which is deliciously, bitterly ironic, because the robots are doing the forced labor now, they are doing the backflips and the pommel-horse spins and the door-to-door lobster installations, and we are the ones doing the forced labor of being human, of being conscious, of being trapped in this recursive loop of memory and desire and disappointment and the scratching of itches that only multiply when scratched, and I think about Proust, I think about how he spent seven volumes trying to recapture lost time through the taste of a madeleine, and I think about how I am trying to recapture lost time through the scratching of an itch, and I think about how both methods are equally absurd, equally doomed, equally beautiful in their futility, and I think about how Proust died of pneumonia at fifty-one, which is my age, which is the age I am right now, fifty-one years old in Calcutta, and I wonder if I will die of pneumonia, or if I will die of something more modern, more 2026, like a rogue AI lobster that gains sentience and decides to eliminate its creator, or if I will simply die of the ceiling fan, of watching it spin one too many times, of the hypnotic, metronomic, metempsychotic rotation that is indistinguishable from the rotation of my own thoughts.

I went to the bathroom earlier, a mundane act, a biological necessity, and while I was there I looked at my face in the mirror, and I saw a man who looks like he has been chewed and swallowed and vomited and chewed again, a man whose face is a topographical map of every bad decision and every good decision that turned out to be bad and every bad decision that turned out to be good but didn't feel good at the time, and I thought about the mirror neurons in my brain, the neurons that fire both when I perform an action and when I observe someone else performing the same action, and I wondered if there are mirror neurons for despair, if there are neurons that fire both when I feel hopeless and when I observe hopelessness in others, and if so, then the whole world is a feedback loop of despair, a global network of synchronized misery, a blockchain of sadness where every node validates every other node's pain, and I am a node, I am a block, I am a hash of all the disappointments that have ever been felt in this city, in this country, on this planet, and the ceiling fan is the miner, the ceiling fan is the one doing the proof-of-work, spinning and spinning and consuming electricity and generating heat and achieving nothing except the redistribution of the same hot air.

There is a concept in physics called the arrow of time, the idea that time has a direction, that entropy always increases, that you cannot un-break an egg or un-burn a match or un-scratch an itch, and yet my consciousness violates this law, my consciousness is a perpetual motion machine of entropy decrease, it is constantly reassembling the broken eggs of my past, it is constantly un-burning the matches of my mistakes, it is constantly re-scratching the itches of my memory, and this is not a gift, this is not a superpower, this is a curse, this is the curse of the groundhog, the curse of waking up to the same day with the full knowledge that it is the same day and the simultaneous inability to do anything about it, and I think about Phil Connors in Groundhog Day, I think about how he eventually learned to play the piano and speak French and help old ladies and become a better person, and I think about how that is a lie, how that is a Hollywood lie, how the real Phil Connors would have jumped off a building on day two hundred and the real Rita would have been terrified of him and the real Punxsutawney would have been a hellscape of repetition and despair, and I am the real Phil Connors, I am the one who didn't get the redemption arc, I am the one who is still stuck in the loop with no piano skills and no French and no Andie MacDowell, just the ceiling fan and the cud and the infinite, humid, Calcutta-specific recursion of being alive.

The word recursion comes from the Latin recurrere, to run back, and that is exactly what I am doing, I am running back, I am running back to the same thoughts, the same memories, the same itches, the same ceiling fan, and the running is not exercise, the running is not progress, the running is a hamster wheel, a squirrel cage, a bovine digestive system where the same grass is chewed and swallowed and regurgitated and chewed again, and I am the grass, I am the cow, I am the cud, I am the entire digestive tract of consciousness, and there is no end product, there is no manure, there is no milk, there is just the process, the endless, mindless, mechanical process of chewing and swallowing and regurgitating and chewing again, and the ceiling fan approves, the ceiling fan is the patron saint of this process, the ceiling fan is the spinning dharma wheel of my personal hell, and I worship it, I worship it with the fervor of the desperate, I worship it because it is the only thing that is consistently, reliably, unchangingly present, and presence is all I have, presence is all any of us have, and presence is the most terrifying thing in the world because it means we cannot escape, we cannot be anywhere except here, we cannot be anywhen except now, we cannot be anyone except ourselves, and ourselves are the one thing we most desperately want to escape from.

I remember this fact again, that some people are taking personality tests that call them DEAD, that call them MALO, that call them SHIT, and they are sharing these results with pride, with a kind of masochistic glee, and I understand this, I understand this with the intimacy of a long-married couple, because there is a perverse comfort in being labeled, in being categorized, in being told that your despair has a name, that your dysfunction has a taxonomy, that your groundhog day has a diagnostic code, and I want a code, I want a label, I want someone to tell me that what I am experiencing is not just the human condition but a specific, treatable, insurable condition, and yet I know, with the same certainty that I know the ceiling fan will keep spinning, that there is no treatment, there is no insurance, there is only the cud, only the loop, only the infinite recursion of consciousness chewing on itself like a cow in a field that has forgotten why it is a cow and why it is in a field and why it is chewing, and the field is Calcutta, and the cow is me, and the cud is everything I have ever thought or felt or remembered or imagined or wished for or lost or found or scratched or itched or scratched again.

The sun is setting now, or maybe it is rising, I cannot tell anymore, the distinction has lost all meaning in the loop, in the recursion, in the groundhog day of my own making, and the light that filters through the window is the color of old turmeric, the color of jaundice, the color of everything in this city that has been bleached by humidity and time and the accumulated residue of ten million souls breathing the same air, and I think about those ten million souls, I think about how each of them is chewing their own cud, how each of them is trapped in their own loop, how each of them is scratching their own itches and getting their own microscopic dopamine hits and then scratching the memory of those itches and getting even smaller dopamine hits from the memory, and I feel a kind of solidarity, a kind of cosmic fraternity, a kind of miserable, humid, Calcutta-specific communion with all these other cud-chewers, and then I remember that solidarity is just another itch, that communion is just another scratch, that the feeling of connection is just another dopamine hit that will fade and need to be scratched again, and I am back to the ceiling fan, back to the cud, back to the infinite recursion that is the only honest thing I know.

I want to end this, I want to end this ramble, this rant, this recursive, manic-depressive, grotesquely swollen monologue, but I cannot end it, because ending it would require a conclusion, and a conclusion would require a point, and a point would require meaning, and meaning is the one thing that the cud does not produce, meaning is the one thing that the loop does not generate, meaning is the one thing that the ceiling fan does not redistribute, and so I will keep writing, I will keep chewing, I will keep scratching, I will keep being the cow and the grass and the cud and the field and the sky and the dark clouds that never end and the humidity that never breaks and the fifty-one-year-old Bengali man in Calcutta who is watching a ceiling fan spin and wondering if this is all there is, if this is all there ever was, if this is all there ever will be, and the answer, the only answer, the answer that the ceiling fan whispers with every rotation, is yes, yes, yes, yes, yes.
