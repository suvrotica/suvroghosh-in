Happiness is the herpes of human emotions: uncomfortable, periodic, embarrassingly obvious, and everyone pretends it's not happening while secretly popping pills or applying creams of self-delusion to manage outbreaks. Despite being aggressively marketed as life's greatest goal, happiness is fundamentally unhealthy, financially ruinous, and morally suspect. Like religion, gluten-free pastries, and unlimited data plans—this filthy fraud promises everything, delivers nothing, and charges you extra for the pleasure of being fucked over gently.

Consider first its history, because history, as you know, is just humanity's charmingly pathetic effort to make their fumbling fuck-ups look intentional. The very word "happy" slithered its way into English from the Old Norse "happ," meaning luck or chance. So, you see, happiness began as dumb luck—rolling dice in a tavern filled with drunken Vikings—rather than something you could "achieve" through yoga retreats or bullet journaling or staring at your anus in a mirror while chanting affirmations in Sanskrit. Happiness was once accidental. Now it's compulsory. Go figure.

Then philosophers, the original professional bullshitters, took this noble accident and surgically dissected it, cooked it in rhetoric, fried it with metaphysics, and served it up cold on silver platters of logic so convoluted you'd choke on them quicker than an undercooked Calcutta street-roll. Aristotle's "eudaimonia," the smug Greek ideal of flourishing virtue, started the rot: he prescribed happiness like it was hemorrhoid cream, soothing humanity’s existential itch with ethical suppositories. But happiness was not cured; it merely festered beneath the surface of civil society, occasionally breaking through as depressive episodes, consumer debt, and masturbation.

Fast forward, past Freud’s fetish for phallic-shaped cigars and Jung’s kinky affair with collective unconscious orgies, straight into modern capitalism, where happiness metastasized into a multi-billion-dollar tumor of self-help, wellness retreats, SSRIs, and Instagram influencers. You can now buy happiness, rent happiness, lease-to-own happiness, or subscribe monthly like it's fucking Netflix. Happiness has become subscription-based existential dread, one serotonin-dopamine cocktail at a time.

And what do we do about the unhappy majority—the quietly miserable, the comfortably numb? We medicate them, because unhappiness isn't profitable unless you medicalize it. Big Pharma's cash cow grazes placidly on our desperation, secreting milk laced with benzodiazepines and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors—a molecular gangbang in the synaptic cleft. Unhappiness is pathological because someone in a lab coat said so. Neuroscience, psychiatry’s favorite toy, explains our suffering away with amygdalae, synapses, and neurotransmitters—the latrine cleaners of consciousness—sweeping emotional shit under the rug of pseudo-knowledge.

But here's the paradox, as delightful and unpleasant as a Bengali marriage feast after excessive hilsa-fish consumption: the harder you chase happiness, the faster it runs from you. We’ve known this since Buddha—a guy who literally abandoned happiness to find nirvana, and now whose image is ironically plastered on motivational fridge magnets and office mugs, reminding people who hate their jobs that everything is temporary, except perhaps their student loans.

Consider the "Happiness Index," that ultimate capitalist circle-jerk—a way nations measure collective delusion. Finland tops it regularly, but have you ever met a genuinely happy Finn? They look about as thrilled as a Bengali stuck in Calcutta traffic during monsoon, trapped in a Maruti 800 with broken AC and three angry relatives arguing over whose diabetes is more severe. The so-called happiest places on earth are typically frozen, bland, and depressingly clean, as if happiness meant emotional constipation and excellent public sanitation.

Even worse is the tyranny of positive thinking, the fascism of optimism, which insists your unhappiness is entirely your fault. Didn't achieve your dreams? It's because you didn't visualize your chakras properly, you pessimistic fuck. Unemployed, unloved, unhappy? Must be your negative vibes, because clearly systemic inequality, genetic predisposition, or just plain shitty luck doesn't exist. Self-help gurus, armed with PowerPoint slides and Botox smiles, gaslight you into believing that your misery is entirely internal. Their solutions? Visualize success, masturbate frequently, drink matcha, repeat.

In reality, humans evolved miserable for survival. Happiness—a brief, brain-doping trick—is evolution’s carrot on a stick, a fleeting reward like orgasm, used by DNA to propagate more unhappy, deluded descendants. Happiness is designed as a temporary high, just enough dopamine release to get you to mate, buy dumb things, and then suffer buyer’s remorse, all over again, until you're bankrupt, divorced, and dead.

So stop chasing happiness, this unhappy, unhealthy, ubiquitous scam. Embrace misery, bathe in its filth, wallow in existential dread, because at least unhappiness never sends you monthly bills, never lies on a yoga mat pretending everything is fine, never quotes Rumi on Facebook. It’s honest. It’s yours. Fuck happiness—be genuinely, irredeemably, beautifully unhappy.
