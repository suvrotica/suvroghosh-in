The drain outside had produced a smell with ideological confidence.

It rose through the lane in the afternoon heat, entered the room without knocking, and behaved like an official announcement. A simpler man would have accepted it. He would have called it weather, destiny, heritage, adjustment, anything except what it was. He would have smiled, forwarded a slogan, and slept better.

I could have been that man.

There was no constitutional obstacle. A little less reading, a little more obedience, a cleaner habit of looking away, and my life might have been smoother. I could have nodded at power, praised the speech, ignored the file, and said, "What to do?" in that locally perfected tone which converts surrender into wisdom.

But doubt entered early and never left.

Doubt is not glamorous. It does not make a man brave. Often it only makes him troublesome at the wrong table. It notices the damp behind the paint, the arithmetic behind the promise, the small cruelty inside the joke. Once it has shown you the machinery, praise becomes difficult.

That is my inconvenience.

I am not pure. I have compromised, delayed, avoided, flattered, hidden, and failed in the usual human ways. But I cannot easily call sewage incense. Something in me objects. The objection is not grand. It has no flag. It is only a small cough from the corner of the mind.

In Calcutta, this cough can ruin a man's peace. The city has many arrangements built on polite unreality. Everyone knows, no one says. Everyone suffers, everyone adjusts. The system survives because the smell is given another name.

So I sit in the room, unheroic and irritated, while the drain continues its public work.

Outside, someone is praising development.

Inside, I keep the window half open because truth, like bad air, needs somewhere to go.
