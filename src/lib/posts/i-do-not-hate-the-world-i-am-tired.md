The cup was clean, but the table around it was not.

There is a lesson there, though I distrust lessons that arrive before breakfast. One can keep a small object decent while the larger surface gathers dust, paper, old receipts, and the faint stickiness of neglected days. Perhaps this is also how a man continues. Not by loving the whole world, not by forgiving every insult, but by washing one cup and leaving the rest for later.

I do not hate the world.

I am tired of being told I must find it charming.

There is a difference. Hatred has energy. It makes plans. It sharpens itself. Fatigue merely sits down in the plastic chair and looks at the room with the face of a clerk after closing time. It has seen too many small tricks, too many unpaid favors, too many men calling exploitation opportunity, too many polite smiles with a hook behind them.

People mistake this for bitterness because bitterness is easier to dismiss. A tired man asks for accounting. A bitter man can be ignored.

I have not escaped the mess. I have contributed to it in minor, ordinary ways. I have been selfish, evasive, vain, lazy, frightened, and occasionally absurd. But some days the surrounding human machinery becomes too much. The advice, the noise, the performance of virtue, the casual cruelty, the way decent language is used to package indecent behavior.

At such times irritation becomes the last working lamp in the corridor.

Not wisdom.

Only light enough to avoid walking straight into the wall.

Outside, Calcutta continues with its rehearsed impatience. Inside, the fan turns, the clean cup dries, and the rest of the table waits under a thin layer of accusation.

I will wipe it later.

That is not a promise. It is the most honest form of hope available today.
