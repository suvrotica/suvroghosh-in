## How to fly

Raise the nose with W or Arrow Up, but remember that height costs speed. Lower it with S or Arrow Down to gather airspeed again. Bank with A and D or the left and right arrows. On a landscape touchscreen, drag the single flight field. Escape pauses and releases control; R asks for another throw; M mutes; F enters fullscreen where the browser permits it.

The plane has no engine and the city contains no boost button. A long flight comes from exchanging speed and height carefully, then borrowing rising air from roofs, courtyards, traffic, open grass, the Hooghly and approaching weather.

## A city assembled by memory

This is not a municipal map. Calcutta has been assembled by memory and crosswind. A North Calcutta lane can open into bridge country; the Maidan may breathe beyond Park Street; New Town can shimmer across the river. The joins are impossible, but each place keeps its own visual logic. Bridges still span water, ghats meet the Hooghly, Victoria remains in open green space, and Biswa Bangla Gate belongs to broad newer roads.

The mixture of trams, yellow Ambassador taxis, old façades and contemporary towers is a temporal palimpsest, not a claim that every remembered thing occupied one street in one year.

## Read the wind

Tea steam, washing, leaves, newspapers, birds, dust, rain and river ripples lean before a strong gust arrives. Sunlit roofs and open ground send thermals upward. Wind climbs a parapet, accelerates through a lane and rolls into sinking air behind a tall building. At height, the Hooghly offers a broad smoother breeze while New Town's towers shed long lateral gusts.

## Charcoal in three dimensions

The city is built from real three-dimensional geometry so buildings, people, trees, vehicles and bridge members retain depth and parallax. Quantized light, surface-bound hatching, authored outer ink, graphite shadow and erased-charcoal distance treatment make that geometry behave like a densely observed drawing. The folded paper plane remains comparatively crisp: warm notebook paper, visible folds, edge thickness, ruling and handled fibres rather than white plastic.

## Sound, accessibility and controls

Choose Calcutta sound or silence before take-off. Sound never starts without that gesture and stops when the flight is destroyed. Optional captions describe important direction and distance. Calm Flight, Calm Camera, stronger wind marks, a high-contrast corridor, sensitivity and inverted pitch are available as semantic controls over the canvas. Reduced-motion visitors begin in Calm Flight. Portrait phones receive the finished still scene and a request for a wider horizon instead of a cramped live canvas.
