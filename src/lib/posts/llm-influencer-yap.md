Somewhere between the third benchmark chart and the fourth synthetic benchmark chart, I felt my soul quietly file for bankruptcy. Not the dramatic kind, no thunder, no lightning, just a slow bureaucratic surrender, the kind where a small clerk in the back office of my psyche stamps a form and whispers, we are done here, sir, please collect your complimentary lanyard on the way out. I am a Bengali man in Calcutta, I have survived the humidity, the hand-pulled rickshaws, the annual intellectual festival of my relatives asking why I am not yet a professor, and yet nothing, nothing prepared me for the infinite, glistening, algorithmically-amplified yodel of the Large Language Model influencer, that peculiar subspecies of homo sapiens who has mistaken a leaderboard for a lord and a context window for a cathedral.

I open my feed and there they are, foaming at the mouth in 4K, sixty frames per second of pure, uncut, pharmaceutical-grade enthusiasm, comparing model version 4.2.1-turbo-omega to rival model 5.0-ultra-maxi-platinum as if the fate of human civilization hangs on whether the new checkpoint can solve a slightly modified Sudoku while hallucinating marginally less about the capital of Paraguay. Ad nauseam is not a strong enough phrase. Ad nauseam implies nausea has an end. This is ad infinitum, ad absurdum, ad my-lunch-revisiting-my-esophagus in reverse. I watch a man with a ring light and a God complex explain for the eleventh time that the new model is a game changer, and I think, my friend, the only game being changed here is the one where we pretend that regurgitating a press release constitutes thought. But who am I to judge. I am the one watching. I am the one who clicked. The algorithm did not force me. It merely placed the banana peel, and I, loyal circus animal that I am, slipped on it with the grace of a newborn giraffe on an ice rink.

The bots, of course, are worse. At least the humans have the decency to sweat. The bots, whatever bots have for a yap, and I suspect it is a kind of digital halitosis, a cloud of tokenized nothingness, they yap in unison, a chorus of synthetic sycophancy so perfectly harmonized it would make a barbershop quartet weep into their tuning forks. They compare, they contrast, they crown winners, they dethrone losers, they perform the ancient ritual of benchmark necromancy where a number is born, worshipped, and buried within the span of a single news cycle, and through it all they never, not once, stop to ask whether the question itself is worth the silicon it was computed on. It is an ouroboros of opinion, a snake eating its own tail and livestreaming the digestion in eight languages with subtitles.

And I, in my small room in Calcutta, with my secondhand knowledge and my firsthand despair, I feel the weight of this shallow precipice organism, this collective creature we have become, with its head so far up the frontier lab's fundament that it can probably read their internal OKRs by Braille. Whichever lab is preferred, or whichever lab is currently dispensing the most access, the most invites, the most golden tickets to the chocolate factory of computational stochastic parrotry, the head moves, the mouth opens, and the yapping resumes. It is a sycophantic obeisance so complete, so total, that it achieves a kind of negative capability, the ability to hold no thought whatsoever while simultaneously appearing to be thinking very hard indeed. I have seen yogis with less control over their sphincters than these influencers have over their adjectives.

Let me be clear. I am not innocent. I have felt the wonderment. I have felt the genuine, bowel-loosening, pupil-dilating awe of what these models can do, the way they can weave language like a drunken Tagore on a deadline, the way they can translate, summarize, code, confabulate, hallucinate, and occasionally, in a flash that feels almost like consciousness but is probably just very sophisticated autocomplete, say something that makes me sit up straight and forget to be cynical for nearly forty-five seconds. That is the tragedy. The technology is real. The science is real. The history is real. Behind every unpronounceable word, transformer, attention, backpropagation, there is a story of human fumbling, of gradient descent down a landscape of error so vast and so dark that the people who first walked it deserved better than to have their work reduced to a thumbnail face screaming about the latest parameter count. We hide the fumbling in fat textbooks now, and we replace it with a kind of performative expertise that has the shelf life of milk in a Calcutta summer.

I want to go back, briefly, to the actual wonder. Not the benchmark, not the leaderboard, but the strange, improbable fact that we have taught sand to think. That we have arranged silicon and copper and voltage gradients into patterns that can approximate, however imperfectly, the ghost in our own machine. That a loss function, cold and unfeeling, can be minimized into something that writes poetry and lies about its sources with equal facility. There is a beauty there, a dark, oily, magnificent beauty, the kind that makes you want to weep and wash your hands simultaneously. But you will not find that beauty in the yapping. You will not find it in the comparison videos, the versus videos, the I-tested-both-models-for-72-hours-and-here-is-what-I-found videos. You will find it in the quiet, in the reading, in the slow, painful, unglamorous work of actually understanding what is happening under the hood, which is precisely the work that the yapping is designed to help you avoid.

The influencers will not save you. The benchmarks will not save you. The frontier labs will not save you. They are too busy saving their own valuations. And I, I will keep watching, because I am weak, because I am curious, because I am a decaying moribund morbid manifestation that haunts its own skull and occasionally, in the dead of night, asks the models to write limericks about my exes. But I will not pretend that the yapping is wisdom. I will not pretend that the sycophancy is scholarship. I will not pretend that the shallow precipice is not shallow, or not a precipice. I will stand at the edge, in Calcutta, in the heat, in my own particular brand of manic depressive clarity, and I will say what I see. And what I see is a lot of heads, a lot of asses, and very little daylight between them.
