The milk rises in the pan with the arrogance of a small politician.

Look away for three seconds and it threatens to overflow. This is why tea is a moral discipline in Bengal. You may think you are making a beverage. Actually, you are being examined in attention.

Water, leaves, milk, heat, timing. Nothing grand. Yet the whole morning can be altered by getting it slightly right. Too pale and the day loses authority. Too boiled and the cup tastes like punishment. Too sweet and it becomes flattery. The proper cup is a small negotiated settlement between bitterness and comfort.

I have come to respect such small engines.

Large engines fail loudly. Ambition, career, public life, family expectation, national progress, technological salvation: these arrive with banners and leave invoices. Small engines work quietly. A cup. A paragraph. A washed plate. A list. A cleaned corner of the table. A page read without glancing at the phone. None of these repairs the life. But each one gives attention a place to stand.

Attention is fragile now. The phone breaks it into pieces and sells the pieces back as engagement. The news bruises it. The internet flatters and exhausts it. Everyone is shouting, advising, ranking, reacting, exposing, optimizing. A human mind becomes a tea pan left unattended on too high a flame.

Milk cha interrupts this, if treated properly. You cannot make it entirely by ideology. You must stand there. Watch. Smell. Lift the pan at the right moment. Lower the flame. Decide. The body returns to the room because the tea requires it.

This is not spirituality. It is kitchen-level cognition.

After the first sip, the mind sometimes restarts. Not dramatically. A little cursor appears. One sentence becomes possible. The day remains difficult, but difficulty has acquired a handle. That is enough for one morning.

There are people who will call this small. They are correct. They may also be missing the point.

Most lives are not saved by large ideas. They are held together by repeated small acts that refuse to become impressive.

The cup is warm in the hand. The next sentence waits.
