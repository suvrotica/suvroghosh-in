One is a very small number when applied to a life.

One life. Not a spare folded in the cupboard. Not a revised edition with better margins. Not a second sitting after the invigilator has explained the question paper more kindly. One packet, opened once, often without instructions, usually under poor lighting.

Religion offers many installment schemes. I understand the appeal. A return ticket would be convenient. A cosmic correction would soften the arithmetic. It would be pleasant to think that a badly used decade could be reimbursed somewhere with interest.

I cannot believe it.

This leaves the matter blunt. At fifty-one, much of life has already migrated into hindsight. It did not request permission. It took years, chances, errors, rooms, journeys, ambitions, and left behind receipts. You look back and see where obedience was mistaken for virtue, where fear wore the clothes of prudence, where silence was called dignity, where a plan was postponed until postponement became the plan.

No rehearsal. No refund counter.

The temptation is to make this grand. But real life resists grandeur. It is the gas running out when the rice is half-cooked. It is a delayed payment. It is a leaking roof. It is the body ageing without consulting the calendar of unfinished work. It is a room in Calcutta where the city is still described as joyful by people who have not recently tried to solve an ordinary repair in May.

Still, one life is also one opening.

The number is small, but not zero. That matters. A person may be late, financially cornered, professionally uncertain, and still curious. He may read a paper, write a paragraph, learn a tool, explain a concept, help someone avoid a bad error, or notice a public lie before it hardens into common sense.

Usefulness does not have to arrive wearing a banner.

I am suspicious of cheerful reinvention stories. They polish suffering into a product. But I am equally suspicious of total surrender. The fact that a life has not become what it promised does not mean every remaining hour belongs to waste.

The honest scale is smaller now. Less conquest, more salvage. Less destiny, more attention. Less applause, more work that can survive inspection.

The cup is on the table. The day is not generous. But it is still unspent.

One is small. It is also what there is.
