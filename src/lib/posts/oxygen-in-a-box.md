The laptop screen lit up before the room did.

Its white rectangle floated above the table, bright, impolite, and almost medical in its suspicion of darkness. Around it lay the ordinary inventory: cup, cable, old bill, notebook, a spoon with dried tea near the handle. The air had gone stale in the way air goes stale when a man has been thinking too long without doing enough.

I opened the blog draft because the room needed a window.

Not a large one. I am no longer young enough to confuse writing with rescue. A paragraph does not pay rent. A sentence does not fix a leaking pipe or make a client remember an invoice. The internet is not a temple. It is often a market with bad lighting.

Still, the draft box gives the mind somewhere to exhale.

Without it, thoughts remain in circulation. They thicken. They reuse the same corners. Shame meets irritation, irritation meets memory, memory meets hunger, and soon the skull becomes a locked room after a long meeting.

Writing is not purity. Sometimes it is ventilation.

That is enough.

A blog is a strange object for a middle-aged Bengali man in a small Calcutta room. Too public to be a diary, too private to be a business, too careful to be a rant, too messy to be literature with a polished nameplate. It sits between categories, which may be why I trust it.

Maybe no one reads. Maybe one person does while waiting for a train, or sitting under a slow fan, or refusing to answer a message for five more minutes.

The cursor blinks.

It is not hope exactly.

But it keeps opening and closing, opening and closing, like a small mechanical lung.
