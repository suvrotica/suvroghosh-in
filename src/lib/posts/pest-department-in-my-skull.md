The cockroach appears near the tea pan with official confidence.

It pauses, moves its antennae, and seems to request paperwork. I stand in the kitchen holding a slipper and wondering whether the problem is the insect, the kitchen, the city, or the private department in my skull that has become too eager to classify irritants.

The word pest is dangerous because it pretends to be scientific when it is often only personal. A creature becomes a pest when it enters our arrangement and damages what we are trying to preserve. A mosquito near a pond is ecology. A mosquito near the ear at night is litigation.

The same slippage happens with people.

The queue-cutter. The gatekeeper. The smiling liar. The clerk who says tomorrow with the peaceful face of someone who has already murdered tomorrow. The neighbor whose small authority grows enormous on the building noticeboard. After enough encounters, the mind begins sorting people too quickly: useful, tolerable, risky, nuisance.

This is not a noble habit.

It may begin in real frustration. India supplies frustration generously. Bad design, weak rules, scarcity, delay, underpaid staff, inflated egos, fear, bribery, and the tiny theatre of everyday obstruction. A person can be blocked so often by petty power that he starts seeing patterns before seeing individuals.

The danger is that patterns are efficient and cruel.

Compassion enters late with a file. Perhaps the guard has been shouted at all morning. Perhaps the clerk's obstruction is job security. Perhaps the neighbor is lonely. Perhaps the liar learned long ago that truth here walks barefoot. All this may be true, and still the obstruction bites.

The slipper remains in my hand. The cockroach is only trying to survive in the crumbs we have provided.

That thought does not make me affectionate. It makes me careful.

A metaphor is not harmless just because it is private. The words we use inside the skull eventually arrange the furniture of our conduct.

I lower the slipper. The department remains under review.
