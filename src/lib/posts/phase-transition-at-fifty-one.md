The milk rose in the pan with a warning it had not earned.

One second it was obedient liquid, the next it was ambition with bubbles. I lowered the flame too late and a pale ring spread across the stove. This is how transformation often behaves in my room: less like revelation, more like cleaning duty.

A phase transition is a change of state. Ice to water. Water to steam. Milk to a small domestic accusation. The material remains related to itself, but its behavior alters. It flows, hardens, expands, escapes. Temperature rises. Pressure shifts. The old arrangement stops holding.

Fifty-one feels like that, though without the dignity of a textbook diagram.

I do not feel reborn. Rebirth is a word used by people who have excellent lighting. I feel reconfigured by heat. The old versions have not vanished. Student, worker, returnee, consultant, reader, failed planner, man with a rice cooker and too many tabs open in the mind. They remain in solution, visible when the cup is stirred.

But the state has changed.

Some ambitions no longer dissolve. Some humiliations settle at the bottom faster. Some desires evaporate before they can be named. The body, which used to be background infrastructure, now submits daily reports.

Science is useful here because it does not flatter. It says change may be ordinary, irreversible, and slightly messy. It says not every transition is progress. Some are merely what happens when enough pressure meets enough time.

The stove cools slowly. A thin skin forms where the milk dried.

Outside, Calcutta continues heating everything below the level of public complaint.

I scrape the stove with a spoon and think: perhaps this too is a kind of becoming.

Not beautiful.

Only difficult to reverse.
