Acronyms used in this post:

NCERT means National Council of Educational Research and Training, the Indian body that prepares school curriculum and textbooks.

R&D means Research and Development, the organized work of creating new knowledge, tools, medicines, machines, methods, and technologies.

GDP means Gross Domestic Product, the total value of goods and services produced in an economy.

GERD means Gross Expenditure on Research and Development, the share of national income spent on research and development.

DNA means Deoxyribonucleic Acid, the molecule that carries genetic information in living organisms.

IIT means Indian Institute of Technology, India's elite public engineering institutions.

AI means Artificial Intelligence, computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

The laboratory coat is the easiest costume for nonsense to steal.

That is the problem with pseudoscience in India. Not private belief sitting quietly with its lamp and flowers. Not a nervous prayer before an exam. The danger begins when superstition climbs onto the public stage, borrows a microphone, wears a laboratory coat, and announces that it has discovered quantum wisdom in a coconut.

Then the crowd claps.

Then television calls it a debate.

Then a forwarded video turns it into heritage.

Somewhere, in a classroom with a broken fan and a bored child, science quietly loses another citizen.

Private irrationality is a human tax. Everyone pays something. One person fears an unlucky sign. Another reads stock charts like scripture. A third believes a political party has never lied, which is one of the more dangerous superstitions available to civilians.

But the old local whisper now has broadband. A rumor that once moved through a shop, a priest, and a neighbor now travels by algorithm. A miracle seller no longer needs a tent near a station. He needs a ring light, a cheap microphone, a payment link, and a public tired enough to believe reality can be negotiated by video.

This is not harmless.

A half-literate society is not one where people cannot read. It is one where people can read the words but cannot inspect the claim. They can pass exams but cannot ask, "How do you know?" They can calculate marks and train times, but cannot separate evidence from costume.

Science is not a cupboard of facts. Science is a method for not fooling yourself too easily. It is the habit of asking what would prove you wrong. It is the patience to say, "I do not know yet." It separates a beautiful story from a working explanation.

A myth may be beautiful. It may carry poetry, memory, grief, identity, moral instruction, and old human bewilderment. But a myth is not a jet engine. A medicine must heal. A bridge must stand. A vaccine must work. An eclipse must arrive on time whether anyone approves or not.

Reality is wonderfully rude.

India is not short of intelligence. That lazy insult belongs in a municipal drain. India produces brilliant mathematicians, coders, doctors, physicists, engineers, biologists, statisticians, space scientists, and students who solve exam problems under pressure that would flatten many calmer societies.

The trouble is not intelligence.

The trouble is organization.

We built islands of excellence and left the mainland to superstition, coaching, politics, and memory work. We made IITs, space missions, and software exports, but did not build scientific culture deeply enough into ordinary life. Science became a subject, not a civic instinct.

So a student learns Newton's laws, then watches a guru explain that ancient sages already had all modern physics. Another learns a little genetics but never receives evolution in a way that rearranges the world in the mind. An engineer forwards nonsense about miracle cures. A panel discusses whether mythology contains advanced aviation. Someone says ancient science with the tone of a person placing a trump card on the table.

People can know formulas and still not know science.

Evolution matters because it is not only a biology chapter. It is the spine of modern life science. Without it, medicine becomes shallower, ecology confusing, genetics a pile of vocabulary, and human beings mysteriously dropped from the sky like mishandled luggage. When evolution is weakly taught, delayed, softened, or hidden for advanced students only, creationist thinking moves into the empty room early and arranges the furniture.

Children need the story early: variation, inheritance, selection, deep time, fossils, common ancestry, microbes changing, viruses adapting, crops improved by selection, the body carrying old compromises. Then biology becomes intelligible.

Without that, students memorize life.

They do not understand it.

The same failure appears across education. If the periodic table is a chart to memorize rather than one of humanity's great maps, chemistry becomes punishment. If probability is exam arithmetic rather than the grammar of uncertainty, people cannot understand medical risk, weather forecasts, polls, investment scams, or why one dramatic anecdote proves almost nothing.

If evidence is not taught, costume becomes evidence. Loudness becomes evidence. Majority belief becomes evidence.

This is how nonsense wins elections of the mind.

Politics has fed the beast. Vote banks do not thrive on critical citizens. They thrive on dependence. Give people identity, spectacle, grievance, ritual, and emotional compensation. Do not give them tools sharp enough to cut the rope. A questioning citizen is poor raw material for patronage politics.

That is why gurus, miracle sellers, and televised charlatans flourish under political shade. They are not merely entertainers. They are part of an economy of obedience. They teach that truth descends from authority: scripture, leader, celebrity, forwarded video, slow-voiced public figure.

The poor are often blamed for believing. That is too easy. People believe strange things because life is frightening. When hospitals are expensive, courts slow, police uncertain, work unstable, and the future looks like a clerk who has gone to lunch, magic becomes emotional insurance.

So the answer is not sneering.

Sneering is cheap rationalism. It warms the speaker and changes little.

The real work is harder. Build institutions that reduce desperation. Build schools that teach method. Build public health that does not leave people running to miracle sellers. Build media habits that punish fraud. Build law that targets harmful superstition without turning private belief into a police matter. Build a culture where "prove it" is normal.

Jawaharlal Nehru, for all his mistakes, understood science as a way of thinking, not only as machines and laboratories. The republic's Constitution asks citizens to develop scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform. That is a dangerous sentence, beautifully dangerous, because inquiry does not stop politely at a temple gate, party office, family group, television studio, or textbook.

India is behind China scientifically not because Indians are less clever, but because China treated science, manufacturing, engineering, infrastructure, and industrial capability as national power with terrifying seriousness. China is not a moral model. Its censorship, surveillance, and political control are not things a free society should envy.

But its scientific rise is real.

India cannot answer that rise with mythology, slogans, and television pride. You cannot build semiconductors by declaring ancient nanotechnology. You cannot catch up in biotechnology by forwarding untested cures. You cannot become a research power while treating R&D spending as decoration. Science needs money and freedom.

A scientist must be able to say the data does not support the minister. A historian must be able to say the evidence does not support the myth. A doctor must be able to say the cure is fraudulent. A teacher must be able to say the textbook is weak. A student must be able to ask why.

Without that, you get performance science: rockets in speeches, ancient glory in textbooks, innovation banners, startup slogans, and ceremonial buttons pressed while research scholars wait months for a reagent.

Scientific temper is built in lanes and classrooms, not only conferences. It is born when a child asks why the phone heats, why the sky darkens before rain, why fever rises, why antibiotics must be used properly, why eclipses are predictable, why caste is not biology, why a godman's claim needs testing, why a politician's claim needs numbers, why a video is not proof.

Teach probability before superstition hardens into adult confidence. Teach the difference between story and mechanism. A story explains meaning. A mechanism explains how something works. Both matter. Only one runs a power plant.

Teach teachers to say, "I do not know." That sentence should become respectable. Science begins when authority admits uncertainty without losing dignity.

Teach in Indian languages. English-only rationalism is a gated colony. If scientific temper does not speak Bengali, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Malayalam, Kannada, Odia, Assamese, Urdu, and the rest, it will remain an elite hobby.

Build local science media: experiments, explanations, debunking with humor, public health in plain language, evolution with markets and kitchens, probability with cricket, climate with sweat, chemistry with soap, physics with ceiling fans. The ordinary world is already a laboratory. We forgot to label it.

Regulate harmful fraud. A person may believe privately what he likes. The moment someone sells a false cure, tells patients to abandon evidence-based care, exploits grief for money, or endangers others, law should arrive.

The deeper fight is prestige. Right now nonsense has glamour. Science has PDFs. This is not a fair contest. Truth must learn to travel without becoming cheap. It must become alive.

India does not need to become less Indian to become more scientific. A mature civilization can preserve songs, food, festivals, stories, languages, griefs, jokes, gods for those who want them, and disbelief for those who do not. What it cannot preserve, if it wants to live, is the right to lie about reality without consequence.

The final enemy is not faith.

The final enemy is unaccountable certainty.

Science is not certainty.

Science is disciplined uncertainty.

That is why it works.
