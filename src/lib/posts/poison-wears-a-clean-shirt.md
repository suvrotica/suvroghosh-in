The clean shirt enters the room before the man does.

Crisp collar. Soft voice. Correct greeting. A face arranged into civility. Such people are easy to misread if you were raised to confuse polish with goodness. I was. Many of us were. We were told to notice manners, language, posture, education, family background, the visible signs of being properly brought up.

Poison has learned these signs.

It does not always arrive shouting. Often it arrives with a smile and a reasonable request. It says collaboration when it means free labor. It says opportunity when it means risk transferred downward. It says family when it means obligation without consent. It says positivity when it means silence about harm.

The crude villain is almost comforting. At least he saves time. The polished taker is more efficient. He makes you doubt your objection. He converts your discomfort into a personal failing. Perhaps you are too sensitive. Perhaps you misunderstood. Perhaps everyone does this. Perhaps asking to be treated fairly is itself a sign that you lack maturity.

This is how small social poisons work. They do not kill the day. They contaminate the water.

After enough exposure, a person becomes careful in ways that look unattractive from outside. He answers fewer calls. He pauses before saying yes. He reads the friendly message twice. He notices whether praise arrives just before a request. He becomes less generous with access to his time, and some people call this bitterness because they benefited from the earlier arrangement.

Calcutta, like every old social place, has a refined culture of surface. The polite phrase, the indirect pressure, the cousinly request, the small emotional invoice tucked into a conversation about nothing. To survive it without becoming cruel is difficult.

I do not want to become suspicious of everyone. That is another kind of poison. But I no longer trust the clean shirt by itself.

Manners are welcome. Evidence is better.

The man sits. The shirt remains immaculate. I listen to the request and wait for the stain.
