The kettle has not yet begun its thin, reedy whistle, the water still contemplating its molecular sloth, and I am already a dead man walking. Not dead in the clinical, pulseless sense that would relieve my creditors, but dead in the way a fluorescent tube is dead before it flickers—that stubborn, stuttering half-life where light is theoretically possible but practically a bureaucratic nightmare. I stand in my kitchen, which is not a kitchen but a galley of despair with a gas cylinder, and I measure out my survival in spoonfuls of Assam tea leaves. This is the golden hour. The only hour. The hour where I am, against all pharmacological odds, almost functional. Almost.

I did not choose this body. This body, this 51-year-old Bengali chassis with its faulty neurochemistry and its debt-ridden serotonin receptors, chose me in some cosmic accident of genetics and geography. I am a man who wakes up with negative expectations, which sounds pessimistic until you realize it is merely accurate forecasting. The mood will drop. It always drops. The only variable is the latency period, that brief, shimmering interval between consciousness and catastrophe where I might—might—get something started. A sentence. A thought. A piss taken with intention rather than desperation. The chronobiologists call this the cortisol awakening response, that sharp spike of glucocorticoids that evolution designed to jolt our ancestors into running from saber-toothed tigers. I have no tigers. I have only the mailman and my own amygdala, which is, if we are being honest, a far more persistent predator.

Here is the mathematics of my morning, rendered in the cold, unfeeling notation of someone who once studied computer science and now computes only his own decay. Let $t = 0$ be the moment of waking. Let $M(t)$ represent my mood as a function of time, where $M$ is measured in micrograms of willpower per deciliter of blood. We observe:

$$M(t) = M_0 e^{-\lambda t} + \epsilon(t)$$

Where $M_0$ is that initial, fraudulent burst of morning clarity, $\lambda$ is the decay constant of my dopaminergic system (approximately $0.7\ \text{hr}^{-1}$ in my case, though your mileage may vary), and $\epsilon(t)$ is the stochastic noise of existence—the neighbor's dog, the price of onions, the memory of something I said in 1997. The half-life of my competence is roughly fifty-eight minutes. After that, the curve flattens into the asymptote of the afternoon, that long, beige expanse where I am technically alive but operationally deceased.

I drink the first sip. It burns. Good. Pain is proof of interface, that I am still connected to the physical world, that the server has not yet crashed. The tea contains caffeine, 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine, a purine alkaloid that blocks adenosine receptors in the brain. Adenosine, that molecular sandman, accumulates during wakefulness like interest on a loan I never took out. The caffeine molecule, $C_8H_{10}N_4O_2$, wedges itself into the receptor sites like a drunk uncle at a wedding, preventing the drowsiness signal from reaching its destination. It is a beautiful hack, really. A denial-of-service attack on my own fatigue. For about ninety minutes, I am a functional human being. I can write. I can think. I can pretend that the day will not, inevitably, dissolve into the thick, syrupy morass of the depressive afternoon.

But the body keeps its own ledger. The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, that ancient trinity of stress and survival, has its own agenda. The paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone, which tells the anterior pituitary to release adrenocorticotropic hormone, which tells the adrenal cortex to release cortisol. This cascade, this Rube Goldberg machine of misery, was designed for emergencies. Mine fires at 6:30 AM with the regularity of a Japanese train, except the destination is not Osaka but oblivion. The cortisol rises, the mood falls. The tea cannot compete. Nothing can compete.

I have tried the drugs. Oh, how I have tried them. Lithium carbonate, that ancient salt of the earth, $Li_2CO_3$, which stabilizes mood by mechanisms we still do not fully understand—how comforting, to be medicated by mystery. Lamotrigine, an anticonvulsant repurposed for the convulsions of the soul. Quetiapine, the atypical antipsychotic that turns you into a doughy, somnolent approximation of yourself. These are the tools of my trade now, not compilers and algorithms, but molecules that modulate the reuptake of neurotransmitters I cannot see, in a brain I cannot fix, for a life I cannot quite justify. The drugs do not eliminate the depression; they merely flatten it, transform the sharp peaks and valleys into a featureless plain of mild despair. It is, as the clinicians say, "managed." Managed like a forest fire. Managed like a nuclear meltdown. Managed like my bank account.

The second cup of tea is a wake for the first. I know, even as I drink it, that the window is closing. The $\lambda$ in my personal equation is asserting itself. The $\epsilon(t)$ is growing louder. Soon I will be dragging this carcass, this sack of Bengali biomass, through the hours like a zombie in an apocalypse that consists entirely of paperwork and memory. Half-witted. Half-dead. Emotionally, entirely, thoroughly deceased. The zombie metaphor is apt, I think. Romero understood something fundamental about modernity: that the most terrifying thing is not death, but the persistence of motion after the death of meaning. I will answer emails. I will nod at people. I will perform the pantomime of productivity while internally, the lights are off and the shop is closed.

But now. Now, in this brief interregnum between sleep and stupor, I am almost a person. The tea is hot. The morning is, if not kind, at least indifferent rather than actively hostile. I can write this. I can observe the absurdity of my own neurochemistry with the detachment of a naturalist watching a dung beetle roll its ball. Look at him go, the little fellow. Look at him push that ball of excrement up the hill. He thinks it matters. He thinks he is getting somewhere. And perhaps, in the limited universe of the dung beetle, he is. Perhaps meaning is not found but made, manufactured in the brief, golden hour before the cortisol kills the cat.

The clock ticks. The tea cools. The curve descends. I am Sisyphus with a teacup, and the boulder is my own head. But for now, for this small, stolen, caffeinated moment, I am awake. I am writing. The words are coming, and they are not entirely terrible. That is not hope. Hope is too expensive a commodity for a bankrupt man. It is, rather, a brief cessation of the downward pressure, a momentary glitch in the system, a reprieve before the inevitable relapse into the long, gray afternoon where I will sit, and stare, and wonder how it is possible to be so tired after doing absolutely nothing at all.

The cup is empty. The hour is over. The zombie awakens, or rather, the man sleeps and the zombie walks. The tea leaves settle at the bottom like the sediment of my ambitions. Tomorrow, if there is a tomorrow—and the actuarial tables suggest there will be—I will do this again. I will wake. I will doubt. I will boil water. I will drink the bitter, brown liquid that briefly, mercifully, tricks my brain into thinking that it wants to be alive. And in that trickery, in that brief, fraudulent surge of $M_0$, I will find not happiness, but something adjacent to it. Something that looks, from a distance, and in bad lighting, almost like a reason to continue.
