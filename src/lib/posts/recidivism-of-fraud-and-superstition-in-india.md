The first smell is incense trying to defeat a staircase.

One stick burns beside a cracked tile deity. Below it, the drain conducts its own argument. Flowers, faith, damp concrete, phenyl, old water, and neglect mix into the familiar Indian proof that civilization often begins by covering a smell rather than removing it.

That is where the word recidivism belongs.

Recidivism usually means returning to crime after punishment or correction. But societies can return too. India returns to many things after being punished by them. Godmen after scandal. Astrologers after failed predictions. Miracle cures after hospital visits. Brokers after being cheated by brokers. Politicians after being betrayed by politicians. "Adjustment" after every previous adjustment made the system worse.

This is recurrence with ritual.

Fraud here comes in two costumes. Small fraud wears a sweaty shirt and sits on a plastic chair. It asks for a little extra, no receipt, one small service charge, one shortcut. Large fraud arrives in air-conditioning and says restructuring, market headwinds, liquidity stress, strategic exposure. The small fraud steals a fan. The large fraud steals the ceiling and calls it infrastructure.

Respectability is the washing machine.

Enough English, legal opinion, charity photographs, political proximity, and spiritual vocabulary can make theft look like vision.

Superstition works similarly. Private faith is one thing. A person whispering a name in the dark is not my target. Human beings need lamps; night is large. The problem is public superstition: the stage, the microphone, the donation box, the miracle product, the real estate, the political blessing, the loud certainty that evidence is bad manners.

Science asks: show me.

The room laughs.

Yet reality keeps records. Sugar rises. Debt compounds. Bridges crack. Banks fail. Bodies do not obey slogans. The false explanation may come with incense, Sanskrit, nationalism, corporate English, family pressure, or a soft voice. Reality remains unimpressed.

Fraud in India often arrives as reassurance.

Do not worry.

Those words should be handled like a live wire. Do not worry, the promoter is reliable. Do not worry, the file will move. Do not worry, the baba is genuine. Do not worry, the documents are manageable. Do not worry, it is only a small adjustment.

Then the floor gives way.

We return not always because we are fools. Sometimes we return because truth has a cost. Doubt has a cost. Evidence has a cost. Refusing the family astrologer has a cost. Refusing the bribe has a cost. Refusing to smile at the local fixer has a cost. Saying no may be morally clean and practically expensive.

So compromise gathers residue.

One grain behind the ribs. Then another. A society becomes heavy with swallowed disgust and calls it maturity.

The greatest recidivism may be public memory. We remember a family insult for thirty years, but forget a financial scam in three news cycles. We remember who took the larger piece of fish at lunch, but forget who signed the dangerous approval. Correction would require memory. Memory would require shame. Shame would require standards.

Standards are inconvenient before disaster.

Everyone wants punishment afterward. Fewer people want prevention: documents checked, receipts demanded, questions asked, shortcuts refused, evidence required, excitement delayed. Prevention is boring. It has no garland. It only keeps the house from burning.

Somewhere, another small fraud is being born over tea.

Someone says, "There is one way."

The sentence enters the room smiling.

The lock has already begun falling in love with the thief.
