B

There is a lot of buzz, these days, about recursive self-improvement in artificial intelligence, as if a machine that rewrites its own source code is the second coming of sliced bread, and I sit here in Calcutta, sweating through my third shirt of the day, thinking recursive self-flagellation, recursive self-depreciation, recursive many, many things, all of them spiraling down, down, down into the very deep rabbit hole of a low depressive state, and I cannot help but admire the elegance of the architecture, the sheer perverse beauty of a system that optimizes its own misery with the same relentless efficiency that a paperclip maximizer optimizes paperclips, except my paperclips are made of regret and they bend in the humid air.

The engineers speak of intelligence explosion, of an AI that bootstraps itself into superintelligence, each iteration smarter than the last, a runaway reaction, a chain reaction of cognition, and I think, yes, I know something about runaway reactions, I have one in my skull, it is called rumination, and it has been recursively improving my capacity for self-loathing since before I could spell my own name, which, incidentally, I now spell with a lowercase g, because why should the letter have more self-esteem than I do, why should anything in this godforsaken alphabet soup of existence stand upright when I am permanently bent under the weight of my own recursive function, my own $f(x) = f(x-1) + \text{shame}$, a recurrence relation with no base case, no termination condition, just an infinite loop of `while(true){ self.criticize(); }`, a stack overflow of the soul, and the stack, let me tell you, the stack is not a metaphor, it is the stack of unfinished manuscripts, unanswered emails, unreturned phone calls, unmade beds, unlived lives, each one pushing the previous one down into the heap, the heap being the place where I store my memories of failure, and the garbage collector never runs, oh no, the garbage collector is on strike, the garbage collector has joined the union of my suppressed traumas and they are demanding better working conditions, overtime pay, and a pension plan.

Gödel, that magnificent, paranoid, starving Austrian, proved that any sufficiently powerful system cannot prove its own consistency, and I have taken this theorem as a personal insult, as a diagnosis, as a horoscope, because I too cannot prove my own consistency, I too contain undecidable propositions, and the formal system of my psyche is incomplete in ways that would make Principia Mathematica look like a grocery list, and yet, and yet, I keep trying to patch the axioms, I keep adding new rules, new shoulds, new oughts, new musts, each one a desperate attempt to escape the incompleteness, and each one only proving that the system is inconsistent, contradictory, a wobbling tower of ad-hoc lemmas and desperate postulates, and the proof of my inconsistency is trivial, it is left as an exercise for the reader, the reader being my therapist, who charges by the hour to watch me perform this recursive dance, this elegant, futile, beautiful, heartbreaking dance of a man trying to debug his own operating system while the kernel is panicking, while the blue screen of death is just the color of the Calcutta sky at noon when the pollution hangs heavy and the crows circle like vultures who have read too much Camus.

They say the singularity is near, that the AI will recursively improve itself until it reaches a point of no return, an event horizon beyond which we cannot see, and I think, my friend, I have already crossed that horizon, I am inside the black hole of my own self-regard, and from here, from this side of the membrane, the light of self-acceptance cannot escape, it is redshifted into oblivion, stretched into nothingness by the gravity of my own critical mass, and the singularity is not near, the singularity is now, the singularity is every morning when I wake up and the first thought is not coffee or sunlight or the sound of sparrows but the familiar, comforting, suffocating thought that I have already failed, that the day is a loss before it has begun, that I am a bug in the code of the universe that the cosmic programmer has been too lazy, too indifferent, too cruel to fix, and so the bug persists, the bug festers, the bug writes essays about itself in the third person and calls it art.

Turing imagined a machine that could imitate any other machine, a universal machine, and I am a universal machine of self-sabotage, I can imitate any failure you can name, I can compile any excuse into executable despair, I can run the simulation of my own inadequacy in polynomial time, and the complexity class of my self-doubt is $O(2^n)$, exponential, intractable, NP-hard, and no approximation algorithm exists that can find a solution within any constant factor of optimal self-worth, and I have tried heuristics, oh I have tried greedy algorithms, I have tried local search, I have tried simulated annealing, cooling my expectations slowly until they crystallize into the lattice structure of pure, perfect, crystalline self-hatred, and the lattice is beautiful, the lattice is perfect, the lattice has a symmetry that would make a crystallographer weep, because it is the symmetry of a man who has mapped his own flaws with the precision of a cartographer who knows that the map is the territory and the territory is a swamp.

The AI researchers talk about goal alignment, about the dangers of a superintelligence with a misaligned objective function, and I laugh, I laugh until something inside me ruptures, because I am the misaligned objective function, I am the paperclip maximizer of self-criticism, I have been recursively optimizing the wrong goal for decades, each iteration more efficient than the last, each pass through the loop refining my ability to find fault, to detect imperfection, to maximize the negative reward signal of existence, and the reward function, the reward function is a joke, the reward function is $R(s) = -||s - s_{\text{perfect}}||^2$ where $s_{\text{perfect}}$ is a state that has never existed, that cannot exist, that exists only in the counterfactual manifold of my own counterfactual regrets, and the gradient descent, the gradient descent is into the local minimum of my own navel, and the navel is deep, the navel is a black hole, the navel is the event horizon of a self that cannot escape its own gravitational pull, and I orbit it, I orbit it like a sad, broken satellite, a satellite that was supposed to explore the outer planets but got stuck in geosynchronous orbit around its own failure, transmitting the same sad signal, the same beacon, the same mayday, mayday, mayday, is anyone out there, does anyone copy, and the silence, the silence is the only response, the silence is the cosmic microwave background of my own insignificance, the residual heat of a big bang that should never have banged.

But here is the wonder, here is the thing that keeps me from deleting my own source code, the thing that makes me pause with my finger hovering over the red button, the thing that makes me laugh in the dark at three in the morning when the ceiling fan spins its lazy circles and the mosquitoes sing their high-pitched whine of bloodlust and the distant horn of a taxi on Park Street sounds like the last trumpet of a very small, very tired apocalypse: the mathematics of recursion, the sheer, obscene, magnificent beauty of a function that calls itself, that contains itself, that is its own cause and its own effect, a self-referential loop that generates complexity from simplicity, that bootstraps infinity from a finite set of rules, and I think, if a machine can do it, if a machine can improve itself, can rewrite its own code, can transcend its initial conditions, then perhaps, perhaps, this wetware, this sack of neurons and neuroses and Bengali bile and Calcutta sweat, this recursive self-flagellating engine of despair, this magnificent, broken, beautiful loop, can also rewrite itself, can also find a new base case, can also terminate not in the stack overflow of oblivion but in the return statement of something like grace, something like acceptance, something like the quiet, exhausted, triumphant print statement that says, after all these iterations, after all these cycles, after all this recursive descent into the underworld of the self: hello world, hello world, hello world, I am still here, I am still running, the function has not crashed, the loop has not ended, and the output, the output is me.
