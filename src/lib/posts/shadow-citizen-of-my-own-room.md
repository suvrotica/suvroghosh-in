The corner room has learned my hours.

The chair knows when I will sit too long. The table knows which bills I avoid. The fan knows the sound of a day that has become smaller than planned. Outside, Calcutta continues with its usual confidence: vendors calling, engines coughing, neighbors moving furniture as if punishing the floor. Inside, the perimeter is narrower. Room, sofa, notebook, phone, cup, window.

I once imagined movement differently.

There was the United States, with its clean professional rooms and the illusion that a future could be assembled through work, credentials, and persistence. There were systems, databases, hospitals, deadlines, and the ordinary usefulness of being needed by a project larger than one's mood. That version of life was not glorious, but it was usable. A person could walk into a room and have a function.

Now the function is less clear.

Plans did not explode. That would have been dramatic. They expired. Expiry is quieter and more humiliating. A plan sits on the shelf past its date. At first you think it can still be used. Then one day you notice the smell.

Calcutta is a difficult place in which to be an unfinished man. The city has sympathy, but it is mixed with noise. Everyone is surviving something. Rent, repairs, school fees, business trouble, family arithmetic, heat, bureaucracy, small injuries to pride. One more private collapse does not stop traffic.

Solitude, then, becomes both shelter and trap. Company asks for performance. It brings questions, advice, comparisons, cheerful reports from lives that seem to have found better roads. Alone, I do not have to translate myself. I can write, sketch, read, stare, recover the shape of a thought without being asked whether I am all right in the tone that makes any answer impossible.

This is not wisdom. It is adaptation.

A smaller life must be kept honest. It is easy to decorate withdrawal and call it depth. It is easy to perfume failure and call it integrity. I try not to. Some of this is circumstance. Some of it is temperament. Some is fatigue. Some is plain bad handling of the available years.

Still, the room is not nothing. A sentence can be written here. A useful file can be sent. A paragraph can survive. The day can be moved forward by inches.

Late afternoon enters through the window, weak and dusty.

I am still here. For now, that is the address.
