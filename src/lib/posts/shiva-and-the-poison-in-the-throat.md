The throat tightens before the polite voice appears.

The phone rings. The number is familiar. The sentence is familiar too. Two more days. Just a little delay. Accounts has processed it. Please understand. All the soft cushions placed around one hard fact: payment has not come.

I say, "Yes, all right."

The throat keeps the rest.

This is where the old story of Shiva holding poison begins to make domestic sense. Not as calendar art, not as festival decoration, but as daily mechanics. The poison cannot be swallowed, because it will enter the whole body. It cannot be spat out, because the room will become unlivable. So it is held in the throat, glowing invisibly, while ordinary conversation continues.

Indian life trains this muscle early.

The unpaid favor. The delayed fee. The relative who borrows dignity along with time. The client who praises quality and discounts labor. The official who says come tomorrow with the calm of a man distributing weather.

Restraint is praised as character by people who benefit from it. From inside, it feels like carrying heat behind the tongue.

I am not heroic. The throat does not become blue. It becomes tired. The body records what language refuses to release. That is the private cost of "no problem."

After the call, the phone cools in my hand. The tea has gone flat. The invoice remains on the table with its patient face.

Payment did not come.

Nectar did not come.

The kettle still works.

I put water in it and let the throat wait.
