At three in the morning, the mind becomes a bad lawyer.

It repeats itself and calls the repetition evidence. It builds metaphors from damp cardboard. It invents an opponent, defeats him easily, then returns to the same charge with fresh stationery. The ceiling fan turns. The room is dark. Somewhere a vehicle passes. Inside the head, a court nobody elected continues proceedings.

The first trick is tautology.

I am failing because I am a failure. Nobody calls because I am forgettable. The future is closed because it feels closed. These sentences look like explanations, but they are only circles wearing shoes. They begin and end in the same accusation. No new evidence enters. The mind mistakes repetition for proof because repetition has rhythm.

The second trick is metaphor.

Metaphor can clarify. It can also poison. One says, "My life is a locked room," and suddenly every window is ignored. One says, "I am a machine with broken parts," and the body becomes a workshop of contempt. One says, "Everyone has moved ahead," and life becomes a railway platform where the self is permanently late.

Bad metaphors are not decorative. They organize perception.

The third trick is the straw man.

The mind invents a cheerful idiot who says, "Everything is fine." Then it defeats him, because everything is not fine. This feels intelligent. It is often avoidance. The real question is more difficult: what is wrong, what is not wrong, what can be done, what must be endured, and what is simply being exaggerated by fatigue?

At night, fatigue removes proportion.

A small task becomes a life verdict. A delayed reply becomes abandonment. A bodily sensation becomes prophecy. A memory becomes destiny. The mind connects every dot, including dots that were not on the page. The result is not insight. It is a conspiracy of private reasoning.

I do not say this from a mountain of serenity.

I say it from ordinary rooms where the fan makes its tired circle and the phone sits nearby like a small black witness. I know the appeal of bad logic. It gives shape to pain. It says, at least, here is the reason. A false reason can feel better than no reason at all.

But false reasons collect interest.

They make the next night easier to enter and harder to leave. The tautology grows smoother. The metaphor hardens. The imaginary opponent becomes familiar. Soon the mind has built a little court system that runs automatically whenever the lights go out.

The remedy is not instant positivity.

That is only another bad argument, this time wearing clean clothes. The better practice is cross-examination. What evidence would count against this thought? Am I using a metaphor as if it were a measurement? Have I defined myself by the worst hour? Is this a fact, a fear, a memory, or a sentence that sounds powerful because I am tired?

Writing helps because paper is slower than panic.

A sentence on paper can be inspected. A sentence inside the head behaves like smoke. Once written, the thought loses some of its priestly authority. It becomes grammar again, and grammar can be revised.

Three in the morning is not a philosopher.

It is a time of low resources, old files, and poor lighting. Important decisions should not be outsourced to it without appeal.

The fan turns. The court adjourns badly. Morning will not solve everything.

But morning has better evidence.
