The tea sat in the cup like a small brown judge.

It had overboiled, darkened, and acquired that stern surface which suggests the beverage has read your file and found omissions. I looked at it and wondered what probability could explain this blog: a middle-aged Bengali man in Calcutta, writing about rice, mathematics, failed plans, invoices, old competence, and the weather inside a room.

The chance cannot be high.

A more smoothly arranged life might not have produced it. A busier life would have spent the sentences elsewhere. A more prosperous life might have purchased better distractions. Comfort is not silent, but it does fill the room with furniture. It gives a man errands, plans, calls, dinners, obligations, repairs, small pleasures, larger bills, reasons to leave.

A constricted life leaves space of a harsher kind.

Into that space comes a laptop, a cup, a headache, a sentence, a memory of America, a bad day in Calcutta, a thought about databases, a thought about a childhood lane, a thought about why intelligence does not automatically become livelihood. The blog is not born from one cause. It is a probability cloud of inconvenience.

Writing did not solve the room.

But it changed the air slightly. A sentence takes pressure and gives it shape. A paragraph makes a ledge. One can stand there for a minute and look around without falling directly into the afternoon.

Maybe that is enough explanation.

The fan turns hot air from one side to the other. The tea cools. The cursor waits with the patience of a small instrument.

I take a sip.

The judge remains unconvinced, but allows proceedings to continue.
