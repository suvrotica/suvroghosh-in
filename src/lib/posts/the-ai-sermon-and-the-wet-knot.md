The knot in the string had become wet, and that made it worse. Dry knots can sometimes be teased apart with patience. Wet knots tighten under the finger, darken, and sit there with the quiet arrogance of a small problem that has learned endurance.

The modern advice to anxious workers has begun to feel like that knot.

Use AI.

That is the sermon. Use AI, learn AI, prompt better, automate yourself, become ten times more productive, turn uncertainty into opportunity, do not be left behind. The words arrive with the warmth of advice and the hardness of threat.

Some of it is true. AI can help. It can draft, summarize, translate, explain, compare, debug, brainstorm, classify, and reduce the terror of a blank page. A person who refuses useful tools out of pride may suffer unnecessarily. Skill still matters, and new skill always demands some discomfort.

But "use AI" has become too easy a sentence.

It treats the worker as if the only missing piece is technique. Learn the tool and the economy will make room. Become faster and demand will appear. Produce more and value will follow. Sound fluent and trust will arrive.

That is not how markets behave.

A person can learn AI and still have no clients. A student can master prompts and still face a thin hiring market. A designer can generate concepts faster and still lose work because buyers now expect ten concepts for the price of one. A programmer can ship drafts faster and still be judged by systems that value headcount reduction more than craft.

Productivity is not the same as bargaining power.

That distinction is missing from the sermon. The tool may make output cheaper. Cheaper output may help some workers and weaken others. If everyone can produce more, the market may simply raise the expected amount of production. The gain does not automatically travel to the person doing the work.

History is full of tools that increased productivity while leaving many workers tired, replaceable, and grateful for smaller pieces of the benefit.

AI intensifies this because it works on language, code, analysis, media, and judgment-like tasks. It enters the polished parts of work, the places where people once signaled competence. It can make a nervous beginner look fluent and a careless manager look strategic. It can also expose how much paid work consisted of formatting uncertainty into confident documents.

That exposure may be useful. It may also be cruel.

The people giving the sermon often have cushions: capital, audience, credentials, networks, or time. They can experiment. They can pivot. They can absorb failed months. The person receiving the sermon may have rent due, family obligations, an old laptop, weak internet, no client pipeline, and no room for heroic reinvention.

Telling that person to "just use AI" is not advice. It is a refusal to see the knot.

The better advice is slower and less exciting. Use AI for tasks where it genuinely saves time. Learn enough to avoid being frightened by it. Build judgment so you can inspect its output. Preserve skills that make you useful when the machine is plausible but wrong. Document your work. Find people who need outcomes, not words. Understand the business around the tool.

And admit that not all economic pain is a training problem.

Some pain comes from weak demand. Some from concentration of power. Some from platforms that capture value. Some from employers shifting risk downward. Some from education that prepared people for forms of work now being repriced. Some from the old fact that there are more competent people than decent opportunities.

AI may become a ladder for some. It may become a treadmill for others.

That is why policy, institutions, schools, unions, firms, and professional communities matter. The future of work cannot be solved by individual prompt discipline alone. A society cannot ask every person to become a one-person startup and then act surprised when most of them are exhausted.

In Calcutta, a wet knot is not a philosophical problem. It is a thing in the hand, resistant and ordinary. You work at it carefully because pulling harder makes it tighter.

The sermon keeps playing from the screen. Use AI. Learn AI. Become faster. The knot remains, dark with water, waiting for someone to admit it is more than string.
