The AC compressor started outside the wall with a metallic cough.

For three seconds, the room stopped accusing me. The sound was not beautiful. It had no music in it, only metal, wiring, and the small violence of a machine deciding to work. But it interrupted the inward rotation, and some interruptions are mercy.

A black hole is a region where gravity becomes so strong that escape is no longer available past a boundary. The boundary is called the event horizon. Physics gives the idea grandeur. Daily life makes a smaller version.

There are afternoons with an event horizon.

You begin with one thought: the unpaid work. Then another: the failed plan. Then another: the message unanswered. Then the body, the room, the old road not taken, the future with its bad manners. Each thought pulls the next one inward. Soon nothing escapes cleanly.

Then the compressor coughs.

The air shifts. The skin cools. The prosecution pauses. For a moment I am only a man in a room with an appliance making more effort than expected.

It is not salvation. It is a mechanical edit.

Still, editing matters. A life can become unbearable not only because of large facts but because there are no commas. Everything runs into everything else. Heat into shame, shame into memory, memory into the calendar, calendar into money, money into the body.

The compressor inserts a break.

Outside, the unit rattles. Inside, the curtain moves slightly. I place one hand on the table and feel the faint vibration pass through wood into bone.

The black hole remains.

But for a few seconds, the machine holds the edge open.
