My future does not have a road in front of it. It has a moat.

Unfortunately, the moat has mosquitoes.

Not the grand moat of history books, with stone walls and royal posture. Mine is a Calcutta drain-moat: greenish, patient, plastic floating in it, the smell arriving before the philosophy. On certain mornings, before the tea boils, it feels less like metaphor than municipal planning.

Middle-class descent rarely arrives as a cliff.

It is a staircase. One polite step at a time. A delayed payment. A repair postponed. A shirt kept beyond its dignity. Dental work deferred. Pride adjusted. The old phrase white collar begins to feel less like identity and more like laundry.

Education complicates the fall.

It lets you understand the mechanism while still failing to stop it. You can explain databases, uncertainty, systems, and institutions, yet still stare at next month's money like a student facing an exam in a subject nobody taught.

That is the moat.

On this side: certificates, old competence, jokes, stubbornness, unpaid invoices, and enough vanity to keep using complete sentences.

On the other side: stability, or something dressed like it.

Between them: buzzing, heat, water, expense, and the daily question of what must be crossed first.

Application? Rice? Shaving? A call? A bill? The day does not permit heroic strategy. It demands triage.

A mosquito lands on my ankle with excellent confidence.

I miss when I slap.

The moat remains impressed by nobody.
