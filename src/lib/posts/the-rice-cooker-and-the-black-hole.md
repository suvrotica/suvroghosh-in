The rice cooker clicks from cook to warm with a small authority the universe has never managed.

I respect that click. It says something has completed. Water has entered grain. Heat has done its work. The machine, cheap and slightly stained, has produced an edible fact. In a life full of open loops, this is no minor achievement.

A black hole is a region where gravity has become so intense that even light cannot escape once it crosses the boundary called the event horizon. This is the plain version. No velvet. No cosmic incense. Matter goes in. Information becomes difficult. The outside observer sees only the edge and must infer the rest.

Some days the mind feels like that edge.

Money worry goes in. Work worry goes in. Age goes in. Old plans go in. News goes in. A sentence someone said years ago goes in. Nothing returns in the same shape. The interior becomes unavailable. From outside, a man is merely standing beside a rice cooker in a Calcutta room, waiting for lunch.

Domestic routine matters because it resists the pull. Wash rice. Measure water. Press switch. Wait. Eat. These are not profound actions, but they anchor the body to a sequence. Cosmic despair dislikes sequence. It prefers abstraction, vastness, indefinite falling. The rice cooker says: first this, then that.

There is a mercy in machines with limited purpose. The rice cooker does not ask about meaning. It does not optimize my brand. It does not predict the labor market. It does not recommend a new life strategy. It cooks rice. The modesty is almost philosophical.

People speak of thinking big. Sometimes the task is to think small enough to survive the hour.

Steam fogs the lid. The room smells faintly of lunch. Outside, the city continues its larger collapses and continuations.

The cooker clicks. I open it and let the heat rise.
