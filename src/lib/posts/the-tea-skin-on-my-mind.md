The tea had grown skin again.

That thin beige film floated on top of the cup with the authority of a government seal. It forms when milk tea is abandoned too long. In my case, abandoned means fourteen minutes, two feet from my hand, while I stand in the kitchen and vanish into thought.

This is how a day can begin: not with ambition, but with uncollected tea.

My mind does not rest. It patrols. It reopens files, questions old decisions, replays conversations, calculates futures, doubts the calculation, then doubts the doubt. Thinking would be respectable. Thinking wears spectacles and attends lectures. This is less dignified. It is a warehouse of small clerks working overtime without supervision.

The phrase "I do not know" has always seemed beautiful to me. Clean, modest, accurate. A steel spoon. Unfortunately, many rooms prefer certainty. Certainty enters with a loud voice and behaves as if volume were evidence. To say "I do not know" is often to lower one's market price.

So I observe more than I speak.

Observation has its own cost. You begin to notice pauses, delays, status signals, the half-second laugh that follows power rather than wit, the small pressure changes in conversation. A normal person hears talk. I hear plumbing.

This is not superiority. Superiority would be comfortable. This is seeing too much and trusting oneself too little. The mind becomes a detective and a frightened schoolboy at the same time.

The tea skin thickens while all this internal administration continues. That is the insult. The actual world is doing simple chemistry. Milk proteins, cooling, surface film. The mind is building a court case about existence.

Perhaps the cup is wiser.

It does not ask where I belong. It merely cools when neglected.

I lift it, break the skin with a spoon, and drink what remains.
