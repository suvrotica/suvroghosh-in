The bathroom mirror had fogged only where my breath reached it.

Above the mist hovered my face: neither young nor properly old, neither dramatic nor especially memorable, the kind of face that enters a room without causing any administrative difficulty. The world does not object. That is part of the insult.

Quantum mechanics, dragged down to bathroom level, is useful here.

Before observation, a particle may be described as existing in several possible states. Once measured, the possibilities collapse into one result. A job application behaves similarly. Before being read, I am experienced, curious, capable, still useful. After observation, I become a line in a pile, a date, a cost, a maybe, a no.

This is not physics as science. It is physics as household metaphor, and therefore only half respectable.

Still, the metaphor knows something.

A middle-aged man can live in superposition for surprisingly long. Potential consultant. Former bright boy. Reader. Writer. Failed applicant. Useful mind. Unmarketed service. Human being. The states coexist until the world looks directly and asks for one clean answer.

Then collapse.

Not always catastrophic. Sometimes merely clerical.

Hope also has a half-life. In physics, half-life is the time it takes for half of a radioactive substance to decay. In ordinary life, it is the time between sending a careful email and accepting that no reply is coming.

The mirror clears.

Observation complete.

The face is still mine, which is both the bad news and the available data.
