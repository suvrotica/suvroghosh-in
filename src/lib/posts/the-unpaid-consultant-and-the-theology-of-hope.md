The cruelest work is not free work.

Free work at least arrives honestly. It says charity, favor, foolishness, cousin's project, please help. You may refuse it or resent it, but the label is visible.

The dangerous thing is almost-paid work.

It speaks English. It says consulting. It arrives with a call, a shared document, a polite timeline, and the soft glow of future payment. "Just help us now," it says. "Once this clears, we will settle everything."

Then it borrows the mind.

Hope is the fuel here. Not pure hope. The more embarrassing kind. Hope with invoices attached. Hope that checks UPI. Hope that rereads a message to see whether "soon" has become "today." Hope that knows better and still opens the laptop.

A paid invoice is wonderfully boring. That is its virtue. It arrives. It leaves a record. It confirms that time happened and was not merely converted into someone's convenience.

An unpaid consultant hungers for that confirmation as much as for the amount. The money matters, yes. But so does the proof that one's work entered the world as more than vapor.

Calcutta is full of soft promises. The city has perfected the tone: affectionate, delayed, plausible, impossible to deposit.

I have fallen for it too often.

Not because I believed every sentence. Because I wanted one of them to become solid.

The laptop waits.

Next time, I tell myself, let hope arrive with a transaction ID.
