## How to read the threads

Begin with a chapter, or choose a year and follow what the atlas has selected on either side. Every record carries a place and a date at the precision its evidence supports. Open its sources to follow the claim back to the archive.

A thread is an invitation to look across. **Shared time** places records beside one another in a common period. **Thematic comparison** is an editorial pairing. A **documented relationship** requires evidence of an actual connection. The first two do not quietly become the third.

Calcutta is the centre of the left ribbon; Bengal and India enter as explicitly named context. The American ribbon identifies national, state or city scope where appropriate. These are selected windows into lived worlds. Empty space records a gap in this selection.

## Dates have edges

The common spine uses Gregorian chronology. A precise day, a month, a year and a documented span make different claims. An event known only to a year has broader date precision; it has not thereby continued from January to December. Uncertainty about a date is separate from the duration of an event.

The historical edition makes comparisons at the resolution the sources allow. It does not manufacture an hour of the day, or apply a modern time-zone offset to an earlier period. A postcard preserves the precision and relationship of its selected comparison. Its caption belongs to that evidence.

## A life need not become a dataset

This edition is history, not an invented memoir. A future Calcutta–Texas edition could follow publicly chosen personal memories across the same instant, but that would require the author's explicit choices about what belongs in public. A marriage, a hospital shift, an illness or a period of silence is never owed to a visualization.

Here, the calendars offer a smaller invitation: pause inside a year, look across, and notice how much a date can hold.
