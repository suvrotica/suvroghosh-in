The old company backpack hangs from the chair long after the job has gone. That is how the future may begin: not with torn clothes, but with an expired ID card, a neat email signature, and a person still saying basically before explaining why the economy has temporarily misplaced him.

The future begging in Indian cities may not always stand at the traffic signal. Some of it will arrive as a link.

AI will not create precarity from nothing. India already has underemployment, credential inflation, unpaid hustle, fake meritocracy, informal survival, family pressure, and polite despair. AI may do something more specific: weaken the white-collar promise that education, English, software, and office work were a staircase out of chaos.

At first the fall will be renamed transition. Analyst becomes consultant. Consultant becomes freelancer. Freelancer becomes trainer. Trainer becomes course seller. Course seller becomes community builder. Community builder becomes lead-generation expert. The language remains scented while the income becomes irregular.

White-collar mendicancy will ask not only for money, but for belief.

Please subscribe. Please refer. Please endorse. Please attend the webinar. Please fund the course. Please join the group. Please scan the code. Please believe I am still useful.

The older street beggar embarrasses conscience. The displaced white-collar hustler may embarrass intelligence. He speaks your language. He knows your fears. He knows your child needs work, your savings are thin, your family pride is fragile, your future is expensive, and your fear of falling is already awake at night.

Fraud can grow from this soil.

Not because every displaced worker becomes dishonest. Most will simply struggle. But the gap between obligation and legitimate income creates pressure. A person exaggerates a skill. Sells a service he only half understands. Hides a risk. Charges an advance. Delays delivery. Discovers that sincerity and deception use some of the same tools: confidence, urgency, testimonials, neat formatting, and a story about temporary difficulty.

AI lowers the cost of simulation. A rented room can look like a company. A single person can produce customer support, certificates, apology emails, legal-sounding language, fake training material, dashboards, and outreach at scale. The small scam becomes less artisanal.

The fashionable districts will see polished versions first: reskilling bootcamps, guaranteed placement claims, trading groups, fake productivity systems, miracle workflows, imported-sounding credentials. The poorer edges will receive cheaper copies: loan pressure, forged documents, fake call-center jobs, exam coaching traps, medical rumors, QR-code spirituality, and the familiar man who can arrange something for a fee.

The infection is not the people. It is the broken bargain moving through them.

India sold white-collar respectability as household insurance. A job in IT or finance was not only salary. It was rent, parent care, school fees, emergency savings, and social weather protection. When that protection weakens, the injury is not only economic. It is loss of legibility.

The dangerous group is the educated precarious: credentialed but insecure, articulate but cornered, ashamed but visible, angry but not organized. They will not merely suffer the future. They will interpret it, package it, and sometimes sell pieces of that interpretation to one another.

The response must be practical and unsentimental. Teach people the anatomy of digital fraud. Protect freelancers from nonpayment. Build real employment exchanges, not decorative portals. Tie training to actual demand. Enforce small fraud as well as large fraud. Create safe public systems so the crossing, the inbox, and the family chat do not become parallel welfare offices run by panic.

Personally, the ethic must be colder and kinder: verify before trusting, refuse without contempt, help through channels that can be examined, avoid urgency, avoid secrecy, and remember that desperation with polished English is still desperation.

The backpack remains on the chair. The person opens another tab. The new street may be digital, but the asking has already begun.
