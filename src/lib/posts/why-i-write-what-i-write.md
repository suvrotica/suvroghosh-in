The pen scratches because the thought will not sit quietly.

That is the simplest explanation. I write, draw, joke, exaggerate, cut, rearrange, and return to the page because certain pressures become worse when left unshaped. They do not need applause. They need a route out of the skull.

This is not a theory of art. It is plumbing with better nouns.

Language is a narrow pipe. Entire monsoons of feeling are expected to pass through it while wearing formal shoes. Some days the sentence clogs. Some days the joke arrives before the truth because truth, by itself, would be too heavy to lift. Some days a drawing says what a paragraph cannot: the angle of a face, the tired suspicion in an eye, the absurdity of a public man inflated beyond his moral weight.

I do not write because I have solved anything. That would be fraud. I write because I have not solved it, and the unsolved thing keeps knocking.

The internet has made this both easier and more ridiculous. A person can publish from a small room in Calcutta and be ignored by almost the entire planet in less than a second. This is a technological miracle of some purity. Earlier, obscurity required effort. Now it is distributed at broadband speed.

Still, obscurity has its uses. It lets the work remain slightly honest. One is not forced to please a crowd that has not arrived. One can write about a tooth, a bus, a bad mood, a public lie, a statistical idea, a half-dead ambition, and not pretend they belong to separate universes. A blog is unfashionable enough to be free.

Drawing helps because words can become too clever. A line is harder to flatter. It either carries weight or it does not. Satire helps because anger without form becomes noise. Science helps because reality, however unpleasant, is still preferable to decorative illusion.

What I write is therefore not a brand. It is a pattern of return. I return to Calcutta, to systems, to the lower-middle-class arithmetic of money and dignity, to the lies people tell in polished language, to the small pleasures that keep a day from turning entirely grey.

If there is a purpose, it is not to inspire. Inspiration is often just a sugar packet handed out at the site of structural failure.

The purpose is to look, to name, to leave a mark.

A cup ring on the table. A line on paper. A sentence that says: this was the weather here.
