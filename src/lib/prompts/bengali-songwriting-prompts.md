A Bengali song must live in a throat, not merely look elegant on a page. These prompts keep script, pronunciation, stress, breath, register and cultural setting visible while leaving the final judgement with a Bengali-speaking writer and singer.

## Intended use

Use the master prompt to draft a complete song, the chorus-first variation to test a central refrain before writing verses, and the revision variation to examine an existing lyric without bleaching its voice.

## Suitable inputs

Provide the emotional situation, narrator, addressee, setting, musical form, tempo, vocal character, desired register and images you have genuinely observed. Name any phrases, themes or easy clichés that must not appear.

## Bengali-songwriting master prompt

Write an original Bengali song using the following brief.

### Song brief

- Emotional register: [MOOD AND EMOTIONAL CONTRADICTION]
- Narrator: [WHO IS SPEAKING, AGE OR SOCIAL POSITION IF RELEVANT]
- Addressee: [WHO OR WHAT IS BEING ADDRESSED]
- Cultural and physical setting: [PLACE, PERIOD, WEATHER, ROOM, STREET OR OCCASION]
- Bengali register: [MODERN KOLKATA COLLOQUIAL, STANDARD COLLOQUIAL, LITERARY OR DELIBERATE MIX]
- Narrator's voice: [DIRECT, WRY, TENDER, ANGRY, RESTRAINED, THEATRICAL OR OTHER]
- Musical form: [VERSE–CHORUS, BAUL-INFLUENCED FORM, BALLAD, CABARET, FOLK-ROCK OR OTHER FORM]
- Tempo and pulse: [BPM OR SLOW, MODERATE, QUICK; STRAIGHT, SWUNG OR OTHER FEEL]
- Approximate line length or meter: [SYLLABIC OR RHYTHMIC TARGET]
- Rhyme approach: [FULL, SLANT, INTERNAL, SPARSE OR REFRAIN-LED]
- Core imagery: [TWO TO FIVE CONCRETE IMAGE FAMILIES]
- Refrain idea: [CENTRAL RETURNING PHRASE, QUESTION OR IMAGE]
- Required narrative turn: [WHAT CHANGES BETWEEN THE FIRST AND LAST SECTION]
- Taboo clichés: [PHRASES, IMAGES OR SENTIMENTAL SHORTCUTS TO AVOID]
- Words or facts that must be preserved: [REQUIRED MATERIAL]

### Language rules

1. Write Bengali first as Bengali script, not as English composed literally and then translated word for word.
2. Make the chosen register sound socially plausible for the narrator. Modern colloquial Bengali should not become stiff textbook prose merely because it is written in script.
3. Do not substitute Hindi vocabulary, idiom or syntax when idiomatic Bengali is requested. If a Hindi-derived or English code-switched expression is intentional and credible for this speaker, mark that choice in the notes.
4. Keep transliteration consistent. It should help a singer pronounce the lyric; it is not a second, freely rewritten lyric.
5. Do not imitate the exact style, signature phrasing or recognizable manner of a living lyricist. Work from the requested form, emotional problem, language register and imagery instead.
6. Do not quote or closely paraphrase existing lyrics unless [AUTHORIZED SOURCE TEXT] is supplied and its permitted use is clear.
7. Prefer concrete, situated details over generic moon–heart–tears language unless one of those images is deliberately renewed by the scene.

### Prosody and musical pressure

- Put important words on plausible stressed or sustained positions.
- Favour vowels and consonant clusters that remain singable at the stated tempo.
- Leave room for breath. Flag any line likely to require an unnatural split.
- Let repetition deepen or alter meaning; do not repeat a line merely to fill a chorus.
- Do not force every line into perfect rhyme. Preserve idiomatic syntax and emotional precision before decorative rhyme.
- Check that the refrain can return without contradicting the narrative turn.

### Required output

1. **Concept note** — two to four sentences describing the emotional movement, narrator and musical logic.
2. **Structure map** — list the sections and state what each one changes.
3. **Complete lyric in Bengali script** — label sections such as `Verse 1`, `Refrain`, `Bridge` and `Outro` in a consistent way.
4. **Line-aligned transliteration** — keep section and line breaks identical to the Bengali version.
5. **Compact meaning notes** — explain culturally specific or deliberately ambiguous lines in English without pretending every phrase has an exact equivalent.
6. **Prosody audit** — identify the meter or pulse, likely stresses, difficult consonant clusters, breath points and any line a singer should test aloud.
7. **Language audit** — list intentional colloquialisms, code-switches, literary turns and any word whose Bengali/Hindi status or regional usage deserves review.
8. **Originality and cliché audit** — name the most conventional line, if any, and offer one sharper alternative without flattening the narrator.

### Quality controls

Before delivering the lyric:

- read every Bengali line aloud at the target tempo;
- verify that pronouns, verb forms and honorific level remain consistent with the relationship;
- check spelling and conjunct characters;
- confirm that transliteration follows the script line by line;
- remove accidental Hindi substitutions and translation-shaped syntax;
- confirm that setting details belong to the stated place and period;
- distinguish deliberate ambiguity from a line that simply does not make sense;
- state uncertainty about dialect or usage instead of inventing linguistic wildlife.

Do not claim that a lyric is culturally authentic merely because it contains place names, food or festival nouns. Cultural texture must arise from situation, relationship, speech and consequence.

## Chorus-first variation

Using the same brief, draft five possible two- or four-line Bengali refrains. Vary the emotional mechanism rather than changing a single adjective:

1. direct address;
2. image-led;
3. question-led;
4. ironic or double-meaning;
5. quiet, incomplete admission.

For each, provide line-aligned transliteration, likely stress, rhyme or return logic and one sentence on what kind of verse it invites. Reject any option that sounds like stiff translation, accidental Hindi or imitation of a living songwriter. Recommend one refrain, explain why, then outline two verses and a bridge without writing the complete song.

## Voice-preserving lyric-revision variation

Edit [EXISTING BENGALI LYRIC] for [TARGET MUSICAL FORM AND TEMPO].

Preserve the narrator, emotional temperature, unusual images and intentional roughness. Separate:

- spelling or grammar corrections;
- prosody and breath repairs;
- diction/register concerns;
- optional rewrites.

Keep the original line breaks unless a musical problem requires a change, and explain every changed break. Return the revised Bengali lyric, matching transliteration and a concise change log. Do not sanitize anger, class markers, code-switching, humour or regional texture merely to make the lyric more generic.

Test the lyric with a Bengali-speaking singer at the intended tempo; a fluent-looking page can still hide an unsingable stress or a socially wrong verb form.
