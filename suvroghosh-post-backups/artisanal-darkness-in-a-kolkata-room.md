The room wakes before I do, and it has not improved overnight.

The bedsheet is an argument. The cup has become archaeological evidence. The phone waits with the soft malice of a clerk who knows there are pending files. Light enters through the curtain without enthusiasm. I sit up slowly, not like a saint, monk, dawn-walker, lemon-water person, or laminated success story, but like a Kolkata drain after a difficult week.

Modern life has made even falling apart a branding problem.

One is expected to be presentable in collapse. Market-ready in confusion. Polished while privately negotiating with unpaid bills, stale air, old shame, and the sour little knowledge that the day has already begun without requesting consent. There should be a clean name for this. Premium despair. Handcrafted discouragement. Small-batch darkness from the southern edge of the city.

I dislike the packaging.

Respectability is a powerful local industry. It likes ironed shirts, controlled faces, correct sentences, and the appearance of upward motion. It does not ask whether the motion is real. It asks whether the photograph can survive inspection. A man may be falling through the floor, but if the shirt is decent and the caption sounds balanced, society may approve the descent.

This creates a peculiar pressure.

One must not merely endure. One must endure attractively. The room may be disordered, the mind stale, the future uncooperative, but the public self should remain usable. Uploadable. Employable. Reassuring to acquaintances. Not too needy. Not too strange. Not too visibly unsorted.

The old world had shame. The new world has shame with layout.

It gives templates. It says convert your suffering into a journey, your worry into a brand, your exhausted survival into content with a tasteful thumbnail. Even sincerity now arrives with recommended formats. The self becomes a product still trying to pass quality control.

Some mornings I refuse.

Not heroically. Refusal, in my case, often looks like sitting badly and drinking water from a cup that should have been washed earlier. But there is a small dignity in not turning every wound into packaging. A room can be dark without becoming artisanal. A person can be tired without producing a lesson. A bad day can remain a bad day, not a newsletter.

Calcutta is helpful here because it resists polish.

The city shows wiring, dampness, repair, traffic, uneven paint, sudden kindness, old insult, and practical bargaining in public. It does not hide its backstage well. That can be exhausting, but it is also a correction. Not everything is a clean surface. Not everything should be.

So I let the room remain partly unpresentable.

I fold one corner of the sheet. I wash the cup. I ignore the grand project of becoming admirable before breakfast. The darkness does not vanish, but it loses some of its boutique authority. It becomes ordinary again, which is less dramatic and therefore more useful.

Outside, the clouds collect without revelation.

For a moment I expect a sign.

The stomach answers first.
