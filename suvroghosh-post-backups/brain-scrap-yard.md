The brain sits in the skull like a scrap yard that learned literature.

Old metal everywhere. Bent signs. Useful parts mixed with rust. A chair from a vanished office. A cracked mirror. A machine that might still work if someone knew which wire to touch. Memory, intelligence, shame, technical skill, schoolboy vanity, workplace injury, stray sentences, unfinished ambition: all piled together under a roof that leaks during bad weather.

Sometimes I wish I could become less clever.

Not foolish. Foolishness has its own expenses. I mean less continuously aware. Less skilled at making connections between one insult and another, one failure and its cousin, one small present discomfort and the entire archive of prior discomforts. Intelligence is useful until it becomes a clerk that refuses to stop indexing pain.

The mind can turn anything into material.

A line from a conversation, a phrase in an email, the angle of a chair, the silence after a message, the smell of a room in summer. It labels, stores, cross-references. Later, without invitation, it retrieves. The result is not wisdom. It is a warehouse with poor access control.

Calcutta adds inventory daily.

Heat. Lane noise. Unfinished repair. Old books. Bad roads. A shop sign from another decade. A smell from childhood. The peculiar tiredness of a city that keeps surviving without becoming easy. For a mind already overstocked, the city is not background. It is supplier and auditor.

The scrap yard contains valuable parts.

I must admit that. The same brain that torments can also arrange a sentence, solve a technical problem, remember a pattern, detect nonsense, feel a sudden comic angle in an otherwise ordinary humiliation. To complain about it entirely would be dishonest. The machinery has kept me company, earned money once, built things, read books, survived rooms.

But useful machinery can still be loud.

The wish to remove the clever parts is really a wish for quiet. A wish to stop interpreting every vibration as news. A wish to let a day be a day instead of a case study in decline, class, history, economics, family weather, and the private failures of the republic. A wish to look at a cup and see only a cup.

This wish never lasts.

Soon the cup becomes evidence. The stain becomes metaphor. The sink becomes governance. The unpaid bill becomes macroeconomics with a personal invoice attached. The brain, shameless contractor, resumes work.

Perhaps the task is not demolition.

Perhaps it is sorting. Keep the useful parts. Label the dangerous ones. Do not let every old blade lie exposed. Build a path through the yard so that one can cross it without tearing the foot. Accept that some machinery will never be beautiful, only serviceable.

At night, the room smells faintly of old heat.

The fan turns. The archive mutters. Somewhere inside the scrap yard, one machine continues stamping forms no one requested.

I leave it on for now.
