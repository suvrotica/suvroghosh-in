The fish on the plate knows what it is. Rice knows what it is. Dal knows what it is. They do not hold press conferences. They do not announce that they invented physics in a previous era. They do not ask to be worshipped for simply existing.

That honesty is refreshing.

India has developed a strange public habit: take something broken, smelly, failed, or embarrassing; put a garland around it; add a ceremonial phrase; then call the result heritage. A drain is not fixed. It is contextualized. A superstition is not tested. It is defended. A bad claim is not corrected. It is promoted as pride.

This is how rot gets upgraded.

I like culture. I am Bengali; liking food, memory, language, argument, and old songs is practically built into the furniture. But culture is not a license to lie about reality. Myth can be beautiful without becoming engineering. Ritual can comfort without becoming science. A story can carry meaning without being turned into a laboratory report.

The trouble begins when pride works backward from conclusion to evidence. We decide the past must already contain everything. We decide our side must be great. We decide every embarrassing question is an insult. Then reality is forced to stand outside the room while sentiment takes attendance.

This is not patriotism. It is insecurity wearing a flag.

Real public reason is much plainer. Does the bridge stand? Does the school teach? Does the clinic work? Does the drain carry waste away? Does the claim survive evidence? Does the policy reduce harm? Does the office answer? Does the number add up?

These are not anti-national questions. They are adult questions.

The poor suffer most from this fog. Bad roads, weak schools, unsafe water, costly care, uncertain work, and then, as compensation, a speech about spiritual wealth. That is an old trick: take away the meal, then praise the holiness of the empty plate.

Superstition survives not only because people are foolish. It survives because life is frightening. When public systems are unreliable, magical explanations become emotional insurance. The quack appears where trust has failed. The loud guru appears where institutions are slow. The grand myth appears where the ordinary future looks locked.

So the answer is not mockery of the frightened. The answer is accountability for those who sell fog to the frightened.

There is a difference between reverence and fraud. Between memory and manipulation. Between being rooted and being buried.

A society that cannot name broken things becomes expert at perfume. It says implementation challenge where it means failure. It says sensitivity where it means fear. It says pride where it means avoidance. It says tradition where it means nobody wants to clean the mess.

But civilization is mostly boring competence. Clean water. Safe buses. Working drains. Fair exams. Honest accounts. Clinics where fear is not treated as a market. Schools where children learn to ask how we know. Public offices where an ordinary citizen can receive an ordinary service without becoming a supplicant.

These things do not trend well because they lack costume. They keep people alive anyway.

The first act of patriotism may be less dramatic than a slogan. It may be bending down with a broom, a test kit, a ledger, a repair manual, and enough honesty to say: this is not sacred. This is broken.

The fish cools on the plate. Truth, too, spoils quickly in heat.
