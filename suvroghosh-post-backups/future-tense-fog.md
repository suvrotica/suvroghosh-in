The towel on the chair had reached a dangerous middle state.

Not wet enough to accuse me. Not dry enough to forgive me. It hung there in the thick Calcutta air with the damp ambiguity of everything I keep postponing. A towel can become a small calendar if one lives carelessly enough. It records morning failure, afternoon compromise, and the evening decision to pretend the matter will improve by itself.

The future has the same smell.

People speak of the future as if it were a road. Take the right turn, choose the correct option, enter the OTP, submit the form, and some clean next chapter will open. In a small room, the future is less like a road and more like fog on the inside of glass. You know something is there. You can make out shapes. But when you move toward them, they soften.

At fifty-one, possibility no longer arrives as a parade. It arrives as a question with poor lighting. Can one still work? Can one still repair the body? Can one still answer messages without feeling conscripted? Can one still be useful without becoming available for every small extraction?

I do not know.

This is the punishment of future tense. It keeps grammar alive after certainty has left. I will. I may. I should. I must. Each phrase hangs in the room like laundry that refuses to dry.

Outside, people are going somewhere. Offices, markets, coaching centers, bright shops, counters where forms are stamped, counters where forms are rejected. The city continues to believe in motion because motion looks better than doubt.

Inside, I pick up the towel and press it to my face.

It smells faintly of soap, heat, and unfinished weather.

Tomorrow remains undecided, which is its oldest trick.
