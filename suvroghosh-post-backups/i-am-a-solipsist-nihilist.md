The bread is still untoasted when the universe becomes doubtful.

This is poor timing. No one should conduct metaphysics before tea. The mind is underfed, the kettle is undecided, the room is warm, and yet some inner clerk opens a file titled: what if nothing means anything and all experience is merely a private cinema?

Very good. But first, where is the butter knife?

Solipsism is the idea that only one's own mind can be known with certainty. Nihilism says, in its rough popular form, that meaning is not waiting outside with a garland. Both ideas have their uses if handled carefully. Mishandled before breakfast, they become household pests.

Calcutta does not care whether the world is real. The milk must be boiled. The bill must be paid. The lane will flood whether or not perception has philosophical foundations. This is one of the practical advantages of living in a city with infrastructure problems: reality keeps presenting receipts.

Still, the mind wanders. It asks why any of this matters. It asks whether the self is a room with mirrors. It asks whether public ambition is a theatrical misunderstanding performed by mammals in formal shirts. It asks whether meaning is invented, discovered, inherited, rented, or simply cooked fresh each morning like something perishable.

The answer, annoyingly, may be breakfast.

Not because breakfast solves philosophy. It does not. But it proves that a body cannot live on conclusions. A person may doubt cosmic meaning and still need salt. He may distrust grand narratives and still prefer the toast not burnt. He may suspect that the universe is indifferent and still move the cup away from the edge of the table.

This is not a refutation. It is a discipline of scale.

Large questions should be allowed into the house, but not permitted to run the kitchen before morning tea. They are guests, not landlords.

The toast darkens. The kettle clicks.

The universe remains uncertain, but breakfast has become available.
