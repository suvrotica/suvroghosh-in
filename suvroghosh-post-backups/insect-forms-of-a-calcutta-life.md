The pencil darkened the side of my palm before the drawing became anything.

This is how most honest things begin in my room: a stain first, then a line, then the suspicion that the line knows more than I intended. I was drawing a man's face and, without planning it, gave him the posture of an insect. Not a monster. That would be too grand. Just a small efficient body arranged around appetite.

The human face is a public relations department. It softens everything. Give a manipulator tired eyes and suddenly we search for his difficult childhood, his pressures, his misunderstood heart. We become generous at the exact moment when accuracy would be more useful.

But change the anatomy slightly and the intention clarifies.

The one who extracts favors becomes a narrow feeder. The one who folds his hands while calculating advantage becomes a patient little mechanism. The one who flatters before asking for free work acquires the correct angle of approach.

It sounds cruel until I include myself.

Then it becomes documentation.

I have my own small mouthparts. I too have looked for advantage, protection, praise, discount, escape. I too have stood upright in society while private machinery worked underneath. The drawing is not a courtroom. It is a mirror with more legs.

Calcutta supplies endless models. The polite request that is really a claim. The friendly call that arrives only when something is needed. The respectable person whose manners improve as your usefulness increases. Nothing spectacular. That is why drawing helps. It gives scale to the minor.

By evening my palm is black with graphite. The paper holds several faces that no longer pretend to be fully human and one small figure that looks uncomfortably like me.

I do not tear it up.

Some records should remain ugly.
