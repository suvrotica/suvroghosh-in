The clap sounded larger than I intended.

Two hands met in a small rented room, and the noise went out through the wall as if applying for public circulation. I had not planned a performance. I was not making a statement. The body merely wanted one clean sound after a morning of sticky thoughts and unfinished work.

But a neighborhood is a recording device with curtains.

Calcutta has a refined system for classifying men who do not fit. It does not need evidence. Evidence would slow the process. A man without regular office hours, regular income, regular social proof, and regular explanations becomes available for interpretation. He is strange. He is arrogant. He is useless. He is not right. The phrases differ. The verdict remains.

Useless is the sharpest one.

A thief has a profession. A loud fool has a constituency. A corrupt man may have a vehicle, which improves his moral standing in many local minds. But a middle-aged man who reads, writes, fails, and cannot convert his education into visible comfort is a more confusing object.

So I clap.

Not often. Not nobly. Sometimes only twice, as if checking whether I am still audible to myself.

There are days when that is enough trouble for everyone. Respectable society forgives cruelty faster than oddness because cruelty usually has a format. Oddness stains the furniture.

I know how this sounds. I am not claiming secret greatness. I have wasted time. I have avoided necessary work. I have been difficult, vain, and smaller than my own sentences. Still, the local file marked useless does not contain the entire man.

The rice cooker clicks.

The room settles.

I clap once more, softer this time, and the wall keeps its opinion.
