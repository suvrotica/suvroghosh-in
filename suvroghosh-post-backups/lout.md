The lane before sunrise has not yet formed an opinion of me.

This is one of dawn's kindnesses. Later, the day will remember everything. Work not done. Money not earned. Messages not answered. Plans not executed. The body's complaints. The mind's evasions. The old social ledger in which a middle-aged man is expected to have become more impressive by now.

But before sunrise, the lane is neutral.

The shutters are down. The tea shops are not fully awake. The air still has a little softness left in it, before traffic and heat begin their daily occupation. Even Calcutta's noise seems to be waiting for permission. In that interval, a person can be less accused by his own life.

I have called myself many unhelpful names. Lazy. Failed. Difficult. Unmarketable. Late. There is a rough satisfaction in such words, the way scratching a mosquito bite feels like a solution for two seconds. But self-insult is not insight. It is often just pain trying to sound efficient.

The dawn does not care for my labels.

It presents practical facts. A road. A sky. A body standing in the early air. A day not yet spoiled by commentary. This is not forgiveness, exactly. The bills remain. The work remains. The past remains. But the hour is not crowded. It allows one small reset.

Perhaps that is all dawn can offer: not hope in the grand sense, but a little unassigned time.

I have learned not to waste it on verdicts. The mind loves verdicts because verdicts feel like completion. "You are this." "Your life is that." "Nothing will change." Strong sentences. Useless sentences. Dawn is better used for weaker verbs.

Walk. Wash. Boil. Write. Sort. Begin.

The day will become complicated soon enough. Let it arrive and do its paperwork. For now the lane is grey, the air is thin, and no one has yet asked me to explain myself.

That is not much.

It is open.
