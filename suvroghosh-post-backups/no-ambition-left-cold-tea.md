The tea had cooled into a flat brown argument.

A thin skin gathered on top, the biscuit had surrendered to humidity, and the cup looked less like refreshment than evidence. I stared at it with the seriousness of a man trying to remember when ambition last entered the room and whether it took anything useful when it left.

There was a time when ambition had architecture. Study, work, America, competence, return, build, recover, become. The verbs stood in a row like obedient schoolboys. Then life opened the gate and the verbs ran in different directions.

What remains is less dramatic.

Rice has to be cooked. Messages have to be answered or strategically ignored. The body has to be moved. A bill must be paid late but not too late. A sentence must be written before the day hardens completely.

Physics has a word for running down: entropy. It is what happens when order leaks away. Heat spreads. Tea cools. Rooms gather dust. Plans lose alignment. A life that once pointed toward something begins to point toward many small emergencies.

This is not defeat in the theatrical sense. No curtain falls. No one announces the end. The biscuit simply bends when dipped.

At fifty-one, ambition may no longer be the proper instrument. It is too large, too polished, too interested in applause. Duty is uglier but more durable. Duty says wash the cup. Cook the rice. Write the paragraph. Do not let the month eat the whole table.

I dip the biscuit again. It breaks and sinks.

For a moment I watch it with more attention than it deserves.

Then I drink the tea anyway, because even a reduced life produces work for the mouth.
