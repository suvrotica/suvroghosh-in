The tea pan sat in the corner like evidence.

Its blackened bottom faced the room with the patience of an old examiner. The cup was empty. The morning had technically begun, but my body had not signed the attendance register. This is a smaller scandal than history prefers, yet it is the kind on which a day may turn.

Getting up is not always a decision.

Sometimes it is negotiation between knees, stomach, heat, shame, habit, and the faint memory that tea exists. The bed argues for delay. The room argues for surrender. The pan argues for procedure.

Water first.

Then tea dust.

Then milk, sugar, flame, attention.

A civilization may be collapsing elsewhere, but in this corner of Calcutta the immediate question is whether I can stand long enough to prevent the pan from boiling over. This is useful humility. It reduces metaphysics to timing.

The foam rises too fast, as usual. I lower the heat. A little spills anyway. The stove receives it without surprise.

The first sip burns my tongue.

After all the private drama, the day begins with a common error. I find this reassuring. Grand thoughts cannot survive hot tea without becoming slightly ridiculous.

Outside, the city is already moving. Scooters, shutters, voices, vendors, keys, gates, pressure cookers, the long rough machinery of morning. Nobody applauds because one man has become vertical. Nobody should.

Still, the cup is full now.

The sun has not been started by me, obviously.

But from inside the room, it agrees to continue.
