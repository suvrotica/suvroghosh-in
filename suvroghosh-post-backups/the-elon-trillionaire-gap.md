The fan rattled above me while the phone displayed another enormous number.

It concerned Elon Musk, wealth, markets, rockets, vehicles, platforms, predictions, rankings, and the usual global circus of men being measured in sums too large for the room in which I was sitting. The exact figure mattered less than its scale. After a point, money stops being arithmetic and becomes weather for everyone else.

I looked at my rice cooker.

It did not look back with confidence.

There is a strange humiliation in comparing a private life to public numbers. One knows the comparison is foolish. A trillion is not meant to be placed beside rent, tea, data recharge, and the small cost of repairing a chair. Yet the mind does it anyway because the same world contains both figures. Somewhere a valuation rises by amounts I cannot imagine. Somewhere else I decide whether a purchase can wait.

The gap is not only financial.

It is sensory. Their numbers arrive as charts, announcements, interviews, launch footage, magazine covers. Mine arrive as sweat under the collar, a delayed payment, a notebook margin, a cooker light, a bill folded twice and placed under a cup.

Technology speaks the language of scale. Scale, disruption, platform, moonshot, exponential growth. The room speaks in smaller verbs. Boil. Wait. Scrape. Pay. Reply. Stretch.

I am not against ambition. I have admired machines, code, engineering, audacity. But awe becomes indecent when it forgets the human scale below it. A society can worship acceleration so completely that it loses interest in the people standing still because the fare increased.

The phone goes dark.

The fan keeps rattling.

For a moment the largest number in the room is not valuation or wealth but the few grains of rice stuck to the spoon, refusing to fall.
