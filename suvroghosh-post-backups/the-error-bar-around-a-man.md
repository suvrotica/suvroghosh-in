The bathroom mirror had toothpaste spots arranged like failed astronomy.

In that small rectangle of glass, a face appeared that many systems would prefer to summarize quickly. Age. Income. Status. Usefulness. Appeal. Productivity. A few numbers, a few labels, one file closed.

People love point estimates.

A point estimate is one number offered as the answer. In statistics, it can be useful, but it is never the whole truth. A measurement should travel with uncertainty. How was it taken? Under what conditions? How much variation surrounds it? What does the instrument miss?

Gossip has no error bar.

That is why it feels so confident.

Society says failed, single, broke, difficult, odd, finished. The words are efficient. They fit in a mouth. They travel fast. But a man is not a thermometer reading. He is not one bank balance, one bad year, one awkward photograph, one afternoon of silence, one room seen from outside.

The error bar is the space around the verdict.

Inside that space are pages read, work done, work unpaid, help given, help refused, mornings lost, mornings recovered, jokes that arrived late, small decencies nobody recorded, private cowardices nobody prosecuted, and the stubborn fact that a human being leaks beyond his labels.

This is not an argument for innocence.

The number may be bad. The evidence may be unpleasant. A life can be genuinely damaged, poorly managed, and difficult to defend. Statistics does not rescue the data by sentiment. It only asks us not to pretend the estimate is the universe.

I wipe the mirror with the edge of a towel.

The face becomes clearer and not necessarily better.

Still, there is margin around it.
