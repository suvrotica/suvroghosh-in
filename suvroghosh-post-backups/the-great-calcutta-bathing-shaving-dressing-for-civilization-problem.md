The bucket is not an object. It is an accusation with a handle.

There it sits in the bathroom, blue and half full, holding water with the patience of a retired examiner. The mug floats inside it. The towel hangs from the nail. The mirror waits above the basin as if it has already heard the case and is only waiting for the accused to enter.

People call this a simple routine.

Bath. Shave. Dress.

Three verbs. Very neat. The kind of instruction given by people whose mornings still obey them. In a difficult Calcutta room, however, the process becomes a public works project. Towel location, soap condition, razor negotiation, floor slipperiness, shirt selection, and the terrible business of looking directly at one's own face before society has been sufficiently prepared.

Civilization is not always marble, constitution, anthem, or conference.

Sometimes civilization is a man standing at a bathroom door, deciding whether he can be bothered to rejoin the republic of the washed.

The first splash is the worst. It announces the body as fact. Heat, skin, age, hair, belly, neck, all suddenly present and unwilling to remain theoretical. Then the soap begins its little argument. The razor catches near the chin. The towel dampens too fast. A shirt is chosen because it is less guilty than the others.

For a short while after, one becomes almost acceptable.

Not transformed. Let us remain honest. Only moved from evidence to participant.

By the time I sit under the fan, I am already sweating again. The old republic returns from the armpits.

Still, the bucket has been answered for today.
