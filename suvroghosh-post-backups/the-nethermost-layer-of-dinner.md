The rice stuck to the bottom of the cooker with dry moral certainty.

Not tragedy. Let us not flatter dinner. It was only rice, overheld by heat, pressed into a thin white fossil at the bottom of the pot. But the spoon made that scraping sound, and suddenly the evening acquired a lower register.

Every day has a nethermost layer.

On top, there are explanations. Work was delayed. Heat was bad. Money is tight. The body is tired. The city is loud. People are careless. These are true enough. But beneath them sits the harder layer, the part one scrapes at when the room has gone quiet and the food has cooled.

Why is this still the arrangement?

No answer arrives. Only the spoon.

The cooker is an honest machine. It does not pretend dinner is culture. It produces heat, steam, and result. If the water is wrong or the timing bad, it leaves evidence. Human life could learn from this clarity.

Instead we decorate.

We call survival simplicity. We call exhaustion maturity. We call having no better option discipline. Then the pot cools, and the bottom tells the truth.

Still, the stuck rice can be eaten if scraped carefully. This is not hope, exactly. It is technique.

Later, the body insists on its ordinary claims. Thirst. Bladder. Sleep. A small pain in the knee. Biology continues its bookkeeping after philosophy has closed for the day.

I wash the pot badly.

One grain remains near the rim, white and stubborn, refusing to become part of the lesson.
