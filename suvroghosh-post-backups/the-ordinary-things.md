The cup had a brown map inside it.

Not a useful map. It would guide no traveler except perhaps toward the sink. Still, the stain had borders, rivers, a darker inland region where the tea had rested too long. I held it near the window and thought: this is the sort of thing I keep noticing.

Small things.

The book left open face down. The pencil dust on my fingers. The rice grain under the table. The line in a drawing that makes a face suddenly look ashamed. The fan's uneven rhythm. The particular color of afternoon on a wall that has seen too many seasons without being repainted.

None of this looks like achievement.

That may be why I trust it.

The world has built a loud trade around achievement: results, scale, impact, growth, visibility, the whole brass band. Ordinary noticing has no such parade. It merely records that a life passed through a room and left marks.

A man is not only his income, though income matters brutally. He is not only his failures, though they take up space. He is also his inventory: what he saw, what he named, what he refused to let blur into one general fog.

This is why I write about cups, heat, books, stains, food, and the small insulting objects of daily life. They are not decorative. They are witnesses. A room that looks empty to another person may be crowded with evidence if one has lived inside it long enough.

I rinse the cup.

The stain fades but does not fully leave.

That seems fair.
