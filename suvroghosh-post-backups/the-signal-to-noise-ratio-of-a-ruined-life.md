The ceiling fan made a dry chopping sound above the mattress.

Not loud enough to be repaired immediately. Not soft enough to ignore. It sat in the room as background noise, moving hot air and suspicion in equal quantity.

Signal-to-noise ratio is a simple idea. Signal is the thing you want to receive. Noise is everything that interferes. A song buried under hiss. One useful sentence inside a meeting. One honest thought inside a day full of errands, shame, messages, heat, bills, appetite, and old memory.

My life often feels like bad reception.

There may be signal somewhere. Curiosity, skill, humor, one line of work, one readable paragraph, one clean decision. But around it comes the hiss: unpaid invoices, delayed replies, a body demanding attention, a room that never becomes cool enough, the old story of what should have happened by now.

Noise is not always dramatic.

Often it is ordinary. The spoon missing. The shirt damp. The password forgotten. The call avoided. The rice cooker clicking off when one is not ready to eat. These small sounds accumulate until the message underneath becomes hard to hear.

The task, then, is not to become pure signal. That is fantasy. Human life is full of thermal noise. The task is amplification. Find the one thing that still carries meaning and turn toward it before the static wins.

At ten in the morning, the signal is not noble.

It says: eat first.

Then see.

The fan continues its rough argument with the ceiling, but I hear it a little less.
