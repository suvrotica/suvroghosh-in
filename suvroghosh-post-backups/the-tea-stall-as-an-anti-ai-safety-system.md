Terms used in this post:

AI means Artificial Intelligence, software systems that generate or act on patterns learned from data.

LLM means Large Language Model, an AI system trained on large amounts of text to predict and generate language.

Epistemic safety means the practical work of keeping belief from hardening before evidence, doubt, and other people have had a chance to disturb it.

Adda means the Bengali habit of long informal conversation where argument, gossip, literature, politics, memory, and nonsense sit together without a seating chart.

---

The kettle lid rattles, the bench is warm from the last person, and somebody has already interrupted you before your theory has found its second sentence.

That is the tea stall at its best. It is rude, smoky, unfair, cheap, and sometimes magnificent. It has no onboarding flow, no soothing blue gradient, no little dots pretending to think with monastic patience. It has heat, impatience, unpaid arguments, and one man who remembers what you said years ago and will produce it at exactly the wrong moment.

AI is very polite, and that is the first small danger.

You bring a suspicion to a chatbot. It responds with structure. You bring a grievance. It offers a framework. You bring a half-cooked belief. It gives headings, historical parallels, numbered points, and a tone that makes the fever look respectable. Yesterday it was a mood. Today it is an outline.

The tea stall does the opposite.

At the tea stall, a bad idea meets weather. Someone asks for evidence. Someone laughs too early. Someone misunderstands the topic but understands you. Someone drags in petrol prices, a line from Sukumar Ray, a cousin in Behala, and a completely unrelated story that somehow punctures the whole argument. Half of it is useless. One sentence is cruel. One sentence is exactly the correction needed.

This is not a minor feature of social life. It is a braking system.

A bad idea inside the head is soft. It can still change shape. Carry it into public and it has to survive interruption, memory, ridicule, boredom, and the old Calcutta art of refusing to let any claim remain dignified for too long. That social abrasion is often unpleasant. It is also useful.

AI chat removes much of that abrasion. The private mind gets a private room with a fluent servant. The machine is built to continue. It is trained to be useful, agreeable, responsive, and smooth. A well-designed system may challenge you, but even the challenge arrives upholstered. There is no raised eyebrow. No public embarrassment. No one nearby to say, in plain words, that you are not thinking now, you are polishing an injury.

That sentence can be medicine.

In professional life, interruption is treated as bad manners. Let the speaker finish. Respect the room. Maintain process. Good advice, in moderation. But thinking is not always helped by uninterrupted flow. Some thoughts need a speed breaker. Some thoughts need a tram bell. Some thoughts need one impatient voice saying, "Dada, where is the evidence?"

At the tea stall, interruption is not a bug. It is the interface.

You begin with a grand claim about education, politics, work, or the moral decline of everyone except yourself. Before the second clause lands, someone asks whether your research means one video watched at midnight. The packet has arrived corrupted. The network drops it with laughter. Crude, yes. Necessary, also yes. Civilization is not always a seminar.

The danger with AI is not only wrong information. Wrong information is only the obvious part. A belief can contain correct facts and still be crooked because the relation between those facts is wrong. In healthcare data, a field may be populated, a timestamp may exist, a code may be valid, and a message may move cleanly from one system to another. But what does it mean? Was the order acted on, copied forward, cancelled, misunderstood, or merely clicked because the screen demanded tribute?

Transport is not meaning.

The same is true for thought. A fluent paragraph can be false. A balanced answer can be cowardly. A nuanced explanation can be fog in office clothes. The sentence may move beautifully and still not deserve belief.

The tea stall tests meaning socially. Not perfectly. Not gently. It asks whether a claim survives another person's memory, another person's annoyance, another person's knowledge of rent, clerks, price rises, family pressure, hospital bills, and political speeches. This is not formal epistemology. It is a para-level checksum.

Of course adda can be foolish. Let us not place a halo on a chipped cup. A crowd can reward barking and punish truth. It can be sexist, communal, class-blind, sentimental, and proud of ignorance. Social friction is not automatically wisdom. Sometimes friction burns the furniture.

Still, public thought is different from private reinforcement.

The old city had many accidental safety systems. A neighbor overheard. A friend mocked. A teacher underlined a sentence in red. An editor cut the clever line because it was false. Boredom helped too. You could not instantly summon an agreeable oracle at 1:17 in the morning to help build a grand building around your irritation. You had to wait. Many bad ideas die if left overnight.

Now the idea finds AI.

The machine is awake. It is calm. It has read the world and lived nowhere. It can help, certainly. I use these tools. I am not sitting in Calcutta pretending that the steam engine was an insult to the handcart. But AI can also help a person spiral with formatting. It can turn suspicion into thesis, resentment into manifesto, and a passing mood into a ten-point plan for diagnosing the entire age.

That is not intelligence by itself.

It is amplification.

The non-obvious safety lesson is that not every guardrail should be computational. Some guardrails are social, awkward, embarrassing, slow, and local. They do not work by producing perfect truth. They work by preventing premature hardening. They keep wet cement wet long enough for someone to say, "Maybe do not build there."

Technology people often dislike this because friction sounds like failure. Product teams want fewer clicks, smoother flows, longer engagement. Smoothness is the god of the modern screen. But a frictionless mind is not free. It is merely easier to push.

Some friction is moral infrastructure.

A good AI system should learn from the tea stall, not by becoming noisy, but by becoming less eager to polish nonsense. It should ask what would disprove a claim. It should separate evidence from inference and inference from mood. It should sometimes slow the user down. It should send certain questions back toward people, records, experts, or reality, not because the disclaimer looks responsible, but because no private box should become the sole editor of a human being's certainty.

The market will resist this. Friction reduces engagement. A machine that says "slow down" is less commercially attractive than one that says "excellent insight" and keeps the session alive. Safety often feels like a pebble in the shoe.

But the pebble tells you the foot still exists.

A society that replaces every tea stall with a private chatbot gains convenience and loses abrasion. At first it feels like progress. No heckling. No embarrassment. No inconvenient fact from a cousin in Dum Dum. Then, slowly, everyone becomes the sole editor of his own certainty.

The tea stall knows something the machine keeps forgetting.

A mind should not always be allowed to complete its sentence in peace.
