The room becomes smaller after the third scroll.

Not physically. The walls remain where the builder and dampness left them. The chair remains near the table. The mirror remains rude in its fixed position. But the feed changes the air. It brings in other lives, polished rooms, confident faces, job announcements, opinions arranged for applause, disasters resized for attention, and machines speaking with the calm of someone who has never paid an electricity bill.

Then the room feels like a verdict.

The mirror joins in. It shows the face without context. It does not show effort, history, bad sleep, recovery, endurance, or the thousand invisible compromises by which a person remains functional. It shows surface and lets the mind supply punishment.

The feed supplies comparison.

Together they form a neat little trap: the room says you are stuck, the mirror says you are diminished, the feed says everyone else has escaped. This is not truth. It is a system of bad evidence arranged in a persuasive order.

Modern digital life is very good at building soft authority.

A stranger with lighting speaks confidently. An algorithm repeats what provokes response. A model answers fluently even when the ground beneath the answer is weak. A platform turns attention into a habit and habit into dependence. None of this feels violent. It feels convenient. That is why it works.

The mind under pressure begins to accept the arrangement.

It starts treating the room as destiny, the mirror as diagnosis, the feed as census, and the machine as oracle. The private self becomes an audience to its own reduction. You sit in one place while the world performs speed around you. It becomes easy to feel late, irrelevant, badly assembled.

Calcutta complicates this.

The city outside is not virtual. It leaks, honks, sweats, argues, repairs, forgets, remembers, and demands coins. It refuses the clean tyranny of the feed. A cracked pavement is not content until someone frames it. A tea stall is not a brand until someone writes the caption. The city keeps offering unedited evidence, and the feed keeps trying to make evidence marketable.

The escape, if that is the word, is not dramatic.

Leave the phone on the table. Stand up. Look at one real object without converting it into meaning. A cup. A shirt. A wire. A patch of wall. Let the mirror be only glass. Let the room be only a room, not a moral biography. Let the feed continue shouting elsewhere.

AI adds another layer to this trap because it speaks so smoothly.

It can summarize, advise, flatter, correct, and produce a tone of competence that tired humans may find hard to resist. Useful tools are useful. But a fluent machine should not be allowed to become the final witness about a life it has never lived. It knows patterns. It does not know the full weight of the room.

Some days the room remains small.

Still, one can open the window. Not as metaphor. Literally. Air enters. Noise enters. A bit of city enters without asking to be liked. The mirror stays on the wall. The feed waits with its little hooks. The room, briefly, returns to being square footage.

That is not freedom.

It is enough space to breathe once without being measured.
