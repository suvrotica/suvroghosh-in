My shirt was damp before the laptop finished waking up.

The market likes suffering formatted properly. It prefers PDFs, clean emails, clear deliverables, and the impression that the person behind the work has no stomach, weather, fear, or laundry. The honest version is less attractive: one middle-aged man in a hot Calcutta room waiting for Gmail to decide whether he is still a person or only a service provider.

The inbox opened.

"Please confirm."

"Kindly clarify."

"Can we get this by EOD?"

EOD means End of Day, though in consulting it often feels like End of Dignity. The phrase arrives politely, which makes it more efficient.

Alan Turing proposed a test in which a machine might be judged intelligent if its answers could not be distinguished from a human's. My smaller test runs in reverse. Can a tired human still produce outputs smooth enough to pass through the market's machine?

Reply in time.

Sound calm.

Attach the document.

Do not reveal the heat, the unpaid worry, the body, the room, the small collapse before opening the mail.

If the output arrives, the judge behind the glass says: useful enough.

This is not dignity, but it resembles employability from a distance.

After the call, the room returns. Tea cold. Fan tired. Laptop warm under the palm. I do not feel proud. I feel translated into service.

Then the electricity flickers.

For a moment the screen dims, and the whole test waits for power.
