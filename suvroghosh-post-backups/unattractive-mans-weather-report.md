Male unattractiveness does not always arrive dramatically.

Often it comes like damp on a wall. One day a man is not handsome but still serviceable. Some years later, after bad sleep, delayed money, tired clothes, and dental postponement, he realizes he has become socially low-voltage.

This is my weather report.

Not catastrophic. Not sunshine either. Cloudy, humid, unstable.

People think attractiveness is about face and body. Sweet idea. In middle age, attractiveness is a coalition government. Income holds one ministry. Teeth another. Posture, clothes, hair, confidence, sleep, and the ability to leave the house without looking administratively defeated all demand portfolios.

When money weakens, several ministries resign.

The shirt hangs differently. The back bends before the conversation begins. Even the smile becomes cautious because it knows the mouth has financial history.

Possibility was once a cosmetic. Youth owns it cheaply. It straightens the spine and lends brightness to poor fabric. Later, possibility becomes more expensive. It wants proof.

Still, a man is not permanently weathered into one condition.

Repairs are possible. Not miracles. Bolts and hinges. A better shirt. A paid bill. A cleaned tooth. A walk taken before the day wins. One honest piece of work. The body, that old union of complaints, may suspend agitation briefly.

The young man will not return, and perhaps he should not. He was foolish in ways I no longer have the budget to maintain.

The report remains uncertain.

Cloudy, humid, unstable, with a slight chance of dignity.
