Some mornings I wake up and the day has already removed the furniture.

Not the actual furniture. The chair is there. The table is there. The water bottle, the phone, the fan turning above me with the patience of an underpaid clerk: all present. Calcutta has begun outside without asking permission. A scooter coughs. A pressure cooker declares breakfast. Someone drags a bucket across a floor.

Inside, the assembly is incomplete.

The self is not always ready at waking. This is hard to explain to people who rise like switches. They imagine thought begins immediately, identity reports for duty, appetite takes attendance, purpose opens the door. On some mornings none of this happens. There is only the body on the bed trying to remember the order of operations.

Ceiling first.

Fan.

Light.

Heat.

Name.

Room.

The process is slow and faintly absurd, as if the universe must be rebuilt from household objects. The mind does not yet have a narrator. Without a narrator, even trouble lacks grammar. It is not a story. It is weather inside the walls.

Tea helps, though not in the miraculous way tea is advertised by those of us who depend on it too much. Tea gives the morning a handle. Boil water. Find cup. Measure leaves. Wait. Pour. Hold. These actions are small, sequential, and therefore merciful. A routine is a rope laid across a flooded patch of road.

The mistake is to demand meaning too early. Meaning is not ready before the kettle. The first hour should not be asked to defend the entire life. It has enough trouble locating the spoon.

So I lower the scale. Sit up. Wash face. Open window. Do not read bad news immediately. Do not review finances before the first cup. Do not believe the morning's emptiness is a verdict. It may be only a condition.

This is not optimism. It is procedure.

By midmorning, sometimes, a little furniture returns. A sentence. A task. A mild irritation. The useful machinery of being a person begins to click into place. Not fully. Enough.

Outside, the city has no idea what was reconstructed inside this small room.

That is fine. Most repairs are invisible from the street.
