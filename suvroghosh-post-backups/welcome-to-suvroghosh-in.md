The cup leaves a brown ring on the table before the first sentence has decided whether it wants to exist.

That is as good an introduction as any. SuvroGhosh.in is not a polished hall where the chairs are aligned, the language has been combed, and every thought enters wearing shoes. It is closer to a small Calcutta room where the fan makes an old mechanical complaint, the tea cools too quickly, and science, politics, memory, technology, class, failure, and jokes of questionable usefulness all sit too close to one another.

The `.in` matters. Not as patriotism. I have no talent for decorative belonging. It matters because this site is written from India, and more particularly from Calcutta, where abstraction has to cross drains, heat, rent, repairs, family expectation, public noise, and the daily comic opera of systems that almost function.

I write about science because reality deserves defense from superstition and fashionable nonsense. I write about technology because machines have become our new municipal clerks: everywhere, powerful, opaque, and capable of ruining a day through one small invisible assumption. I write about society because public life in India often feels like a long queue in which everyone suspects the form has already expired. I write satire because plain anger becomes dull if it is not given some intelligence and shape.

This is also a record of stubbornness. Not triumph. I have no interest in pretending that a life becomes meaningful because the correct paragraph has been arranged around it. Many things fail. Careers turn sideways. Plans lose air. A person may return from elsewhere with education, skill, and experience, and still find himself bargaining with ordinary uncertainty in a room that does not care about his resume.

But writing remains.

Not as salvation. That word is too large and too clean. Writing remains as a practical act, like keeping a notebook dry during rain. A sentence does not repair the road. It does not pay the bill. It does not make a society honest. But it can preserve the shape of an observation before the day grinds it into dust.

So this site will be untidy because the world is untidy. It will move from AI to atheism, from healthcare data to fish markets, from mathematical ideas to the small humiliations of lower-middle-class life. The point is not variety for its own sake. The point is that everything is connected once you stop pretending the mind lives in separate departments.

If you have come here for a brand, you may be disappointed. If you have come for a human notebook, still warm from use, then pull up the nearest broken chair.

The tea is getting cold. The sentence has begun.
