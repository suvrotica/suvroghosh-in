The room was hot before noon had finished introducing itself.

June in Calcutta does not arrive as weather. It arrives as a notice pasted on the body. The wall sweats, the chair sweats, the bucket looks defeated, and the ceiling fan performs movement without producing much evidence of result.

This is the kind of thing I write about.

Small things.

Not because small things are charming. Often they are not. A cup, a bad tooth, a damp towel, unpaid work, old anger, rice stuck to the pot, a shirt that has lost its argument with the body. These objects do not ask to be literary. They merely remain.

Large subjects are easier to fake.

Civilization, nation, history, technology, destiny, culture. One can stand at a distance and produce noble smoke. Small things are rude. They sit on the table and ask whether the sentence is accurate.

I write about them because I am a small subject myself. Not unimportant to myself, obviously. Vanity survives many collapses. But small in the public sense: one room, one laptop, one body, one budget, one set of recurring embarrassments.

Naming these things does not redeem them.

It does something less grand and more useful. It prevents blur. If the day is going to be difficult, let it at least be specifically difficult. Let the heat have its proper texture. Let the cup, the fan, the bill, the stain, the silence be entered into the record.

The clouds gather outside and do not rain.

Even the sky, it seems, enjoys withholding closure.
